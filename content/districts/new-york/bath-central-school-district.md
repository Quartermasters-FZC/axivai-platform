Bath Central School District is centred on a village that runs its own electric utility. The Village of Bath's municipal system is separate from NYSEG, the main utility in Steuben County. That gives Bath a different grid partner for depot charging than most of its neighbours. Its NYSERDA fleet plan is underway, which puts it ahead of most Steuben County districts. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,324 students in 2024–25, down from 1,349 in 2023–24 and 1,369 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 3% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 94.81 square miles in Steuben County, in NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about 14 students per square mile. Routes run from the village out into rural towns.
- **Size in the county:** Bath is the third-largest of the 13 Steuben County districts in NYSED's report, after Corning City (4,354) and Hornell City (1,360).
- **Budget:** $55,698,009 proposed for 2026–27, up 9.51% from $50,861,699. The tax levy rises a much smaller 2.4%, to $9,589,552 (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

Across the Southern Tier, NYSED reports that 44 of 54 responding districts (82%) own and operate all their buses. Only 4 (7%) contract out all transportation. Electric buses are still rare in the region. Four districts run them:

| District | Electric buses | Fleet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ithaca City | 7 | 90 |
| Johnson City | 4 | 40 |
| Downsville | 1 | 14 |
| Chenango Valley | 1 | 37 |

None is in Steuben County. Bath's first electric buses would be among the first in the county. Range will be the first question for routes that leave the village for rural towns in a Steuben winter.

## Depot power and the grid

The NYS Department of Public Service lists two electric providers for Steuben County: NYSEG, and the municipal utility in Bath, Bath Electric, Gas and Water Systems. It also supplies gas in the village. NYSED's report notes that the Southern Tier gets its power mainly from NYSEG, with "some small utility providers" serving a few districts. Bath is one of the places that applies.

Which provider serves Bath's bus depot will decide whom the district negotiates with for charger capacity. The FEP's load estimate should go to that provider early, so the district learns whether the existing service can take the first chargers.

Planning status in Steuben County, from NYSED's October 2025 report:

- **Underway:** Bath, Arkport, Bradford, Hornell City and Wayland-Cohocton (5 districts);
- **Applied:** Addison;
- **No contact:** Avoca, Campbell-Savona, Canisteo-Greenwood, Corning City, Hammondsport, Jasper-Troupsburg and Prattsburgh (7 districts).

Across the Southern Tier, 13 districts expect to need service upgrades for a fully electric fleet, and 38 of 54 (70%) had a utility needs assessment or load letter. See [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap) for the upstate grid picture.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Bath.
- **Reserves:** the district's 2026–27 schedule of reserves shows about $1.55 million in a capital reserve, marked for capital projects only.
- **Budget pressure:** spending rises 9.51% in 2026–27 while the levy rises 2.4%. The gap is covered by state aid and other revenue, which leaves little room in local taxes for higher bus costs.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Bath's plan shows the depot needs more power than its utility can supply in time, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses run while the connection is upgraded. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
