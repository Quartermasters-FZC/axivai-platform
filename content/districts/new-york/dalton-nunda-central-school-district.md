Dalton-Nunda Central School District, known locally as Keshequa, has done the planning step that most New York districts have not. NYSED's October 2025 report lists its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as **Complete**. Only 16 of the 70 districts in the Finger Lakes region were that far along. What Keshequa does not yet have is an electric bus. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 504 students in 2024–25, against 496 in 2023–24 and 525 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is 4% below two years earlier.
- **Schools:** NYSED lists three: Dalton-Nunda Elementary, Dalton-Nunda Middle School and Dalton-Nunda Secondary School. The district's name joins the two communities, Dalton and Nunda, that it serves.
- **Area:** 118.2 square miles in southern Livingston County, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Density:** about four students per square mile. Among Livingston County's eight districts, only Dansville (125.8 square miles) covers more ground, and it enrolls more than twice as many students.

## Fleet and transportation

Keshequa did not return NYSED's 2025 zero-emission busing survey. It was one of only two Finger Lakes districts that did not; the other was Avon, also in Livingston County. As a result the state's report carries no fleet count, charger count or route data for the district, and we do not publish an estimate.

What the report does show is the region around it. In the Finger Lakes, 68% of districts own and operate all their buses, and most fleets are 21 to 40 buses. Two Livingston County neighbours already run electric buses: Livonia (one in a fleet of 38) and York (one in a fleet of 23).

Keshequa is the eighth-largest of the 70 Finger Lakes districts by area, and one of the least dense. The completed FEP is the document that models its routes against winter battery range and sets its charger count.

## Depot power and the grid

Three electric utilities serve Livingston County: National Grid, Rochester Gas and Electric (RG&E) and NYSEG (Public Service Commission). The state's utility territory data place both the village of Nunda and the hamlet of Dalton in RG&E's territory. Whichever utility feeds the bus garage sets the make-ready program, the upgrade process and the timeline.

NYSED's regional data show what Finger Lakes districts are running into:

- **Upgrades.** 27 districts expect their electric service will need upgrading for a fully electric fleet. Seven expect to expand their depot and three need a new one.
- **Load letters.** 41 of 68 responding districts (60%) have completed a utility needs assessment or hold a load letter.
- **Top barriers.** Districts rated utility support upgrades, lack of external funding and lack of voter support as the hardest problems.

A completed FEP normally includes the service size the depot needs. The next step is to turn that number into a service request to the utility. NYSED's report found that a lack of adequate electric service was a main reason districts had few chargers. Upstate grid timing is covered in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Keshequa. In Livingston County, Dansville holds one voucher ($257,250, approved May 2024).
- **Regional picture:** 21 Finger Lakes districts reported state or local zero-emission funding, averaging about $272,682. Only one reported a federal award.

A district with a completed FEP can move straight to voucher applications and a bus proposition. Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the FEP's depot load needs a utility upgrade that will take longer than a bus order, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage so Keshequa's first electric buses run while the grid work is finished. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
