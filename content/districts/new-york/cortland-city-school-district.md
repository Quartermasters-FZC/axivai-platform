Cortland put an electric bus on the ballot and lost. In May 2025 the Cortland Enlarged City School District asked voters to buy one electric bus and three diesel buses. They said no, on the same day they turned down the budget. The plan had come with a free charger from the City of Cortland and a solar field being built next to the Kaufman Center. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Name:** officially the Cortland Enlarged City School District; NYSED lists it as Cortland City School District.
- **Enrollment:** 1,819 students in 2024–25, down from 1,841 in 2023–24 and 1,858 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 45.75 square miles, in NYSED's Central New York labor market region.
- **Density:** about 40 students per square mile. The district covers the small city of Cortland and the rural towns around it.
- **Budget:** the 2025–26 budget passed at a revote on 17 June 2025, 510 to 272 (65%). It totals $61.2 million (607 News Now).

## Fleet and transportation

Cortland owns and runs its own buses, like 41 of the 48 responding districts in Central New York (85%). It keeps a five-year replacement schedule for its buses and asks voters each spring to fund the next set.

The 2025 ballot shows how the district tried to start electrifying:

- **Proposition 2:** buy one electric bus and three diesel buses. The district said NYSBIP funding would make the electric bus cost the same as a diesel one.
- **Proposition 3:** buy four diesel buses, only if Proposition 2 failed, so the replacement schedule stayed on track.

On 20 May 2025, voters rejected the budget and Proposition 2. The three other propositions passed (607 News Now). The fallback brought four diesel buses instead of one electric bus.

Before the vote, the district held an electric school bus open house at its bus garage, 1 Valley View Drive, on 25 and 26 March 2025. Matthews Buses sponsored it, with sessions on battery technology and safety. First responders had their own session.

## Depot power and the grid

The City of Cortland, where the bus garage sits, is in National Grid's service territory, according to the state's utility territory data.

Cortland had a charging plan ready. According to the district, the City of Cortland is building a solar field next to the Kaufman Center and will provide the district one charger for free. That is an unusual starting point: a charger offered at no cost, beside a new local generator, at a district that has yet to buy an electric bus.

One charger serves one or two buses. A full fleet needs much more. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Cortland's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) status as **"Initial Contact Made"**. The FEP is the free study that sizes chargers and the service upgrade a fleet needs. Across Central New York, only 3 of 49 districts had a completed plan, and 20 (41%) had made no contact with NYSERDA at all.

The regional numbers show how early Central New York is:

- **One electric bus in the region:** Fabius-Pompey ran the only zero-emission bus among 45 reporting districts, and none had any on order.
- **Voters are a barrier:** six districts in the region had put zero-emission bus propositions to voters, and only one passed. Districts rated lack of voter support among their hardest challenges.

For the wider grid picture upstate, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Cortland. No district in Cortland County holds one.
- **Charger:** one free charger offered by the City of Cortland alongside the solar field.
- **Local:** bus purchases go through the annual ballot. Any future electric bus will need a voter-approved proposition, alongside a NYSBIP voucher.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in our [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

One free charger will not carry a full electric fleet at the Valley View Drive garage. If the utility upgrade takes longer than the bus orders, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage so the buses run in the meantime. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
