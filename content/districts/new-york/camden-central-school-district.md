Camden Central School District covers 303.4 square miles of Oneida County, near the Tug Hill Plateau. By area it ranks 13th of the 685 districts in the regional tables of NYSED's October 2025 zero-emission report. That size drives everything about its buses: a fleet of about 60 vehicles, long rural runs and heavy lake-effect winters. Camden has a NYSERDA fleet plan underway and no electric buses yet. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,915 students in 2024–25, against 1,894 in 2023–24 and 1,892 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Need:** 59% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 303.4 square miles, in NYSED's Mohawk Valley labor market region. That is about six students per square mile. Only Town of Webb, Poland and Adirondack cover more ground in the region.
- **Budget:** the 2026–27 state filing shows spending of $59,838,634, **down 3.18%** from $61,804,479, with a tax levy up 2.75%.

## Fleet and transportation

Camden owns and runs its buses. NYSDOT recorded **126 safety inspections** of its vehicles, two of them failed, in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026. Buses are inspected twice a year, which points to a fleet of about 60 buses and other vehicles. Few Mohawk Valley districts run fleets that size. In NYSED's survey, 31 of the 47 districts that described their fleets had 20 buses or fewer, and only one had more than 60.

Spread across 303 square miles, that fleet works long runs with light loads. Route length, not student count, sets how big the batteries need to be and how often buses must charge.

Camden's reserves are lean. NYSED's 2026–27 schedule of reserves lists funds for workers' compensation, unemployment, employee benefits and retirement contributions, but **no capital or bus reserve**. Districts such as Brocton and Cassadaga Valley save ahead for buses this way. Camden will rely on yearly propositions, state transportation aid and vouchers.

## Depot power and the grid

The state's utility territory map places the village of Camden in **National Grid** territory.

Two local factors will shape charging:

- **Winter.** Tug Hill lake-effect snow means long cold spells and slow roads. Cold batteries lose range, and heating the cabin draws on the same battery. Buses that preheat while plugged in start their routes with more range, but preheating adds to the garage's peak load.
- **Midday charging.** On the longest runs, a bus may need to recharge between the morning and afternoon runs. A fleet of 60 charging midday as well as overnight needs a much bigger electrical service than a diesel garage was built for.

A load this size can mean a new transformer. Large power transformers have taken two to four years to arrive (NIAC, June 2024).

The plan that sizes this is in progress. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Camden's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **underway**. In the Mohawk Valley, 33 of 54 districts had a plan underway, 4 had finished one and 11 had made no contact with NYSERDA. None of the region's responding districts owned an electric bus, and two were on order region-wide.

For the grid picture across upstate New York, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Camden. In recent awards, a full-size electric bus has drawn a $257,250 voucher.
- **Reserves:** no capital or bus reserve in NYSED's 2026–27 schedule.
- **Budget pressure:** 2026–27 spending falls 3.18%, while the projected enrollment count in the tax filing falls 2.26%.

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Camden's fleet plan calls for a larger National Grid service or a new transformer at the bus garage, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on site so the first electric buses run, and charge midday, before the upgrade is finished. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
