Bay Shore was running electric school buses before New York required them. In autumn 2019 four Blue Bird Vision electric buses went into service on Bay Shore routes. They were paid for in part by a $695,509 EPA grant, and the district's contractor, Suffolk Transportation Service (STS), operates them. Seven years on, the district has not yet started a NYSERDA fleet plan. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 5,529 students in 2024–25, down from 5,604 in 2023–24 and 5,608 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 6.3 square miles in Suffolk County, in NYSED's Long Island labor market region.
- **Density:** about 880 students per square mile. Routes are short. The challenge is the number of buses charging at once, not range.
- **Budget:** $211,651,256 for 2026–27, up 4.71% (NYSED property tax report card). Transportation is $10,642,223, almost flat on the year before (district budget issue).

## Fleet and transportation

Bay Shore contracts its transportation to STS, which owns the buses. The electric bus project is the best documented early case on Long Island:

- **May 2018:** the district decides to apply to the EPA for electric buses and charging.
- **4 February 2019:** the EPA accepts the proposal.
- **2019:** a $695,509 Diesel Emissions Reduction Act (DERA) grant goes to Bay Shore UFSD to replace four diesel buses with electric ones and pay for charging stations.
- **September 2019:** the four buses arrive in the first week of September and start running routes about a month later.
- **19 November 2019:** the EPA marks the project at a ceremony in Bay Shore.

The project was named C.L.E.A.N. B.U.S.E.S. The diesel buses it replaced were 2001 models that each ran about 10,000 miles a year. STS owns the four electric buses and one of the two charging stations, which cost about $50,000 each. Each electric bus cost about $330,000. State sales tax added close to $30,000 per bus (School Transportation News).

**The partnership grew.** In May 2021 STS and Bay Shore announced a second DERA grant of more than $1 million for seven more electric buses. That grant extended the "Clean Buses" initiative to neighbouring [Brentwood](/electric-school-buses/new-york/brentwood-union-free-school-district) and Central Islip (School Bus Fleet, May 2021). In 2023, School Transportation News gave STS its Green Bus Summit Fleet Award (Islip Bulletin).

## Depot power and the grid

The 2019 project did not just add chargers. It came with a new transformer and new power distribution panels (School Transportation News). That is the step that usually takes longest on a depot, and Bay Shore's contractor has already been through it once, at a small scale.

The scale is the issue now. Four buses need a modest connection, while a full fleet serving 5,500 students needs a large one. Bay Shore is on the Long Island Power Authority (LIPA) grid, operated by PSEG Long Island. NYSED's October 2025 report shows that Bay Shore did not submit the state's zero-emission survey and had made **no contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan. Across Long Island, 97 of 124 districts (78%) were in the same position, and only one had a completed plan.

For contracted districts on Long Island, the next transportation contract is where yard power gets decided. See [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging) for options when one yard can't hold every charger.

## Funding

- **Federal:** the $695,509 EPA DERA grant (2019) for four buses and charging. A second DERA grant of more than $1 million, announced in May 2021, covers seven buses across three districts.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers under Bay Shore's name.
- **Local:** transportation costs about $10.6 million a year, paid through the STS contract.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Growing from four electric buses to a full contracted fleet will need far more power at STS yards on the PSEG Long Island grid. While an upgrade is pending, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so new buses run on schedule. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
