Brockport Central School District has its first electric school bus. NYSERDA approved a $257,250 NYSBIP voucher for one IC Bus CE Series electric bus in October 2025 and closed the project in August 2026. That is one bus in a fleet of about 100 vehicles, so nearly all of the transition is still ahead. Brockport also sits in National Grid territory, while RG&E serves Rochester and much of the rest of Monroe County. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 3,151 students in 2024–25, against 3,149 in 2023–24 and 2,986 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 69.6 square miles in western Monroe County, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region. That is about 45 students per square mile.
- **Budget:** the 2026–27 state filing shows spending of $108,501,820, up 5.85%, with a tax levy up 3.40%.

## Fleet and transportation

Brockport owns and runs its buses. NYSDOT recorded **204 safety inspections** of its vehicles, six of them failed, in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026. Buses are inspected twice a year, which points to a fleet of about 100 buses and other vehicles.

## Depot power and the grid

The state's utility territory map places the village of Brockport in **National Grid** territory. Rochester and much of Monroe County are in RG&E territory, and neighbouring Spencerport has its own municipal utility. Brockport's charging work therefore runs through National Grid's upstate process, not RG&E's.

One bus may charge from existing service. A fleet of about 100 cannot. Moving the large-bus fleet to electric will mean dozens of chargers at the bus garage, a larger service and probably new transformer capacity. Large power transformers have taken two to four years to arrive (NIAC, June 2024), so the order of work matters: plan, utility application, then buses.

The plan is in progress. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Brockport's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **underway**. In the Finger Lakes region, 42 of 70 districts had a plan underway, 16 (23%) had finished one and 10 had made no contact with NYSERDA. Six districts in the region reported running electric buses, led by Gates-Chili with 15 of 88. Districts reported 12 more on order.

Lake Ontario winters add load. Buses that preheat while plugged in start their routes with more range, and that preheating counts toward the garage's peak demand. For the grid picture across upstate New York, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** one voucher of $257,250, closed in August 2026.
- **Capital reserves:** NYSED's 2026–27 schedule of reserves lists four capital reserves with projected balances of about $5.1 million, $3.4 million, $650,000 and $150,000. The 2026–27 budget draws $600,000 from the largest.
- **State aid:** as a district-operated fleet, Brockport's bus purchases draw state transportation aid.

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Brockport's garage will need far more power to go from one electric bus to a large share of a 100-vehicle fleet. AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage so new buses run while the National Grid service and transformer upgrades are completed; AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
