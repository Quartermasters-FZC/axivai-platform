AuSable Valley Central School District drives about 475,000 miles a school year, more than 2,600 miles a day, over a district the size of a small county. Its buses work out of two garages, in Au Sable Forks and Keeseville. A facilities study presented in June 2026 raised a third option: build a new charging site with on-site power storage on the Clintonville campus as part of any future school construction. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,048 students in 2024–25, down from 1,102 in 2023–24 and 1,127 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 7% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 274.3 square miles in NYSED's count; the district puts its own coverage at over 300. It takes in all or part of the towns of AuSable, Black Brook, Chesterfield, Jay, Wilmington, Keene, Franklin, Peru and Willsboro, inside the Adirondack Park.
- **Density:** under four students per square mile.
- **Budget:** a revised 2026–27 budget of $38,355,400 passed on 16 June 2026, 1,087 to 512. The first proposal, $38,605,400, carried a 12% levy increase, more than $1 million over the tax cap. The revised plan holds the levy increase to 5.95% and spends $830,450 less than in 2025–26 (NYSED property tax report card; district).

## Fleet and transportation

AuSable Valley owns its fleet and employs its own drivers, monitors, mechanics and dispatcher. The transportation department reports:

- **Fleet:** 46 vehicles, made up of 34 full-size buses, 8 small buses and 4 vans.
- **Riders:** about 1,200 students a day, carried to 9 public, non-public, special education and vocational schools.
- **Runs:** 70 a day, morning and afternoon.
- **Distance:** about 475,000 miles a school year, an average of over 2,600 miles a day. That is roughly 37 miles per run.

The 2026–27 budget cuts reached transportation. The district eliminated its 5 p.m. bus run, made other transportation reductions and delayed equipment replacement purchases.

The June 2026 enrollment and facilities study recommends moving 6th grade to the Middle/High School in 2026–27 and 5th grade in 2027–28, and closing Au Sable Forks Elementary in 2028–29. It estimates the extra ride time for some 5th and 6th graders at 10 to 12 minutes, with no extra buses needed. Its list of savings also includes closing a bus depot and moving its equipment. No final decisions have been made.

## Depot power and the grid

Both existing garages were named in the scope of the district's 2024 building work: the $24 million capital project and the $5.5 million energy performance contract (EPC), which also covers the district's biomass heating plants. Voters approved both on 14 February 2024, 349 to 141 and 363 to 119. The published project wording covers alterations to the garages but does not mention chargers.

The facilities study looks further ahead. It suggests that a new elementary school on the Middle/High School campus, estimated at about $63 million, could also give the district a place to "transition to electric buses". The idea is a new charging and parking area with power storage, and possibly a solar canopy over the buses. That would put the depot on the campus most buses already serve.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists the district's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as **Underway**. Across the North Country, only 4 of 65 districts had a finished plan; 36 were underway and 23 (36%) had made no contact. North Country districts are served mainly by NYSEG and National Grid, and six told NYSED they will need a new bus depot.

Three issues should drive the FEP here:

- **Route energy in deep cold.** About 37 miles per run on average, over mountain roads, leaves margin on paper. The longest runs need testing against winter range with preheating while plugged in.
- **One depot or two.** Wiring two garages costs more than one. If the district consolidates depots, chargers belong at the one it keeps.
- **Service upgrades.** A full-size fleet of 34 buses needs a large service. A load letter from the utility early in the FEP shows how many chargers fit without an upgrade.

For the wider grid picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for AuSable Valley.
- **Local:** the 2026–27 budget leaned on $1.2 million of fund balance, and the district warns that this level will "significantly affect" its reserves. Bus replacement will need NYSBIP vouchers and state transportation aid to carry the electric premium.
- **Building aid:** the facilities study prices a new school at an 80% state aid rate, leaving about $12.6 million to local taxpayers over 20 years. The study places its electric bus charging site inside that same project.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

A new charging site tied to a future school project would take years, and buses bought from 2032 may need power first. AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at an existing garage so those buses run while the permanent depot and its grid connection are built; AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
