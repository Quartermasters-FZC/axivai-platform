Clymer Central School District has already seen what electric buses would mean for its routes. A NYSERDA study found that six of its eight runs would need a midday charge, and that its bus garage has enough power for one charger. At the same time, Clymer is weighing a merger with Sherman and Panama that would put all three districts' buses under one roof. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 317 students in 2024–25, down from 340 in 2023–24 and 345 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is an 8% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 77.85 square miles in the southwest corner of Chautauqua County, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region.
- **Density:** about four students per square mile.
- **Merger study:** a 150-page study by White and Partners, presented in June 2026, found a merger of Clymer, Sherman and Panama "feasible" and beneficial to all three. Combined enrollment has fallen 52% in 30 years. The study projects $71.5 million in merger aid over 14 years. On 13 July 2026 all three boards voted to continue, using a community petition to gauge support; Clymer needs 160 signatures. The earliest a merged district could start is 1 July 2027 (Post-Journal).

## Fleet and transportation

Clymer owns and runs its buses. NYSDOT recorded 28 safety inspections of its vehicles, with none failed, in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026. Buses are inspected twice a year, which points to a fleet of about 14 buses and other vehicles. In May 2025 voters approved a proposition to buy school buses (202 to 67) and a new transportation reserve fund for buses and related equipment (202 to 69), along with the 2025–26 budget (196 to 79) (Observer).

The NYSERDA study, presented to the board in December 2024, set out what the garage allows:

- **Bus size.** The study sized batteries to the buses that fit the garage. Larger batteries would need a longer chassis, "which would not fit our bus garage," the superintendent told the board.
- **Route length.** Two routes have too many miles for an electric bus on one charge and would have to change.
- **Midday charging.** Six of the eight runs would need a charge in the middle of the day to complete the afternoon run.
- **Extra trips.** The study covered home-to-school runs only. BOCES runs count, but athletics and other trips would need more charging time during the day.

The board discussed a larger garage and the possibility of buying one or two longer buses alongside buses of the current size. Height, weight, length and the garage doors were all raised as concerns (Observer).

The merger matters here too. The study says a merged district would need to choose one facility for all transportation and plan for electric buses. The longest current ride in the three districts is 50 minutes, and the study says no ride should exceed an hour (Post-Journal).

## Depot power and the grid

National Grid serves the bus garage and gave the district a report on the grid capacity there, including how many chargers the garage can take and how fast they can charge. The answer was tight. In May 2025 the business official told the board that, with current infrastructure, the garage has enough power for **one charger, enough for two buses**. Going past that "would take some work" (Post-Journal).

Midday charging adds a cost problem. The superintendent noted that charging in the middle of the day comes at the highest rate, while overnight charging is cheapest. Managing that balance will most likely need extra staff (Observer).

Lake-effect snow off Lake Erie makes winter range a real constraint on runs that are already too long for one charge.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists Clymer's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as "Underway". Across Western New York, 18 of 80 districts (22%) had a completed plan and 13 (16%) had made no contact with NYSERDA.

For the wider upstate picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Clymer.
- **Local:** voters created a transportation reserve fund in May 2025, which can save money for future bus purchases.
- **Merger aid:** if the merger passes, the study projects $71.5 million in reorganization incentive aid over 14 years.

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Clymer's garage can power one charger today, while six of eight runs need a midday top-up. AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage, adding charging capacity while the National Grid upgrade, or a merged district's new depot plan, is worked out. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
