Greater Amsterdam is one of only four Mohawk Valley districts with a completed NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan. It has also chosen to stop running buses itself. In 2025 it moved most routes to First Student, and its 2026–27 budget shifts the rest, so the district can sell its own buses. NYSED lists the district as Amsterdam City School District; it calls itself the Greater Amsterdam School District (GASD). For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 3,569 students in 2024–25, against 3,543 in 2023–24 and 3,550 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 77.44 square miles in NYSED's Mohawk Valley labor market region. That works out to about 46 students per square mile: a small city with rural towns around it.
- **Budget:** the 2026–27 budget is $111,956,616, up 6.95% from $104,680,842, with the tax levy held flat at $21,501,377 (NYSED Property Tax Report Card). The district expected $83.04 million in state aid (Daily Gazette). The budget went to voters on 19 May 2026 and did not need a revote (NYSED).

## Fleet and transportation

For about 50 years Greater Amsterdam's buses were run by Brown Transportation, a Student Transportation of America (STA) company. That contract was expected to cost $3.7 million in 2024–25 for 44 runs (Daily Gazette).

On 12 February 2025 the Board of Education voted 5–0 to give the contract to First Student for 2025–26, 2026–27 and 2027–28. First Student offered a fleet of 48 new buses and two vans, with GPS tracking and cameras. The board president said the company's electric-bus experience was one reason for the choice, given the state mandate (Daily Gazette). The district gets about 90% state aid on transportation costs.

The district kept a few runs of its own in 2025–26, with only a couple of part-time drivers. The 2026–27 budget adds money to move those last runs to First Student. The superintendent said selling the district's buses should bring in about $800,000, and that the district would no longer carry the cost of the electric mandate itself. "We let the professionals that do this on a national basis take over transportation," he said (Daily Gazette, April 2026). High school students can also ride CDTA buses free.

## Depot power and the grid

Once the district's own buses are sold, charging will happen at First Student's yard, not on district property. The electric bill and any service upgrade then fall on the contractor, and the cost comes back to the district through contract prices.

The district has a head start on the planning. NYSED's October 2025 report lists its NYSERDA FEP as "Complete". Only 4 of 54 Mohawk Valley districts (7%) were at that stage; 33 were underway and 11 had made no contact. Mohawk Valley districts get most of their power from National Grid and NYSEG, and 13 told NYSED they expect to need service upgrades for a fully electric fleet.

The contract runs to June 2028, four years before the 2032 purchase deadline. The next bid is where the district can set electric-bus terms: how many electric buses, when, and who pays for yard power. For charging options away from the main yard, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Amsterdam. First Student holds NYSBIP vouchers in other upstate districts, including 20 in Syracuse and 10 in Albany. STA holds three each for nearby Fonda-Fultonville and Johnstown.
- **Bus reserve:** the district's replacement-bus reserve held $697,398 (NYSED schedule of reserves, 2026–27).
- **Fund balance:** unrestricted fund balance is 15.18% of the 2026–27 budget, above the 4% state limit (NYSED).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Greater Amsterdam's electric buses will charge at First Student's yard on the local grid. If that yard needs more power before the utility can deliver it, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so the first electric buses can run while the upgrade is built.
