Walton Central School District has a finished fleet electrification plan. What it does not have is a place to put chargers. Its bus garage on Delaware Street sits in the regulatory floodway of West Brook and the West Branch of the Delaware River, and it has flooded again and again. In October 2026 voters turned down a $24 million capital project that would have moved the garage to the Stockton Avenue campus. It failed by 22 votes. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 771 students in 2024–25, down from 782 in 2023–24 and 807 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 4% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 147.1 square miles in Delaware County, in NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about five students per square mile. Bus runs are long and carry few riders over Delaware County hill roads.
- **Watershed:** Walton lies in the New York City water supply watershed. That is why the Catskill Watershed Corporation (CWC), which runs flood-mitigation programs paid for by the City, is helping with the garage move.

## Fleet and transportation

Walton owns and runs its own buses out of the Delaware Street garage, in the Village of Walton. The CWC describes it as a critical community facility "subject to repeated flooding". Walton's Local Flood Analysis recommends moving it.

The floodway matters for electric buses for two reasons:

- **Chargers can't stay.** Charging equipment, switchgear and a new utility service are expensive and sit at ground level. Putting them in a building that floods again and again would put them at risk.
- **Buses must get out.** The CWC notes that a site outside the floodway would let the district keep transporting students during extreme weather.

So the electric fleet waits on the garage. Until the garage moves, any electric bus Walton buys has no permanent, flood-safe place to charge.

## Depot power and the grid

**The new site.** The district plans to build on a parcel it already owns, next to the high school campus on Stockton Avenue and outside the floodway. CWC papers from early 2026 put the garage's construction cost at about $14,976,491. They set it inside a wider $21.3 million capital project, of which the district planned to pay about $1.75 million itself. Stormwater work was to go to the CWC as a separate application. The Village of Walton passed a resolution letting the district join the NYC-funded flood buyout program for the old site.

**The plan exists.** NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Walton's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Complete". Only five of the 63 districts in the Southern Tier region had reached that stage, and 29 had made no contact with NYSERDA at all. The FEP sizes the chargers and the service upgrade a fleet needs. That makes it the right input for designing a new garage's electrical room.

**The utility.** NYSED reports that Southern Tier districts get their power mainly from NYSEG. A new garage on an empty parcel needs a new service, and its size should come from the FEP rather than a diesel-era load estimate. Asking the utility early for a load letter shows how big that service can be and how long it will take.

For the wider upstate grid picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Garage design:** in January 2026 the district asked the CWC for $967,787 in design funding to relocate the garage. The March 2026 agenda revised the request to $955,587. Design costs are eligible up to 10% of the estimated construction cost.
- **Capital vote:** Proposition 1 covered the garage move, elevator replacements in all three buildings, new boilers at the middle/high school and a secure entrance at Townsend Elementary. It failed 338 to 360. A second proposition for a drop-off loop at Townsend also failed. The two came to $24 million (WIVT, 14 October 2026).
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Walton.

Program amounts and rules are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Walton has a plan but no flood-safe charging site, and a new garage is now at least one more vote away. AXIVAI brings battery power to a yard by truck, on higher ground, so the first electric buses can run while the garage and its grid connection are settled. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
