Avon Central School District has already finished the study most districts are still waiting on. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Avon's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as complete. Only 16 of the 70 districts in the Finger Lakes region (23%) were that far along. Avon still buys buses the usual way: one bus a year, put to voters each May. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 872 students in 2024–25, down from 876 in 2023–24 and 897 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 3% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 50.09 square miles in Livingston County, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Density:** about 17 students per square mile. That is a mid-sized rural district: routes are shorter than in the Southern Tier but still spread out.
- **Budget:** $29,834,388 for 2026–27, up 7.89% from $27,652,546 in 2025–26 (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

Avon owns and runs its own buses and replaces them through annual ballot propositions. The 19 May 2026 ballot had three items that touch transportation and facilities:

| Proposition | What it authorized | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | One 64-passenger school bus, up to $193,000 | Passed 279–128 |
| 3 | Up to $175,000 from the 2022 Vehicle and Equipment Capital Reserve Fund, for equipment including transportation | Passed 298–112 |
| 4 | A $4,570,000 capital improvement project, using $1,055,000 from the 2021 Building Capital Reserve Fund | Passed 253–156 |

The bus proposition drew the most "no" votes of the transportation items. The capital project covers the Elementary School, the Middle/High School and site work. The public notice doesn't mention the bus parking area or chargers.

Avon's reserves are fairly deep for a district of its size. Its schedule of reserves, filed with the 2026–27 property tax report card, lists several capital reserve funds. The building reserve, at about $4.67 million, is the largest. That money is earmarked for buildings, not buses. But it shows the district plans capital spending years ahead.

## Depot power and the grid

National Grid supplies electricity in the Town of Avon, and RG&E supplies gas (Livingston County utility directory). Livingston County is split between three electric utilities: National Grid, NYSEG and RG&E. That makes the town-level split worth knowing. Avon's buses will charge on National Grid's network.

The completed FEP is Avon's main asset here. A finished plan should already estimate:

- how many chargers the campus lot needs, and of what power;
- whether the existing campus service can carry them or needs an upgrade;
- which runs fit a single overnight charge in a Livingston County winter.

The next step is to take that load estimate to National Grid for a formal service request. In New York the utility side of a depot upgrade often takes longer than buying the buses. See [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap) and [grid connection wait times for EV charging](/research/ev-charging-grid-connection-wait-times).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Avon. The bus approved in May 2026 was not vouchered under the program.
- **Local:** Avon funds buses year by year, through propositions paid from fund balance, tax or reserves. A move to electric would add NYSBIP vouchers and state transportation aid to that cycle.

Program amounts are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Avon's FEP shows the campus service needs a National Grid upgrade, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the bus lot so the first electric buses run while that work is done. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
