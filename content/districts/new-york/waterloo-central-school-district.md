Waterloo Central School District has a state voucher for an electric school bus, approved in July 2025. A year later it told voters its next bus purchase would be diesel. Both facts sit in public records, and together they show where the district is: one electric bus funded, the rest of the 18 buses on daily runs still diesel, and a fleet plan with NYSERDA in progress. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,357 students in 2024–25, against 1,338 in 2023–24 and 1,367 in 2022–23 (NYSED). Enrollment is roughly flat.
- **Area:** 83.08 square miles in Seneca County, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Density:** about 16 students per square mile. Buses run from the village of Waterloo out to the rural towns around it.
- **Schools:** four: Skoi-Yase Primary, LaFayette Intermediate, Waterloo Middle and Waterloo High, all in the village (NCES).
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 budget of $52,433,420 on 19 May 2026, 237 to 140. That is a 4.83% rise on the 2025–26 budget of $50,018,410, the largest school budget in Seneca County this year, with a 1.5% tax levy increase (NYSED property tax report card; Finger Lakes 1).

## Fleet and transportation

Waterloo owns and runs its own buses. Its 2026 budget newsletter reports 18 buses on daily runs.

The district buys buses from a dedicated Bus and Vehicle Capital Reserve Fund rather than borrowing. On 19 May 2026, voters approved Proposition 2, 263 to 114. It authorizes up to $676,345.11 in bus purchases from that reserve, with no added tax levy. The district's reserve schedule filed with NYSED says the money is for three 70-passenger school buses. The budget FAQ answers the obvious question directly: "We will be purchasing diesel buses."

**The electric bus.** NYSERDA's NYSBIP project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists one voucher for Waterloo:

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Bus | Thomas Built Buses Saf-T-Liner C2 Electric "Jouley", model year 2027 |
| Weight class | Class 7 (26,001–33,000 lb) |
| Dealer | Matthews Buses |
| Voucher | $257,250 |
| Approved | 15 July 2025 |
| Status (Oct 2026) | Approved, not yet closed |
| Operator | District (no third-party operator listed) |
| Priority district | Yes |

The project was still open 15 months after approval, so the bus had not completed the program's close-out by October 2026. Waterloo is one of 77 districts statewide with any NYSBIP voucher.

**The garage.** In February 2023, voters approved a $17.9 million capital project, 88 to 41. It covered all four schools, Tom Coughlin Field and the bus garage, funded by $14.9 million in bonds and $3 million from the capital reserve (Finger Lakes Times). Public coverage of the project names the garage but does not describe charger wiring there.

## Depot power and the grid

Waterloo's schools sit in NYSEG territory, according to the state's electric utility service territory map. NYSEG will decide how much power the garage can draw for chargers and what an upgrade costs.

NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Waterloo's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". The FEP sizes chargers and the service upgrade a fleet needs. Across the Finger Lakes region, 16 of 70 districts (23%) had a completed plan and 42 (60%) were underway. Twenty-seven districts in the region expect to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet, and seven expect to expand their bus depot.

One electric bus can usually charge from a single Level 2 charger on existing service. The question comes later, when several buses charge overnight in winter. The FEP should show where Waterloo's garage service runs out, and NYSEG's response to a load request sets the timeline for the rest. The upstate grid picture is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** one voucher, $257,250, approved 15 July 2025, flagged as a priority district.
- **Local:** bus purchases come from the Bus and Vehicle Capital Reserve Fund. The 2026–27 proposition draws $676,345.11 from it for diesel buses.
- **Capital:** the 2023 capital project paid for garage upgrades through bonds and reserves.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).
