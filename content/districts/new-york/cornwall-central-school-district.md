Cornwall holds one of the largest electric school-bus awards in New York outside the big cities. NYSERDA approved 10 vouchers for the district on 17 September 2025, worth $2,572,500. The buses are run by its contractor, Student Transportation of America (STA). Yet NYSED's October 2025 report listed Cornwall as having made no contact with NYSERDA about a fleet plan. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 2,985 students in 2024–25, against 2,989 in 2023–24 and 2,967 in 2022–23 (NYSED). Enrollment is flat.
- **Area:** 38.27 square miles, in NYSED's Hudson Valley labor market region.
- **Density:** about 78 students per square mile. That is suburban by upstate standards.
- **Communities:** the village of Cornwall-on-Hudson, the Town of Cornwall and parts of New Windsor and Woodbury. The district runs three elementary schools, a middle school and a high school on NY 94.

## Fleet and transportation

Cornwall contracts out its buses, as most Hudson Valley districts do. NYSED found that 49 of 83 responding districts in the region (59%) contract out all their transportation, and only 21 (25%) run their own fleets.

The NYSBIP project gives the clearest public picture of what is coming:

| Item | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Buses | 10 IC Bus CE Series, model year 2027, battery electric |
| Weight class | Class 7 (26,001–33,000 lbs), full-size buses |
| Operator | Student Transportation of America |
| Dealer | Navistar Inc. |
| Voucher | $257,250 per bus; $2,572,500 in all |
| Approved | 17 September 2025; status "Approved" (not yet closed) as of 26 October 2026 |
| Project location | New Windsor, ZIP 12553, inside a disadvantaged community |
| Priority district | Yes |

Ten full-size buses at once is a large first step. Only five NYSBIP recipients statewide have more: New York City, Buffalo, Syracuse, Evans-Brant and Albany. In Orange County, only the Enlarged City School District of Middletown matches it, with 10 buses also run by STA.

## Depot power and the grid

The buses will charge where STA keeps them. The NYSBIP project is located in New Windsor, not on district property, at a site in Central Hudson Gas & Electric's territory according to the state's utility territory data. Ten Class 7 buses need a charging site sized for overnight charging of the whole group, plus space for more as the fleet grows.

The district's own planning lags its contractor's. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Cornwall's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) status as **no contact**. Across the Hudson Valley, 56 of 101 districts (56%) were in the same position, and only five had a completed plan. The region reported 23 zero-emission buses on order across nine districts. Cornwall's 10 alone are close to that number.

For a contracted district, a fleet plan matters for two reasons:

- **Route energy.** It models which runs a battery bus can cover in winter and where midday charging is needed.
- **The next contract.** It sets what the district should require from its contractor on depot power, charging and backup when buses are fully electric.

For charging away from the main yard, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** $2,572,500 in vouchers for 10 buses, approved 17 September 2025. Cornwall is flagged as a priority district, and the project sits in a disadvantaged community. $257,250 is the amount for a new Type C bus with the priority, scrappage and vehicle-to-grid amounts added, the most common award in the dataset. Ten buses was also the most a priority district could claim under the caps in force until 1 December 2025 (the greater of 10 buses or 10% of the fleet), as set out in our [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).
- **The clock:** program rules require a voucher to be redeemed within 15 months of approval, once chargers are installed, working and paid for. NYSERDA can grant up to 12 more months. For a September 2025 approval, that points to December 2026 unless extended, so the charging site has to be ready soon.
- **Structure:** the vouchers are tied to STA as third-party operator. The district pays for the service through its transportation contract, not through bus propositions.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Ten electric buses arriving together need a lot of power at one contractor yard. If the yard's grid upgrade isn't ready when they arrive, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so the buses run while the connection is completed. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
