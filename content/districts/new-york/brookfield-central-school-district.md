Brookfield Central School District has 182 students spread over 67 square miles, under three per square mile. It runs a small fleet from its own bus garage and buys buses straight out of its operating budget rather than through separate bond votes. The 2026 capital project is replacing the garage doors and work-bay lighting. Charging could be planned into the building now, but it is not part of the current work. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 182 students in 2024–25, down from 195 in both 2023–24 and 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 7% fall in one year.
- **Need:** 60% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 66.86 square miles in southeastern Madison County, in NYSED's Central New York labor market region. That is about **2.7 students per square mile**.
- **Campus:** one school, on Fairground Road in Brookfield.
- **Budget:** $8,573,168 for 2026–27, up 3.15%. The tax levy of $1,721,333 rises 2%, below the district's 2.95% limit.

## Fleet and transportation

Brookfield owns and runs its buses. NYSDOT recorded 23 safety inspections of its vehicles, three of them failed, in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026. Buses are inspected twice a year, which points to a fleet of about a dozen buses and other vehicles.

The 2026–27 budget shows how a district this size pays for transportation:

| Line (2026–27 budget) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| District transportation (operations) | $611,990 |
| Purchase of bus | $227,000 |
| Transportation aid (revenue) | $676,202 |

The $227,000 bus line buys one 60-passenger bus and replaces a minivan that "is used daily and accumulating many miles", in the district's words. The district says the purchases keep the fleet safe and limit costly repairs on aging buses. If voters had rejected the budget, bus purchases would have been deferred.

Buying one large bus a year inside the operating budget works for diesel. An electric bus costs much more. NYSBIP vouchers for a large electric bus have run at $257,250 each in recent awards, more than the district's whole bus line. From July 2032 every new purchase must be zero-emission, so Brookfield will need vouchers or a separate proposition to keep the same one-bus-a-year pattern.

## Depot power and the grid

The state's utility territory map places the Brookfield area in **NYSEG** territory.

The bus garage is part of the capital project the voters approved. Work was set to start on 4 May 2026, with substantial completion expected by 27 September 2026. At the garages it covers:

- **Bus garage:** two new entrance doors and upgraded lighting in the work bays and storage bays;
- **Storage garage:** seven new overhead doors and one entrance door.

Indoor storage helps electric buses in a Madison County winter, because a bus parked inside and plugged in starts with a warm battery. The project does not list chargers or electrical service work. The district's newsletter also noted a "good old fashioned" 2025–26 winter, with long spells below freezing.

Planning has started. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Brookfield's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **underway**. Central New York is behind other regions: of 49 districts, 3 had finished a plan and 20 (41%) had made no contact with NYSERDA. Only one district in the region, Fabius-Pompey, reported running an electric bus, and none reported any on order.

For the grid picture across upstate New York, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Operating budget:** buses are bought within the budget ($227,000 in 2026–27, up $52,000 from $175,000).
- **Transportation aid:** $676,202 budgeted for 2026–27. The aid is based on spending, so the prior year's costs drive the next year's aid.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Brookfield.

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the fleet plan shows the garage's NYSEG service is too small for chargers, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage so Brookfield's first electric bus runs while the upgrade is completed. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
