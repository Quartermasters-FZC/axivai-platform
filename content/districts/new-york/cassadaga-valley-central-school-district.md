Cassadaga Valley is already running electric school buses on rural Chautauqua County roads. NYSERDA approved vouchers for six Thomas Built "Jouley" electric buses in June 2024, worth $1,543,500, and the project closed in May 2026. In NYSED's October 2025 survey, the district already counted four electric buses in a fleet of 27, the second-highest share in Western New York. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 745 students in 2024–25, down from 755 in 2023–24 and 767 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Need:** 59% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 141.3 square miles in central Chautauqua County, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region. That is about five students per square mile.
- **Base:** the district's buses are registered to Sinclairville in state inspection and voucher records.
- **Budget:** the 2026–27 state filing shows spending of $28,745,237, up 4.99%.

## Fleet and transportation

Cassadaga Valley owns and runs its own buses. It reported a fleet of **27 buses** to NYSED in 2025. NYSDOT recorded 51 safety inspections of its vehicles, three of them failed, in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026, which fits a fleet of that size inspected twice a year.

The electric buses came through one NYSBIP project:

In the NYSED survey, Cassadaga Valley reported four electric buses, or 14% of its fleet. Only Lake Shore (Evans-Brant), at 47%, reported a higher share in the region. NYSERDA closed the six-bus project in May 2026. Six electric buses in a 27-bus fleet is a 22% share.

The district is still buying buses from reserve. NYSED's 2026–27 schedule of reserves lists a bus reserve of about $1.56 million, with the stated intent to seek voter approval in May 2026 to spend $1,010,206 on "school buses and similar vehicles".

## Depot power and the grid

The state's utility territory map places the Sinclairville area in **National Grid** territory.

Six full-size electric buses need six chargers, or careful sharing of fewer, at the bus garage. Every bus added after this one has to fit in the same service. Two local conditions shape the next round:

- **Lake-effect winters.** Chautauqua County's snow belt cuts winter range and adds cabin-heating load. Buses that preheat while plugged in start their routes with more range.
- **Long rural runs.** At five students per square mile, some runs are long. The district's own operating data will show which runs an electric bus can cover on one charge and which need a midday top-up.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists the district's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **underway**. In Western New York, 18 of 80 districts (22%) had a finished plan and 13 (16%) had made no contact with NYSERDA. Districts in the region reported 16 more electric buses on order.

For the grid picture across upstate New York, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** $1,543,500 for six buses. Cassadaga Valley holds 6 of the 461 buses vouchered statewide, in NYSERDA data updated 26 October 2026.
- **Bus reserve:** about $1.56 million, with $1,010,206 proposed for bus purchases in May 2026.
- **Capital reserve:** about $3.05 million set aside for the local share of future capital projects (NYSED schedule of reserves, 2026–27).

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Cassadaga Valley's next electric buses will need charging capacity beyond what its first six use. AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage so new buses run while any National Grid upgrade is completed; AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
