Moriah Central School District bought its first electric school buses on a three-vote margin. On 21 May 2024, voters in this Essex County district approved two zero-emission buses 157 to 154. NYSERDA's vouchers covered $514,500 of the cost. Two years later the district asked voters for a third electric bus and won by a wider margin, 181 to 113. Then, in June 2026, the board put that purchase on hold once the state deadline moved to 2040. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 635 students in 2024–25, down from 651 in 2023–24 and 668 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 5% fall in two years.
- **Need:** 65% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 76.26 square miles around Port Henry and Mineville on Lake Champlain, in NYSED's North Country labor market region.
- **Budget:** voters approved $22,237,254 for 2026–27 on 19 May 2026, 211 to 82.

## Fleet and transportation

Moriah owns and runs its buses. It buys them through bus propositions on the May ballot. The recent record shows how the district mixes electric and diesel purchases:

| Vote | Proposition | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 21 May 2024 | Two zero-emission electric buses, up to $843,357, with $514,500 in grant funding | Passed 157–154 |
| 20 May 2025 | One diesel student transportation vehicle, up to $152,276 | Passed 226–58 |
| 19 May 2026 | One 66-passenger diesel bus (up to $197,000), one electric bus with a wheelchair lift (up to $484,589), one gasoline SUV (up to $42,000); $265,250 in NYSBIP funding | Passed 181–113 |

The 2024 electric buses are Navistar CE Series battery-electric buses, Class 7, model year 2026, sold by Leonard Bus Sales. NYSERDA approved the vouchers on 10 April 2024 and closed the project on 24 June 2026.

The 2026 electric bus was meant to replace the district's aging wheelchair-accessible bus. On 16 June 2026 the board recorded that, with the mandate "pushed out to 2040", the purchase is on hold until further notice. The district will buy only the diesel bus and borrow less, and the 2026–27 budget is unaffected.

The board kept the community involved along the way. Residents were offered an electric bus ride at report card night in November 2025.

## Depot power and the grid

Moriah's campus on Viking Lane sits in National Grid's service territory (Department of Public Service territory map). Any chargers at the bus garage will draw from a National Grid service.

The garage has seen repair work but no public charging plan:

- Voters approved a $14.96 million capital project on 17 December 2019, 168 to 21. It included $1,041,601 for the bus garage: plumbing fixtures, emergency and exit lights, a new wash bay with water and sanitary upgrades, a review of the below-grade fuel storage, and accessibility work. With 95.3% building aid, the local share of the whole project was $703,120.
- In December 2023 the board rejected a bid for bus garage construction. In May 2024 it discussed construction of the bus garage parking lot.

None of these documents mention chargers, conduit or a service upgrade. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Moriah's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". Across the North Country's 65 districts, only 4 (6%) had a completed plan and 23 (36%) had made no contact with NYSERDA.

Winter matters in the Adirondacks, because a cold battery loses range. An FEP should check each route against winter range before the district commits to more electric buses.

The wider grid picture upstate is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** two vouchers of $257,250 each, $514,500 in all, approved 10 April 2024. NYSERDA's data flags Moriah as a priority district. Moriah is one of 79 NYSBIP recipients statewide.
- **The local math in 2024:** the district financed the remaining $328,857 and expected 90% state transportation aid over 12 years. The budget newsletter put the net cost to the district at $32,886 for both buses. In June 2024 the board heard that, under the state's revised aid formula, the district's share was projected to be fully covered by transportation aid.
- **The local math in 2026:** for the three-vehicle purchase, the district expected $265,250 from NYSBIP and $412,505 in transportation aid, leaving $45,834 for the district.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Moriah already runs a garage on a National Grid service and is weighing more electric buses. If the next buses need more power at the garage than the current service gives, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so they run while the upgrade is completed.
