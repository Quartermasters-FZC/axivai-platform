Berlin Central covers more ground than any other district in the Capital Region: 255 square miles of eastern Rensselaer County, with 579 students. That is about two students per square mile. In 2026 its voters turned down a $9.45 million capital project in March and a first budget in May, then passed a reduced budget in June. Through all of it they approved two new diesel buses. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 579 students in 2024–25, down from 625 in 2023–24 and 633 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is an 8.5% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 255 square miles, the largest of any district in the Capital Region table of NYSED's October 2025 report. The next largest, Taconic Hills, covers 196 square miles.
- **Communities:** the district held its 2026 budget meetings in Berlin, Petersburgh, Stephentown and Grafton, which shows how far apart the towns it serves are.
- **Budget:** a $26,799,680 budget failed on 19 May 2026, 210 to 213. A revised $26,351,128 budget with a 2.0% levy increase passed on 16 June 2026, 442 to 273.

## Fleet and transportation

Berlin owns and runs its own buses from a transportation facility on School Bus Lane in Berlin. The buses carry GPS trackers, and the district has added AI-powered stop-arm cameras through a Rensselaer County partnership with BusPatrol.

Fleet replacement continues on diesel. On 19 May 2026 voters approved the purchase of two 66-passenger diesel buses, at up to $355,000, by 233 to 189. The bus proposition passed on the same day the budget failed.

NYSERDA's NYSBIP data, updated 26 October 2026, list no electric bus vouchers for Berlin.

## Depot power and the grid

Two facts from 2026 frame the depot question.

- **Capital money is hard to win.** On 17 March 2026 voters rejected both parts of a $9,450,000 capital project. The $6,350,000 critical infrastructure proposition lost 157 to 392, and the $3,100,000 additional priorities proposition lost 169 to 381, even with a 75.4% state building aid ratio. Any charger and service upgrade at the bus garage would most likely need a future capital vote.
- **Power reliability matters.** The district noted that the March vote day started with widespread power outages. A rural electric bus depot has to plan for outages as well as capacity, because buses that charge overnight need power on the night before a school day.

Berlin's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan was listed as "Underway" in October 2025. The plan should test the longest runs across a 255-square-mile district against winter battery range, and size the garage service for the fleet the district will actually need. Across the Capital Region, 31 of 74 districts (42%) had made no contact with NYSERDA, so Berlin is ahead of many neighbours on planning.

The wider upstate grid picture is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** no vouchers as of October 2026.
- **Local:** buses are bought through annual propositions, such as the $355,000 approved in May 2026, with state transportation aid repaying a share.
- **Facilities:** the failed March 2026 project shows that voters weigh every capital dollar closely. An electric depot project would need to show its aid and grant share up front.

## Where AXIVAI fits

For a district where capital votes are hard to pass and outages happen, waiting on a garage service upgrade is a real risk. AXIVAI brings battery power by truck into storage at the garage, so the first electric buses can charge before a permanent upgrade is funded and built.
