Westbury is one of the few Long Island districts with electric buses already funded. In July 2026 NYSERDA approved ten vouchers, worth $1.96 million, for electric buses run by the district's contractor, First Student. Two years earlier the EPA picked Westbury for a $2.02 million Clean School Bus rebate, also for ten buses. The district has not yet started a NYSERDA fleet plan. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 4,328 students in 2024–25, down from 4,363 in 2023–24 and 4,447 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a fall of about 3% in two years. The district's 2026–27 budget presentation cites declining enrollment as a source of savings.
- **Area:** 3.9 square miles, in NYSED's Long Island labor market region.
- **Density:** about 1,100 students per square mile. Routes are short, and many students in the lowest grades ride.
- **Schools:** six buildings, from Dryden Street Elementary (Pre-K–K) to Westbury High School.
- **Budget:** the proposed 2026–27 budget is $209,932,355, up 4.27%, with a 2.45% tax levy increase. Transportation is $13,278,100 of it, up $334,881 (2.59%).

## Fleet and transportation

Westbury does not own buses. First Student runs its routes, and families track buses through First Student's FirstView app.

Eligibility is generous by state standards. Kindergartners ride at any distance, grades 1–5 ride from half a mile, and grades 6–12 from one mile. Small walk limits in a 3.9-square-mile district mean more riders per square mile, and more buses on the road at the same bell times.

The current contracts came from RFP 2022-16-A, which sought bids for transportation from 1 July 2023 to 30 June 2028, with terms of one, three or five years. The RFP split the work into large buses (65–66 passengers), vans and lift buses (20–30 passengers), athletic and field trips, and summer service.

**The district buys the fuel.** For school-year home-to-school runs, Westbury supplies the fuel itself. The RFP sets the allocation at 10 miles per gallon for large buses and 15 for vans, based on route mileage verified by the district's routing software. Any fuel used beyond that is the contractor's cost. In the 2023–24 budget, contract transportation fuel was a $216,000 line.

That arrangement matters for electrification. A district that already pays for fuel will need to decide, in its next contract, who pays for charging electricity and who owns the chargers at the yard.

## Depot power and the grid

Westbury's buses are parked and fuelled away from district property. NYSERDA's voucher records place the ten-bus project in Hicksville (ZIP 11801), outside the district, which points to a contractor yard rather than a school site.

On Long Island the grid belongs to the Long Island Power Authority (LIPA), which serves about 1.2 million customers. PSEG Long Island runs it under a contract extended through 31 December 2030. NYSED's October 2025 report names PSEG Long Island as the region's one major utility.

Planning is behind funding. NYSED's report lists Westbury as having **not submitted** its survey and as having made **no contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan. Across Long Island, 97 of 124 districts (78%) were in the same position, and only one had a completed plan.

Ten Type C buses charging overnight at one yard is a modest load. It is still new load on a yard built for diesel, and it arrives with a fixed delivery date. For a district that contracts out its fleet, the fleet plan is where route energy, charger count and the next contract's yard requirements get set down. For charging options away from the main yard, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists ten vouchers for Westbury under application 776528, approved on 14 July 2026. Each is $196,000, for a model-year 2027 Thomas Built Saf-T-Liner C2 Jouley, a Class 7 battery-electric bus. The operator is First Student, and the district carries the priority flag. The total is $1,960,000. Background on the program is in our [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).
- **Federal:** the EPA's 31 May 2024 announcement of Clean School Bus rebates in New York lists Westbury Union Free School District for ten buses and $2,020,000.
- **Local:** transportation is budgeted at $13.3 million for 2026–27. No bus proposition is needed, because the contractor owns the buses.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Westbury's first electric buses are funded, and they will need charging at a contractor yard on the PSEG Long Island grid. If the yard's service upgrade runs behind the bus deliveries, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so the buses run while the utility work is finished.
