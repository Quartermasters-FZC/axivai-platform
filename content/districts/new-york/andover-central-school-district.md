Andover has the fewest students per square mile of any of the 80 districts in Western New York: 233 students across 121.1 square miles of Allegany County, fewer than two per square mile (NYSED). Every bus covers a lot of road for a few riders, and the district buys its buses one at a time from a reserve fund. Its NYSERDA fleet plan was underway in October 2025. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 233 students in 2024–25, against 240 in 2023–24 and 236 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 121.1 square miles in NYSED's Western New York labor market region.
- **Density:** about 1.9 students per square mile, the lowest of the region's 80 districts. The Western New York region as a whole has about 32 (162,544 students on 5,088 square miles, NYSED). Andover's runs are long, with few riders on each.
- **Budget:** the 2026–27 budget is $12.5 million, up 8.7% from $11.5 million. The tax levy rises 2.0%, to $2,820,179, within the tax cap (NYSED Property Tax Report Card). The budget did not go to a revote.

## Fleet and transportation

Andover owns and runs its buses. It replaces them at a steady pace from a capital reserve set up in 2017 for bus purchases. The district's filings with NYSED show the plan for each year, with each purchase subject to a public vote:

| School year | Planned from the bus reserve |
| --- | --- |
| 2024–25 | Up to $150,000 for one bus |
| 2025–26 | Up to $210,000 for one bus |
| 2026–27 | Up to $145,000 for one bus and up to $75,000 for a passenger vehicle |

One bus a year is a slow replacement cycle, and it suits a phased move to electric. From 1 July 2032, each of those yearly purchases must be zero-emission. A district this size can add one electric bus and one charger at a time, and learn from each before buying the next.

The catch is price. NYSBIP vouchers for new electric buses run up to $257,250 each in NYSERDA's current data, more than any single purchase in the reserve plans above. For a district with a $12.5 million budget, whether it wins vouchers will decide how fast it can switch.

## Depot power and the grid

Andover owns its buses, so its chargers will go on district property, not a contractor's yard. Two questions matter most:

- **Range.** On a 121-square-mile map, some runs may be too long for a single winter charge. The fleet plan should test each run against cold-weather battery range, and say whether a midday top-up at the garage is needed.
- **Service size.** One or two chargers may fit within the school's existing electric service. A full fleet may not. An early load letter from the utility shows where that line falls.

Planning has started. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Andover's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". In Western New York, 18 of 80 districts (22%) had a completed plan, 43 (54%) were underway and 13 had made no contact. Districts in the region get most of their power from National Grid, NYSEG and RG&E. Thirty-three told NYSED they expect to need service upgrades to run a fully electric fleet.

The wider upstate grid picture is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's public NYSBIP project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Andover.
- **Local:** bus purchases come from the 2017 bus reserve, each approved by voters.
- **State aid:** as a district-operated fleet, Andover finances buses through bus propositions, reserves and state transportation aid, not through a contractor.

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Andover will add electric buses one at a time on its own property. If the fleet plan shows the school's service can't carry the chargers yet, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on site, so the first electric buses can run while the utility upgrade is done.
