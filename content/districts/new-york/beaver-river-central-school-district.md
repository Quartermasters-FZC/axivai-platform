Beaver River Central School District is saving for electric buses on purpose. Its schedule of reserves, filed with NYSED for 2026–27, shows about $1.17 million in a reserve for bus purchases and transportation. The stated purpose is "to help cover potentially escalating costs resulting from electric bus mandate." In April 2026 NYSERDA approved the district's first electric bus voucher, the only one in Lewis County. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 828 students in 2024–25, down from 833 in 2023–24 and 838 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 248.3 square miles in Lewis County, in NYSED's North Country labor market region.
- **Density:** about 3.3 students per square mile. Few riders spread over long distances is the hardest case for battery range.
- **Budget:** $23,778,123 for 2026–27, up 2.25% from $23,255,700. The tax levy rises 2% to $6,205,480 (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

North Country districts almost all run their own buses. NYSED reports that 53 of 58 responding districts (91%) own and operate all their buses, and 36 have fleets of 20 or fewer. Beaver River pays for buses partly from a dedicated reserve, and its first electric bus is now on order under NYSBIP:

| NYSBIP detail | Beaver River's voucher |
| --- | --- |
| Bus | IC Bus CE Series, model year 2027 |
| Weight class | Class 7 (26,001–33,000 lb) |
| Voucher | $257,250 |
| Approved | 17 April 2026 (status: approved, not yet closed) |
| Priority district | Yes |
| Location listed | Castorland |

Six North Country districts already run electric buses, according to NYSED's October 2025 report: Alexandria (4 of 17), Malone (4 of 47), Salmon River (2 of 54), Brushton-Moira, Norwood-Norfolk and Carthage (1 each). Carthage, in neighbouring Jefferson County, runs one electric bus in a fleet of 75.

The region ranked vehicle range as its single hardest problem, ahead of facility work and utility upgrades. On a 248-square-mile map, the FEP will need to match the new bus to a route it can finish on one winter charge, with the heater running.

## Depot power and the grid

National Grid is the electric utility in Lewis County (NYS Department of Public Service). Beaver River's first bus will charge on National Grid's network at the district's depot.

Beaver River's Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) is listed as **underway**. Across the North Country's 65 districts:

- 4 (6%) had a completed plan and 36 (55%) had one underway;
- 23 (36%) had made no contact with NYSERDA;
- 56 districts had no chargers at all, and 13 expect to need service upgrades for a fully electric fleet;
- 41 of 58 (71%) had a utility needs assessment or a load letter.

A first charger may fit on the existing depot service. The FEP has to show what the second, fifth and tenth buses will need.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** one voucher of $257,250, approved 17 April 2026 (NYSERDA project data, updated 26 October 2026). The district carries the priority flag, which qualifies it for higher voucher amounts.
- **Reserves:** about $1.17 million in a bus and transportation reserve, set aside for the cost of the electric bus mandate. A further $624,000 capital reserve is set aside for an upcoming capital project.
- **Regional context:** NYSED reports that 18 North Country districts won state or local zero-emission funding (average about $253,831), and 16 won federal funding (average about $479,211).

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If later electric buses arrive before National Grid can upgrade the depot service, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so those buses run, and can provide backup power in a North Country winter storm. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
