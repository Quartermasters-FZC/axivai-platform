Jefferson Central School District in Schoharie County has 140 students. It is also one of only four Mohawk Valley districts with a completed NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan. The plan is done; the money is the problem. Voters turned down both the district's budget and a bus purchase in May 2025, and again in May 2026. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

This page covers Jefferson Central School District in Schoharie County. It is not South Jefferson CSD or any district in Jefferson County.

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 140 students in 2024–25, against 143 in 2023–24 and 141 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Need:** 63% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 70.13 square miles in Schoharie County, in NYSED's Mohawk Valley labor market region.
- **Density:** about two students per square mile, so every bus covers a lot of road for few riders.

## Fleet and transportation

Jefferson owns its buses and replaces them through propositions on the May ballot. Two years running, voters said no:

| Vote | Budget | Bus purchase |
| --- | --- | --- |
| May 2025 | About $8.1M with a 1.99% levy increase: failed 93–122 | Failed 100–115 |
| May 2026 | $8,307,303 with a 2.59% levy increase: failed 91–143 | Failed 99–135 |

The 2026 budget was within the tax cap, so it needed only a simple majority and still failed.

For a district this size, one bus vote is a large share of the fleet. Each failed purchase keeps an older bus on the road longer.

## Depot power and the grid

Jefferson's campus on State Route 10 lies in NYSEG's service territory (Department of Public Service territory map).

The district's planning is ahead of most. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Jefferson's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Complete". In the Mohawk Valley region:

- 4 of 54 districts (7%) had a completed plan;
- 33 (61%) were underway;
- 11 (20%) had made no contact with NYSERDA;
- no responding district owned a zero-emission bus, and districts reported two on order.

A completed FEP gives Jefferson a route-by-route energy model and an estimate of the charging and service upgrade it needs. What it lacks is voter approval to spend. In a fleet this small, any charger and service upgrade is spread across very few buses and riders.

The wider grid picture upstate is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Jefferson. See our [NYSBIP review](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program) for how the program has been used.
- **Local:** any bus, diesel or electric, needs a voter-approved proposition. Recent results show the vote is the binding constraint.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Jefferson's fleet plan is finished. If the plan calls for a NYSEG service upgrade that would delay the first electric bus, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the bus runs while the upgrade is completed.
