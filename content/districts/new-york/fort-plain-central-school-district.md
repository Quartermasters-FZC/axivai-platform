Fort Plain is still buying combustion buses, and saying so on the ballot. In May 2026 voters approved "three (3) gas/diesel school vehicles" for about $350,000, drawn from reserves. The purchase deadline moved to 2032, so the district has a few more replacement rounds before the switch is required. Its fleet plan with NYSERDA is under way. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 623 students in 2024–25, down from 638 in 2023–24 and 674 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is an 8% fall in two years.
- **Need:** 68% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 56.64 square miles, in NYSED's Mohawk Valley labor market region.
- **Density:** about 11 students per square mile.
- **Budget:** voters approved a $24,240,000 budget for 2026–27 on 19 May 2026, 228 to 52 (Fort Plain CSD).

## Fleet and transportation

Fort Plain owns its buses and replaces a few at a time through a separate proposition each May. Each one is paid from reserves and has no effect on the tax levy. The recent record:

| Vote | Proposition | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 18 May 2021 | School vehicles, up to $275,000, from the Bus Purchase Reserve Fund | Passed 125–16 |
| 21 May 2024 | Three school buses, up to $450,000 | Passed 225–79 |
| 20 May 2025 | Three buses or vehicles, up to $400,000 | Passed 127–32 |
| 19 May 2026 | Three gas/diesel vehicles, about $350,000 | Passed 217–60 |

The district's 2026 ballot notice says it is "eligible for roughly 90% reimbursement" of these purchases through state transportation aid. That aid ratio matters for the electric decision. The district pays only a small share of a bus's net cost, so the local premium for an electric bus is mostly a cash-flow question, not a tax question.

Three vehicles a year is the pace to plan around. From July 2032, each of those annual purchases must be zero-emission. Buses bought in 2026 will still be running when that rule starts.

## Depot power and the grid

Fort Plain sits in National Grid's service territory (New York State utility territory map). The buses are kept at the district's own bus garage. No public document yet sets out whether its electrical service can take chargers.

NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Fort Plain's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". That puts it with the majority of its region: in the Mohawk Valley, 33 of 54 districts (61%) were under way, 4 (7%) had a completed plan and 11 (20%) had made no contact with NYSERDA.

Neighbours show what the first step looks like. NYSERDA's NYSBIP data lists 10 vouchered buses in Montgomery County:

- one for neighbouring Canajoharie CSD, in a project now closed;
- nine at Student Transportation of America yards in Fonda and St. Johnsville, for Fonda-Fultonville, Johnstown and Little Falls.

For a district that buys three vehicles a year, the useful output of the FEP is simple. It should say how many chargers the garage can take on its current service, and when a service upgrade would be needed. The wider grid picture is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Fort Plain.
- **Local:** bus purchases come from reserve funds approved by voters each May, with about 90% repaid through state transportation aid in later years.

How NYSBIP vouchers stack with state aid is covered in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the FEP shows the bus garage needs a bigger National Grid service before it can charge several buses, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage so the first electric buses run while the upgrade is built. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
