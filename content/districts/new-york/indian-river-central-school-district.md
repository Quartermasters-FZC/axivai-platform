Indian River Central School District serves 3,308 students across 283 square miles next to Fort Drum. Its buses run with a contractor, Durham School Services. Voters approved a $70.5 million capital project in May 2025, 258 to 34, and its second phase includes the district's transportation facilities. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 3,308 students in 2024–25, against 3,399 in 2023–24 and 3,403 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 3% fall in two years.
- **Need:** 52% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 283.3 square miles, with schools in Philadelphia, Antwerp and Calcium, in NYSED's North Country labor market region.
- **Fort Drum:** the district describes a "special relationship reaching back many decades" with the Army post, and works with Fort Drum school liaison officers for military families.
- **Budget:** voters approved $107,220,000 for 2025–26 on 20 May 2025, 262 to 34, with no levy increase.

## Fleet and transportation

Indian River's transportation office works alongside Durham School Services. The district's transportation page sends parents with late-bus questions to both the district office and Durham, and Durham recruits the district's bus attendants.

Transportation is a large budget line:

- **$8,759,097** budgeted for transportation in 2025–26, up $561,700 from $8,197,397 the year before. The district listed "increased transportation costs" among the pressures on that budget.
- **A wide area.** The district runs several primary schools, an intermediate school, a middle school and a high school, at about 12 students per square mile.

## Depot power and the grid

Where the buses park and charge decides which utility matters. The district office campus on County Route 29 lies in National Grid's service territory. The Village of Philadelphia also runs its own municipal electric utility (Department of Public Service territory map). Buses kept at a Durham yard would draw from whichever utility serves that site.

The capital project gives the district a natural moment to plan for this:

- **Phase 2** covers the high school, middle school, intermediate school, transportation and athletics.
- **Design and NYSED approvals** run from June 2025 to April 2027, and **construction** from May 2026 to November 2029.
- **Scope** includes replacing electric and fire alarm systems and paving parking lots and sidewalks districtwide.

The public project materials do not mention chargers or conduit. Paving and electrical work at transportation sites are the cheapest points at which to lay empty conduit for future chargers.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists Indian River's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". Across the North Country's 65 districts, 4 (6%) had a completed plan and 23 (36%) had made no contact with NYSERDA. For a contracted district, the plan should model route energy and set out what the next transportation contract requires on yard power.

## Funding

Indian River's revenue mix is unusual. Its 2026–27 budgeted revenue of $110,280,000 breaks down as:

| Source | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| State aid | $77,350,000 |
| Federal Impact Aid | $15,760,000 |
| Fund balance | $9,908,314 |
| Other | $4,480,000 |
| Tax levy and STAR | $2,781,686 |

The local levy covers only about 2.5% of the budget. State and federal aid will shape how the district pays for electric buses as much as local votes will.

- **Capital project:** $70.5 million, with a 98% state building aid ratio, $7.5 million from the 2017 capital reserve, state EXCEL funds and debt service funds. The district said it carries no additional tax impact. A new capital reserve of up to $15 million passed 266 to 28 on the same day.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers under Indian River's name. In Jefferson County, Alexandria CSD holds four battery-electric bus vouchers and Jefferson-Lewis BOCES one. See our [NYSBIP review](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Indian River's electric buses will charge at whatever yard serves its routes, on a National Grid or municipal service. If that yard needs more capacity than its current service allows, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so electric buses run while the upgrade is completed.
