Elmira City School District is one of the few Southern Tier districts building charging capacity before it needs it. Its $139 million capital project, approved by voters on 10 December 2024, includes "electrical service upgrades at the Bus Garage to support EV charging". In January 2026 NYSERDA approved vouchers for its first two electric buses. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 5,065 students in 2024–25, against 5,054 in 2023–24 and 5,146 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 109.9 square miles, in NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about 46 students per square mile, a mix of short city runs and longer rural ones.
- **Priority status:** NYSERDA flags Elmira as a priority district in its NYSBIP data. Its NYSBIP record places the buses outside a disadvantaged community census tract.

## Fleet and transportation

Elmira's NYSBIP record lists no third-party operator. Where a contractor runs a district's buses, as in Fonda-Fultonville or Johnstown, the record names the bus company.

The two vouchered buses are:

- **Model:** IC Bus CE Series, 483.9-inch overall length, model year 2027;
- **Size:** Class 8 (over 33,000 lbs), the largest type of school bus;
- **Dealer:** Leonard Bus Sales;
- **Incentive:** $257,250 per bus, $514,500 in all;
- **Status:** approved 21 January 2026, not yet closed.

Full-size Class 8 buses carry larger batteries than the small Type A buses some districts start with, so each one adds more load to the garage's service when it charges overnight.

## Depot power and the grid

The capital project addresses the depot directly. Its environmental notice, published by DEC on 13 November 2024, lists for the Bus Garage:

- electrical service upgrades to support EV charging;
- a new vehicle exhaust system;
- bollards on site.

The project's design was to be finalised in January 2025, with completion set for 2030 (MyHometownToday).

The timing matters. If the garage service upgrade lands late in that 2025–2030 window, the first two buses could arrive before the garage has its full new capacity.

NYSED reports that Southern Tier districts get their electricity mainly from NYSEG. In the region, 13 districts expect to need service upgrades for an all-electric fleet, and 50 of 54 responding districts had no chargers in 2025–26.

One gap stands out. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Elmira's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) status as "No Contact", although the district answered the survey. The FEP models every route and sizes chargers for the whole fleet. For a district already buying electric buses and rebuilding its garage service, it is the check that the new service is big enough for the buses that follow. Across the Southern Tier, 29 of 63 districts (46%) had no FEP contact.

More on the upstate grid is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** $514,500 for two buses, approved 21 January 2026 (NYSERDA project data, updated 26 October 2026). The bus vouchers are separate from any charger funding.
- **Capital project:** $139 million in total. The district expects New York State to provide $119 million in aid, with the district covering the remaining $20 million (MyHometownToday, 11 December 2024). The bus garage's electrical upgrade is part of that total.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Elmira's electric buses arrive before the garage's new service is energized, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so the buses run on schedule while the upgrade is finished. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
