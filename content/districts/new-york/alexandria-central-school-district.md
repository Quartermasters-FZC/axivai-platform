Alexandria Central School District, in Alexandria Bay on the St. Lawrence River, runs one of the most electric school fleets in northern New York. Four of its 17 buses are zero-emission, according to NYSED's October 2025 report. That is 23.5%, the highest share of any North Country district in the report. Yet the buses voters approved in May 2026 are gasoline. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 446 students in 2024–25, against 448 in 2023–24 and 443 in 2022–23 (NYSED). The district projects 479 for 2026–27 (NYSED Property Tax Report Card).
- **Area:** 72.59 square miles, in NYSED's North Country labor market region.
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 budget of $17,947,495 on 19 May 2026, 369 to 138.

## Fleet and transportation

- **Electric buses:** four zero-emission buses in a fleet of 17, two more than the year before (NYSED, October 2025).
- **The buses:** NYSERDA's NYSBIP data lists four battery-electric Blue Bird Vision buses, model year 2023, sold through New York Bus Sales.
- **Replacement policy:** the district aims to keep every bus under six years old with less than 100,000 miles. That keeps each bus inside a six-year warranty and preserves resale value (budget newsletter, spring 2026).
- **2026 purchase:** on 19 May 2026 voters approved, 371 to 142, borrowing up to $350,000 for two full-size gasoline buses and one 10-passenger van. The district expects about 51% in state aid on the purchase.

The short replacement cycle has a consequence. Under the six-year policy, the gasoline buses bought in 2026 will be due for replacement around 2032, when every new purchase must be zero-emission. At that point Alexandria will need charging for more buses than its first four.

Alexandria is a regional reference point. The Adirondack North Country Association has described it as an early adopter "paving the way" for other districts. The district's transportation team brought an electric bus to ANCA's 2024 Clean Energy Conference in Clayton.

## Depot power and the grid

Across the North Country, 50 of the 55 districts that reported fleet data had no zero-emission buses. The report lists Alexandria alongside Brushton-Moira, Norwood-Norfolk, Carthage, Salmon River and Malone, each running one to four (NYSED). Alexandria has therefore already done what most neighbors have not: specified, bought and powered electric buses at its own garage.

Its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan is "Underway". In the North Country, 4 of 65 districts (6%) had a completed plan, 36 (55%) were underway and 23 (36%) had made no contact. Alexandria did not submit NYSED's 2025 survey.

Two questions for the next phase:

- **Capacity.** Today's garage service supports four electric buses. Moving toward 17 means more chargers, and possibly a larger service from the utility.
- **Winter.** In a North Country winter, plugged-in parking lets buses warm their batteries from the grid before morning runs.

May 2026's $7 million capital project, approved 362 to 147, covers the school building: entry, air conditioning, doors, fire alarm, roof and piping. It includes no transportation or charging work. A second, $8 million turf-field proposition failed, 78 to 435.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** four vouchers of $196,000 each, $784,000 in all, approved 21 May 2025. The project is closed. Alexandria carries NYSBIP's priority-district flag (NYSERDA data, updated 26 October 2026).
- **Local bonding:** the 2026 gasoline purchase was financed by a bond of up to $350,000, with about 51% state aid.

Program amounts are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Alexandria's next step, many more electric buses on one garage service, is a capacity question. If the utility upgrade lags behind the bus orders, AXIVAI brings battery power by truck into storage at the garage so new electric buses can run in the meantime.
