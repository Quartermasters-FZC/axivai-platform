Bethlehem Central was one of the first districts in New York to put electric school buses on the road. It now runs 10 large electric buses, more than any other Capital Region district reported to the state. In spring 2026 it stopped buying them. The district says its electric buses were out of service for an average of 66 days each during the school year, and the May 2026 ballot asked for nine diesel buses instead. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 4,075 students in 2024–25, against 4,092 in 2023–24 and 4,070 in 2022–23 (NYSED). Enrollment is flat.
- **Area:** 44.9 square miles in Albany County, centred on Delmar, in NYSED's Capital Region labor market region.
- **Budget:** voters approved a $119.8 million budget for 2026–27 on 19 May 2026, by 1,988 votes to 566.

## Fleet and transportation

Bethlehem owns and runs its own buses. Its 2024 ballot material says the fleet carries about 2,900 students to seven district schools and covers about 21,476 miles a week. NYSED's October 2025 report counted 104 buses, seven of them zero-emission (6.7%), the highest number in the Capital Region at the time.

The electric fleet was built in steps:

- **May 2021:** voters approved three bus propositions. One of them, electric bus infrastructure, passed 1,939 to 621. The purchase of up to five electric buses was contingent on NYSERDA grant funding.
- **2022:** NYSERDA's New York Truck Voucher Incentive Program provided $1 million toward five Thomas Built Jouley buses. Five 2009 diesel buses were retired to qualify. The buses entered service in September 2022, with "the first phase" of charging in place.
- **2024:** the 2024–25 proposition covered three 70-passenger electric buses and three small gasoline buses, with the electric buses contingent on grants. NYSERDA's NYSBIP records show three Saf-T-Liner C2 Jouley vouchers approved on 9 May 2024 and closed on 28 September 2025.
- **2026:** the district has an electric fleet of 10 large buses.

**The pause.** The district's 2026 ballot page says it is not adding electric buses in 2026–27 "due to ongoing performance and charge management issues impacting service miles". Its electric buses were out of service for an average of 66 days each during the school year. The district says it will resume buying when vehicle cost, charging efficiency and service costs improve. Voters approved nine 70-passenger diesel buses, at up to $1,633,239, by 1,832 to 716. State aid covers about 65% of the borrowing cost, leaving a maximum local cost of about $579,800.

## Depot power and the grid

Charging is part of the problem Bethlehem describes. An electric bus that cannot charge on schedule loses service miles even when the bus itself is sound. For a district at 10 buses, the questions are practical: how many chargers work on a given morning, whether the depot can charge every bus overnight, and what happens when a charger is down.

Scale makes the question larger. Replacing all 104 buses by 2040 means charging roughly ten times the current electric fleet at the depot. Bethlehem's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan was listed as "Underway" in October 2025. Across the Capital Region, only 2 of 74 districts had a completed plan, and 31 (42%) had made no contact with NYSERDA.

Downtime from early electric fleets is a pattern beyond Bethlehem. See [stranded electric buses](/research/stranded-electric-buses) and [school-bus depot power](/research/school-bus-depots).

## Funding

- **NYTVIP:** $1 million for the first five buses (2022).
- **NYSBIP:** three vouchers of $220,500 each, $661,500 in all. The project is closed. Bethlehem is not a priority district in the dataset.
- **Local:** bus purchases are bonded and repaid with about 65% state transportation aid.

Program rules are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Bethlehem's next question is reliable charging at scale, not whether electric buses can run. AXIVAI brings battery power by truck into storage on the yard, which can back up depot charging or add capacity while a permanent service upgrade is built.
