Binghamton holds the largest NYSBIP award in the Southern Tier: 10 electric school buses, $2,572,500 in vouchers, approved on 18 June 2025 with First Student as the operator. The buses are 2027 model-year IC Bus CE Series. At the same time, NYSED's October 2025 report listed the district as having made no contact with NYSERDA about a fleet plan. The electric transition is running through the contractor, not the district. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 4,382 students in 2024–25, against 4,363 in 2023–24 and 4,470 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 10.79 square miles, the smallest district by area in NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about 400 students per square mile, far denser than the rural districts around it. Routes are short.
- **Budget:** the 2025–26 budget put to voters on 20 May 2025 was $154,559,987, a 2.38% increase, with a 3.26% tax levy increase.

## Fleet and transportation

Binghamton contracts most of its pupil transportation. Its 2025–26 budget carries $4,235,135 for contract transportation, up from $4,108,736, and a further $154,130 for other district transportation. The district also keeps a small fleet of its own: six new vans for student transportation were bought with $300,000 set aside in the 2024–25 budget.

**The NYSBIP award.** NYSERDA's project data list one Binghamton application:

- **Buses:** 10 IC Bus CE Series, Class 7 (26,001–33,000 lbs), 2027 model year, battery electric;
- **Voucher:** $257,250 per bus, $2,572,500 in all;
- **Operator:** First Student, Inc.;
- **Status:** approved 18 June 2025, still open in the data updated 26 October 2026;
- **Priority district:** yes, in NYSERDA's dataset.

By dollar value, the award ties for sixth among all districts in the dataset, behind New York City, Syracuse, Buffalo, Albany and Gates-Chili. It is the largest award by value in the Southern Tier.

In Broome County, NYSED's 2025 report counted electric buses already running at neighbouring Johnson City (four) and Chenango Valley (one).

## Depot power and the grid

Ten buses will charge at the operator's yard, not at district property. That makes the yard's electrical service the practical constraint. Ten Class 7 buses charging overnight need a dedicated service, switchgear and chargers, and the utility work for that is usually the longest item in the schedule.

The planning gap is on the district side. NYSED lists Binghamton's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as "No Contact". Across the Southern Tier, 29 of 63 districts (46%) were in the same position in October 2025. For a contracted district, the plan that matters most models route energy and sets out what future transportation contracts should require on charging and yard power. See [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging) for options away from the main yard.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** $2,572,500 for 10 buses.
- **Contract:** about $4.2 million a year for contract transportation (2025–26).
- **Local fleet:** $300,000 for six vans, bought from the 2024–25 budget.

Program rules are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Binghamton's 10 vouchered buses depend on charging at the contractor's yard. If that yard's service upgrade runs behind the bus deliveries, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the buses can carry students while the utility work is completed.
