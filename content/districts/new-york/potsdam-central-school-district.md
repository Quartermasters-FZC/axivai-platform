Potsdam has a written rule for cold. When the wind chill or "real feel" falls below 0°F, the district sends out two extra buses to pick up middle and high school students who normally walk. Winter mornings like those are where an electric school bus loses the most range. Potsdam's fleet plan is already underway with NYSERDA. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,183 students in 2024–25, against 1,223 in 2023–24 and 1,210 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 95.53 square miles, in NYSED's North Country labor market region.
- **Density:** about 12 students per square mile. The district serves the village of Potsdam, home to SUNY Potsdam, and the rural area around it.
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 budget of $45,144,373 on 19 May 2026, 309 to 42 (88%). It raises spending 4.43%.

## Fleet and transportation

Potsdam owns and runs its buses, with district drivers and monitors.

- **2026 purchase:** voters approved two diesel buses and one gas van, at up to $438,897 less trade-in value, by 317 to 33 (91%).
- **Transportation budget:** $1,741,474 for district transportation in 2026–27, up $98,229 from $1,643,245. The district rents no buses.
- **Eligibility:** every student in pre-K to grade 4 rides. Grades 5–8 ride from 1.25 miles, grades 9–12 from 1.5 miles.
- **Cold-weather walker runs:** below 0°F wind chill, one bus covers Evergreen Park and the streets off Market Street, and a second covers Debra Drive, Lawrence Avenue and the streets toward SUNY Potsdam. First pickups are at 6:45 a.m.
- **Routing:** the district plans routes with Tyler Technologies software and shares stops and times through the My Ride K-12 app.

The 2026 purchase is still diesel and gasoline. Buses bought now will run well into the 2030s, so the first electric purchases will join a mostly diesel fleet.

## Depot power and the grid

Potsdam sits in National Grid's territory (NYS electric utility territory map). The village is not among New York's municipal electric systems, unlike Massena, elsewhere in St. Lawrence County.

The North Country is the hardest test of battery range in the state. Cold reduces usable battery capacity and adds heating load. Two numbers from the fleet side point to what the plan should check:

- **Early starts.** Walker runs begin at 6:45 a.m. on the coldest days. Buses that charge indoors and pre-heat on grid power start those runs with a full, warm battery.
- **A plan in progress.** NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Potsdam's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as **underway**. Across the North Country, 36 of 65 districts (55%) were underway and four (6%) had finished. Another 23 (36%) had made no contact.

The district's most recent capital work was a $1.5 million project approved 182 to 25. It renovated the high school art room, middle school restrooms and building controls. It did not include the bus garage. Electrical work for chargers would need its own project or a future capital vote.

The wider grid picture upstate is covered in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Bus propositions:** Potsdam funds buses through annual voter propositions, with state transportation aid.
- **Capital reserve:** the $1.5 million project was funded with $300,000 from the capital reserve and other reserve funds over 15 years, with no tax impact.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Potsdam.

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Potsdam's fleet plan shows the bus garage needs a National Grid service upgrade, AXIVAI can bring battery power to the garage by truck so the first electric buses can run, and charge indoors through the winter, while the upgrade is built. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
