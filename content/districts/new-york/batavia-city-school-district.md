Batavia City School District serves 2,075 students in 25.65 square miles, about 81 per square mile. The seven other Genesee County districts in NYSED's report range from about 8 to 24. Short, dense routes are the easiest kind to electrify, and Batavia's NYSERDA fleet plan is underway. Four of its Genesee neighbours have already finished theirs. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 2,075 students in 2024–25, against 2,092 in 2023–24 and 2,057 in 2022–23 (NYSED). Enrollment has held steady.
- **Area:** 25.65 square miles, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Density:** about 81 students per square mile, against 23.6 in Le Roy and under 13 in every other Genesee County district (NYSED). Routes are short, well within a single charge.
- **Budget:** $66,143,875 proposed for 2026–27, up 4.48% from $63,310,108 in 2025–26. The tax levy rises 2% to $21,206,687 (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

Batavia's route profile is short and dense. That matters because the main limits on electric school buses are winter range and charging time. A district where most runs are a few miles long can usually charge once overnight and run the day, even in a Genesee County winter.

Electric buses are already working nearby. NYSED's October 2025 report lists six Finger Lakes districts running zero-emission buses. One is Le Roy CSD, Batavia's neighbour in Genesee County, which runs one electric bus out of a fleet of 34 and has one more on order. Gates-Chili leads the region with 15 electric buses out of 88.

Across the region, 46 districts (68%) own and operate all their buses, 7 (10%) contract out all transportation and 15 (22%) use a mix. Denser districts are the ones most likely to contract out.

## Depot power and the grid

National Grid is the investor-owned electric utility in Genesee County. The Village of Bergen runs the county's only municipal system (NYS Department of Public Service). Batavia's buses will charge on National Grid's network.

Batavia's Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) is listed as **underway**. The FEP is NYSERDA's free study of chargers, service upgrades and route energy. In the Finger Lakes:

- 16 districts (23%) had a completed plan and 42 (60%) had one underway;
- in Genesee County, Byron-Bergen, Oakfield-Alabama, Pavilion and Pembroke had completed plans, while Batavia, Alexander, Elba and Le Roy were underway;
- 41 of 68 responding districts (60%) had completed a utility needs assessment or held a load letter from their utility;
- 27 districts expect to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet, and 7 expect to need a larger bus depot.

Districts in the region ranked utility upgrades, lack of outside funding and lack of voter support as their hardest problems. For the upstate grid picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap). For what a slow utility connection means for a depot, see [grid connection wait times for EV charging](/research/ev-charging-grid-connection-wait-times).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Batavia.
- **Reserves:** Batavia's 2026–27 schedule of reserves shows about $3.71 million in a capital reserve, with no intended use listed for the year. Reserves of that kind can fund buses or depot work if voters approve.
- **Regional context:** NYSED reports that 21 Finger Lakes districts (31%) won state or local zero-emission funding, averaging about $272,682 per award.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Batavia's FEP finds that the depot needs a National Grid service upgrade before charging can start, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses run in the meantime. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
