Delaware Academy is already rebuilding parts of its bus garage. The scope of its 2023 Capital Improvement Project lists "Electrical Service/Panelboards" for the transportation garage, along with a new boiler, a bus lift and lighting. What the district has not yet done is ask NYSERDA how much power an electric fleet would need. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 706 students in 2024–25, down from 728 in 2023–24 and 720 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 192.1 square miles, the second-largest of the 63 districts in NYSED's Southern Tier region, after Corning.
- **Density:** under four students per square mile. Routes are long and riders are few.
- **Approvals:** the 2023 capital project needed authorization from New York City's Department of Environmental Protection as well as NYSED, NYS DEC, NYS Parks and the Town of Delhi.

## Fleet and transportation

The district runs its own buses and maintains them in its own transportation garage. Its 2023 capital project planning (April and August 2023 community forums) listed this work at the garage:

- paving and parking lot;
- new windows and interior and exterior doors;
- boiler and HVAC replacement;
- **electrical service and panelboards;**
- hazardous materials abatement;
- a lift and new lighting;
- office and breakroom renovation.

The project was capped at $14 million with no added tax, funded with NYS building aid (73.3% aid ratio) and reserves. Voters approved the main project in October 2023; a second, more expensive proposition failed that month by 16 votes. The Daily Star reported in April 2024 that the approved project would "add a new bus lift in the transportation garage." A separate $3.1 million athletic fields project passed 479 to 229 on 16 April 2024.

Electric buses are not yet part of the fleet. NYSED's October 2025 report names the only four Southern Tier districts running zero-emission buses: Ithaca, Johnson City, Chenango Valley and Downsville. Downsville, also in Delaware County, runs one electric bus in a 14-bus fleet.

## Depot power and the grid

Two electric utilities operate around Delhi:

- **NYSEG**, the investor-owned utility the Public Service Commission lists for Delaware County;
- **Delaware County Electric Cooperative (DCEC)**, a member-owned cooperative headquartered on North Depot Street in Delhi. Founded in 1942, it serves more than 5,200 members in Delaware, Schoharie, Otsego and Chenango counties.

Which one serves the garage determines who must approve new load, and on what schedule.

The public project materials do not mention chargers or spare capacity in the new garage service. Whether it has room for chargers is unknown, because the district has no fleet plan. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Delaware Academy as having made **no contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP). Across the Southern Tier, 29 of 63 districts (46%) were in the same position. In the same region, 13 districts expect to need service upgrades for a fully electric fleet, and 38 of the 54 responding districts have a utility needs assessment or load letter.

Two local factors will shape the plan:

- **Distance.** At 192 square miles, some runs may need a midday top-up in winter. The FEP should test each run against cold-weather range.
- **The new service.** A load letter from the utility shows whether the garage's service can take the first chargers or must be upsized again.

Upstate grid timing is covered in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Delaware Academy. In Delaware County, Sidney (2 buses, approved February 2025) and Margaretville (1 bus, project closed) hold vouchers.
- **Capital:** the 2023 project relies on building aid at a 73.3% ratio. Garage electrical work done in a future aided project would be funded the same way.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If an FEP finds the garage's new service too small for a charging fleet, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage so electric buses run while the utility upgrade is completed. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
