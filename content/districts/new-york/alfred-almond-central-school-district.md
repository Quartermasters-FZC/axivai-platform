Alfred-Almond Central School District serves fewer than 500 students across 95 square miles of Allegany County. In October 2025 it was one of 13 Western New York districts that had made no contact with NYSERDA about a fleet electrification plan. Enrollment is falling fast, and its 2025–26 ballot authorized a single bus. Both facts shape how an electric transition will be paid for. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 476 students in 2024–25, down from 492 in 2023–24 and 533 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is an 11% fall in two years. The district projects 474 for 2026–27 (NYSED Property Tax Report Card).
- **Area:** 95.36 square miles, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region. That is about five students per square mile, so routes are long with few riders per bus.
- **Budget:** 2025–26 spending of $17,810,031. Proposed 2026–27 spending is $17,520,185, a cut of 1.63% (NYSED Property Tax Report Card).

## Fleet and transportation

Alfred-Almond owns its buses and finances them through separate propositions. Its 2025–26 budget notice listed:

- **One bus:** authorization to borrow $180,000 for the purchase of one bus, with no increase to the tax levy.
- **Capital project:** authorization to borrow $24,000,000 for a capital improvement project covering district buildings, facilities and sites, also with no levy increase.

A $180,000 single-bus authorization is priced for a diesel or gasoline bus. The most common NYSBIP voucher for a battery-electric Class 7 bus is $257,250, given to 116 buses statewide. The voucher alone is larger than the whole authorization, and it covers only part of an electric bus's price.

If purchases stay at one bus a year, the district will add electric buses one at a time from July 2032, and each will need a charger at the garage.

## Depot power and the grid

NYSED's October 2025 report lists Alfred-Almond's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as "No Contact". Western New York is further along than most regions: 18 of 80 districts (22%) had finished plans and 43 (54%) were underway. Alfred-Almond was among the 13 (16%) that had not started.

The fleet plan is free. For a small rural district it answers three questions before money is spent:

- **Which runs work on one charge.** At five students per square mile, some routes are long. Winter range in the Allegany County hills sets how many runs a bus can do.
- **What the garage service can carry.** One or two chargers may fit on the existing service. A fleet's worth may not.
- **Whether the $24 million capital project helps.** If garage electrical work is ever scoped, conduit for future chargers costs far less when trenches are already open.

The upstate grid picture is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Alfred-Almond.
- **Local:** single-bus borrowing propositions ($180,000 in the 2025–26 notice) and the $24 million capital authorization.
- **Budget pressure:** spending is set to fall 1.63% in 2026–27 as enrollment drops, which limits room for extra electric-bus costs.

## Where AXIVAI fits

A small rural garage may not have the electrical service for more than a couple of chargers. If Alfred-Almond's first electric buses arrive before a service upgrade, AXIVAI brings battery power by truck into storage at the garage so they can run in the meantime.
