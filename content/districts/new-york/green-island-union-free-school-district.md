Green Island covers less than one square mile, and it is one of the few districts in New York that does not buy electricity from an investor-owned utility. The Village of Green Island is served by the Green Island Power Authority (GIPA), a municipal system with a long-term supply contract with the New York Power Authority. The district owns one bus and contracts most of its transportation. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 211 students in 2024–25, down from 235 in 2023–24 and 250 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 16% fall in two years.
- **Need:** 63% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 0.87 square miles, in NYSED's Capital Region labor market region.
- **High school:** since September 2025, students in grades 9–12 attend Watervliet High School under a tuition agreement approved on 4 February 2025, 135 to 28. The district pays about $400,000 a year for about 55 students over five years.
- **Budget:** voters approved a $9,012,604 budget for 2026–27 on 19 May 2026, 49 to 10, with three new capital reserves (technology, furniture, equipment).

## Fleet and transportation

Green Island's transportation is small and mostly bought in:

- **One district bus.** Voters approved a bus proposition on 16 May 2023, 64 to 19. The bus arrived in May 2024. The district said it "will help us lower costs", because it otherwise contracts all transportation.
- **Watervliet runs.** District-provided buses carry grades 9–12 from five stops on Arch, George and Center streets to Watervliet each morning (August 2025 notice).
- **Contract lines in the 2026–27 budget:** $75,000 for contract transportation, $450,000 for special education transportation, $7,000 for field trips and $3,500 for public transportation.

Special education transportation costs six times the regular contract line. Because contractors run those routes, their electrification depends on what the district writes into future contracts.

## Depot power and the grid

**A municipal utility.** Green Island's school sits inside GIPA's territory (New York State utility territory map). GIPA describes itself as "one of only three public power authorities" in the state. It was created by an act of the State Legislature in 1986, and it has bought low-cost power under a long-term agreement with the New York Power Authority since 1978. Its mission statement commits it to reliable service at the lowest reasonable cost to village customers.

That changes the charging question. A district bus charger would connect to GIPA's system, not to National Grid's, and would be billed at GIPA's municipal rates. Any service upgrade at the school would be planned and paid for through GIPA, so it is the authority, not a large utility, that the district's fleet plan needs to consult.

**The building.** Voters approved a $7.7 million capital project on 16 December 2025, 59 to 2, with zero tax impact. It covers roof and rooftop unit replacement, air conditioning, heating, sprinklers, foundation work, fire alarm and public address upgrades, and relocating district offices. It does not include bus charging. Construction planning began in January 2026.

**The plan.** NYSED's October 2025 report lists Green Island as having made **no contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan. In the Capital Region, 31 of 74 districts (42%) were in the same position.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Green Island. Elsewhere in Albany County, Albany, Bethlehem, Guilderland, North Colonie, South Colonie and Voorheesville have vouchered buses.
- **Local:** the one district bus came through a stand-alone voter proposition. A new equipment reserve, approved in May 2026, can hold up to $1 million for future voter-approved purchases.

How NYSBIP vouchers work is covered in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If a charger for Green Island's one or two buses has to wait on a GIPA service upgrade, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on site so an electric bus can run meanwhile. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
