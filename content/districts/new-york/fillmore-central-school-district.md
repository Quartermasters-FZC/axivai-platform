Fillmore Central School District has already done the planning step most rural districts have not. NYSED's October 2025 report lists its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as "Complete". The district still buys conventional buses on short bonds, and NYSERDA lists no electric bus vouchers for it. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 540 students in 2024–25, down from 559 in 2023–24 and 589 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is an 8% fall in two years. The district projects 549 for 2026–27.
- **Area:** 106.9 square miles in Allegany County, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region.
- **Density:** about five students per square mile. Each bus covers a lot of road for few riders.
- **Budget:** $20,634,842 for 2025–26, rising to $22,485,740 proposed for 2026–27 (+8.97%). The 2026–27 tax levy is $2,732,675 (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

Fillmore runs its own buses from a district bus garage. Its 2025–26 budget sets out what that costs:

| Line (2025–26 budget) | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| District-operated transportation | $1,104,690 |
| Bus garage operation | $69,900 |
| Bus garage electricity | $20,000 |
| Gasoline and diesel fuel | $89,600 |
| Bus loan payments (principal and interest) | $177,250 |
| Total pupil transportation | $1,174,590 |

State transportation aid was estimated at $1,113,798 for the same year, close to the total transportation spend.

The district replaces vehicles in small batches, financed with five-year bonds approved by the Board of Education:

- **18 June 2025:** one 2024 Blue Bird Vision 30-passenger bus and two 2024 Chevrolet Suburbans, at no more than $219,439.17.
- **20 May 2026:** two 2026 Blue Bird Vision 65-passenger buses, at no more than $342,130.

Neither notice describes the buses as electric. Both bonds carry a five-year period of probable usefulness, so the district is buying in small, regular steps. Each future step is a chance to add an electric bus without a large one-off vote.

The district also holds a reserve for transportation vehicles: $620,000 in March 2025 and $520,000 on the 2026–27 schedule of reserves (NYSED).

## Depot power and the grid

The district's campus at 104 West Main Street sits in Rochester Gas and Electric (RG&E) territory, according to the state's electric utility service territory data. Today the bus garage uses about $20,000 of electricity a year. Overnight charging for even a few full-size electric buses would add a load of a different order, so the service size at the garage is the key question.

Fillmore's completed fleet plan should already estimate that load. NYSED reports that across Western New York:

- 33 districts expect to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet;
- 48 of 78 responding districts had a utility needs assessment or a load letter;
- districts rate utility upgrades among their hardest problems, alongside voter support and funding.

Fillmore did not submit the 2025 NYSED survey, so its own answers are not in the report. Nearby Allegany County districts are moving at different speeds. Belfast, Bolivar-Richburg and Scio also have completed plans, and Canaseraga has made no contact with NYSERDA.

Rural range matters here too. Routes across 107 square miles of hill country in a western New York winter need to be checked against cold-weather battery range before buses are ordered. The wider grid picture is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Fillmore.
- **Local:** a $520,000 vehicle reserve, plus five-year bus bonds.
- **State aid:** transportation aid of about $1.1 million a year (2025–26 estimate) offsets bus purchases and operations.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Fillmore's plan shows the RG&E service at its garage needs an upgrade, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses run before that work is finished. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
