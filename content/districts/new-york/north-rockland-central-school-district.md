North Rockland serves 7,921 students in northern Rockland County, and enrollment is rising. Its formal name is the Haverstraw-Stony Point Central School District. On electric buses it has not yet started: NYSED's October 2025 report lists no contact with NYSERDA about a fleet plan, and NYSERDA's voucher data shows no electric school bus anywhere in Rockland County. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 7,921 students in 2024–25, up from 7,700 in 2023–24 and 7,703 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 3% rise in one year.
- **Need:** 64% of students (5,073) are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 61.22 square miles in the Haverstraw and Stony Point area, in NYSED's Hudson Valley labor market region.
- **Density:** about 129 students per square mile, far denser than rural upstate districts.
- **Budget:** voters approved the 2026–27 budget on 19 May 2026, 1,096 to 340, with no other propositions (NYSED).

## Fleet and transportation

The size of the task is set by enrollment. Carrying close to 8,000 students to school takes a large fleet, whoever owns it. NYSED notes that in the Hudson Valley most districts contract out: 49 of 83 responding districts (59%) contract all transportation, 21 (25%) own and operate all their buses, and 13 (16%) use a mix.

For North Rockland the ownership question decides who has to act first:

- **If the district owns buses,** its bus propositions, garage and electrical service are the place to start.
- **If a contractor runs routes,** the buses will charge at the contractor's yard, and the district's lever is the contract. Contract terms that require zero-emission buses, and say who pays for chargers, need to be in place well before 2032.

Either way, enrollment growth adds routes. Every new route added before 2032 can still be diesel; every one after must be zero-emission.

## Depot power and the grid

North Rockland is served by Orange & Rockland Utilities (O&R), a Consolidated Edison subsidiary (New York State utility territory map). O&R is one of five main utilities serving Hudson Valley districts, according to NYSED.

Three facts frame the depot question:

- **No fleet plan.** NYSED lists North Rockland's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) status as "No Contact". In the Hudson Valley, 56 of 101 districts (56%) were in the same position, and only 5 had completed plans.
- **Utility assessments lag here.** Only 32 of 83 responding Hudson Valley districts (39%) had completed a utility needs assessment or held a load letter, against 71% in the Capital Region and 62% in Western New York.
- **No local precedent.** NYSERDA's NYSBIP data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchered bus in Rockland County.

At this enrollment, charging will be a fleet-scale load, not a single charger. An FEP is the step that turns route data into a load request O&R can study. For yards that cannot get enough power in time, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** no vouchers listed for North Rockland or for any operator in Rockland County.
- **Local:** voters approved a Facilities Capital Reserve Fund in May 2025, 724 to 144 (NYSED). Its use is set by later votes.

North Rockland's high share of economically disadvantaged students is relevant to NYSBIP priority treatment. The [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program) explains how priority status changes voucher amounts.

## Where AXIVAI fits

When North Rockland's buses start to electrify, the yard that charges them, district or contractor, will need O&R capacity that may take years to deliver; AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on that yard so buses run while the connection is built. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
