William Floyd runs one of the largest contracted school-bus operations on Long Island: 106 large buses and 105 small buses, driving more than 5,850 miles every school day. Transportation now costs the district $31.9 million a year, double what it did a decade ago. No electric buses are funded yet, and the district has not started a fleet plan with NYSERDA. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 9,382 students in 2024–25, up from 9,208 in 2023–24 and 9,008 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a rise of about 4% in two years, against falling enrollment in much of the state.
- **Area:** 11.45 square miles in the Mastics, Moriches and Shirley, in NYSED's Long Island labor market region.
- **Density:** about 820 students per square mile.
- **Budget:** the proposed 2026–27 budget is $332,475,894, up 1.99%. Transportation is $31,877,000 of it, against $30,012,750 in 2025–26 and $15,702,076 in 2015–16.

## Fleet and transportation

The district owns no buses. Its transportation page lists two contractors, First Student and Durham School Services. The 2026–27 budget book sets out the scale:

- **Buses:** 106 large and 105 small, "more than 200 buses and monitors" for school, clubs, sports and homeless students.
- **Distance:** over 5,850 miles driven daily.
- **Destinations:** more than 60 schools. Special education placements and homeless students' locations can be up to 50 miles away.
- **Contracts:** 2026–27 is year four of a five-year large-bus contract and year two of a five-year small-bus contract. On that schedule the large-bus contract runs through 2027–28 and the small-bus contract through 2029–30.

Those two end dates are the district's real electrification milestones. Each new contract can set what share of buses must be electric and who provides charging at the yard.

**A district that has had to replace a contractor at short notice.** In July 2018 the then-contractor, East End Bus Lines, asked for an extra $16.5 million over the last three years of its five-year contract. The district refused and rebid the work. Large buses did not run on the first days of the 2018–19 school year while drivers and the new contractor failed to agree. The board approved an emergency agreement with First Student, which committed to full large-bus service by 17 September 2018 (Patch; News 12). That history is a reason to write yard power and charger ownership into contracts carefully. Chargers bolted into one contractor's yard do not move with the route if the contract changes hands.

## Depot power and the grid

William Floyd's buses park at contractor yards, not on district land. On Long Island the grid belongs to the Long Island Power Authority (LIPA), which serves about 1.2 million customers. PSEG Long Island runs it under a contract extended through 31 December 2030. NYSED's October 2025 report names PSEG Long Island as the region's one major utility.

The planning gap is wide:

- **No fleet plan.** NYSED's report lists William Floyd as having **not submitted** its survey and as having made **no contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan. Across Long Island, 97 of 124 districts (78%) were in the same position.
- **Scale.** At 5,850 miles a day, the fleet averages under 30 miles per bus, well within an electric bus's range. The constraint is not range but power: charging around 200 buses overnight needs yard service measured in megawatts, at sites the district does not control.
- **Long runs.** The 50-mile special education and homeless runs are the ones a fleet plan should model first against winter range.

The district's $78.5 million capital project, approved by voters on 26 March 2025 by 618 to 105, covers repairs and upgrades in every building. Its published scope does not include bus charging. For options away from contractor yards, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers under William Floyd's name. Background on the program is in our [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).
- **Federal:** the EPA's 31 May 2024 list of Clean School Bus rebates in New York does not include William Floyd.
- **State aid:** the district budgets $21.0 million in transportation aid for 2026–27, up from $19.6 million. Aid is paid the year after the spending, so contract costs for electric buses would be partly reimbursed through the same route.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

William Floyd's next bus contracts will need yard power for a fleet of more than 200 buses on the PSEG Long Island grid. Where a contractor yard's upgrade lags the first electric buses, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so buses run while the utility work is completed.
