York Central School District is small, but it is ahead of most of New York on electric buses. It already runs one, and its NYSERDA fleet electrification plan is finished. Only 16 of the 70 districts in the Finger Lakes region had reached that point by October 2025. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 618 students in 2024–25, down from 640 in 2023–24 and 650 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 5% fall in two years. The district projects 592 for 2026–27 (NYSED property tax report card).
- **Area:** 67.67 square miles in Livingston County, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Density:** about nine students per square mile. Few riders per bus, and runs that cover open country.
- **One campus:** the elementary school and the middle/high school share a single address, 2578 Genesee Street in Retsof (NCES). Every route starts and ends at the same site.
- **Budget:** the district proposed $20,989,316 in spending for 2026–27, up 1.95% from $20,587,581, with a 2% tax levy increase (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

York owns and runs its own buses. In the NYSED survey behind the October 2025 progress report, the district reported:

- **Fleet:** 23 buses.
- **Zero-emission buses:** one, about 4% of the fleet.

That puts York among only six Finger Lakes districts with any zero-emission bus in service. Fifty-five of the 60 districts that reported fleet data had none. The others were Gates-Chili, Penfield, Naples, Le Roy and Livonia.

York's electric bus does not appear in NYSERDA's NYSBIP project data (updated 26 October 2026), so it was funded outside that voucher program.

A 23-bus fleet based on one campus makes charging simpler to plan than at districts with scattered lots. All the chargers can go in one place, on one utility service.

## Depot power and the grid

The Retsof campus sits in National Grid territory, according to the state's electric utility service territory map. Parts of Livingston County are served by RG&E, so the campus location, not the county, decides the utility. National Grid will set the cost and timing of any service upgrade for more chargers.

York's completed FEP is the useful starting point. The plan sizes chargers and the electrical service the fleet needs over time. With it done, the district can go to National Grid with a defined load rather than an estimate.

The regional picture shows why that matters. In the Finger Lakes region, 27 districts expect to need an electric service upgrade for a fully electric fleet, seven expect to expand their depot, and three expect to need a new one (NYSED, October 2025). The wider upstate grid situation is covered in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

**Capital planning.** York's reserve schedule filed with NYSED for 2026–27 shows its capital reserve at zero, with the note "Build reserve for 2026 CIP if possible." A capital improvement project is in view. If charger wiring or a service upgrade is part of it, it can be paid for through the capital project rather than the operating budget.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** no vouchers under York's name in NYSERDA's data, updated 26 October 2026.
- **Reserves:** the capital reserve stood at $0 on 30 June 2025, according to the district's NYSED reserve schedule.
- **Local:** as a district-operated fleet, York finances buses through voter-approved purchases, state transportation aid and state incentives.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If York's next electric buses need more power than the Retsof campus service can supply before National Grid finishes an upgrade, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the buses run in the meantime. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
