Greenwich voters said yes to everything on the May 2026 ballot: the budget, a transportation proposition and two capital project propositions. The district buys its own buses every year and its fleet plan with NYSERDA is under way. No bus anywhere in Washington County holds a NYSBIP voucher yet, so a voucher for Greenwich would be the county's first. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 823 students in 2024–25, against 825 in 2023–24 and 855 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Need:** 39% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 95.61 square miles, in NYSED's Capital Region labor market region. Its May ballot carries library propositions for both Greenwich and Easton, two of the communities it serves.
- **Density:** under nine students per square mile.
- **Address:** the district is based at 10 Gray Avenue in the village of Greenwich.

## Fleet and transportation

Greenwich owns its buses. It renews the fleet through a separate vehicle proposition each May, and every one of the last three has passed (NYSED budget vote results):

| Vote | Proposition | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| May 2024 | Equipment | Passed 349–142 |
| May 2025 | Two school buses | Passed 299–137 |
| May 2026 | Transportation proposition | Passed 308–133 |

That steady approval is the base for an electric transition. Under the state law, any bus Greenwich buys from 1 July 2032 must be zero-emission, so five or six more annual rounds of diesel purchases remain before the switch. Each diesel bus bought now will still be in service in the mid-2030s, and must be retired by 1 July 2040.

## Depot power and the grid

The district's Gray Avenue site is in National Grid's territory (New York State utility territory map). The first question for chargers is whether the bus garage's existing service has room, or whether it needs a new service from National Grid.

**Capital work.** On 19 May 2026 voters approved two capital project propositions, 299 to 143 and 278 to 162 (NYSED). The public vote record does not list their contents. If either project touches the bus garage or the campus electrical service, it is the cheapest point to add conduit and panel space for future chargers.

**The plan.** NYSED's October 2025 report lists Greenwich's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". That puts it ahead of much of its region. In the Capital Region, only 2 of 74 districts (3%) had a completed plan, 38 (51%) were under way, and 31 (42%) had made no contact with NYSERDA. The report also records that 46 of 65 responding Capital Region districts had completed a utility needs assessment or had a load letter.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Greenwich, and none for any district in Washington County.
- **Local:** bus purchases are approved separately each May. The 2026–27 budget passed 299 to 142.

How NYSBIP vouchers combine with state transportation aid, and what they cover for chargers, is set out in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the FEP finds the bus garage needs a new National Grid service before its buses can charge, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses run while the service is built. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
