Cobleskill-Richmondville Central School District is wiring its high school for electric bus charging, and its supplier is not a big investor-owned utility. It is Richmondville Power & Light, the village-owned utility founded in 1918. The village is setting a 1,000 kVA transformer at the high school "for the bus chargers", expected in late November or early December 2026. The project shows how transformer supply, not just money, sets the pace of depot work. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,479 students in 2024–25, down from 1,500 in 2023–24 and 1,511 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 181.2 square miles, one of the larger districts in NYSED's Mohawk Valley labor market region.
- **Density:** about eight students per square mile, so runs are long and riders per bus are few.

## Fleet and transportation

The district owns and runs its buses. NYSDOT recorded 86 safety inspections of its vehicles in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026, two of which failed and needed re-inspection. Buses are inspected twice a year, so that points to a fleet of about 40 to 43 buses and other vehicles.

Across 181 square miles, some winter runs will need more than one charge a day or a larger battery. Each run should be modelled against cold-weather range, not rated range, before buses are ordered.

## Depot power and the grid

The district sits across two electric systems. The village of Cobleskill is in National Grid's territory (formerly Niagara Mohawk), according to the state's utility territory data. The high school and the Radez school are on Richmondville Power & Light (RPL), a municipal utility that distributes low-cost NYPA hydropower and has not changed its electric rates since 2011.

RPL's monthly reports to the village board track the school's electrical work through 2026:

- **March 2026:** RPL is coordinating a new service for Radez and "a planned line and service to the High School for bus chargers".
- **April 2026:** the school plans "a few EV Chargers" at the high school. Radez is moving from an 850-amp to a 2,000-amp service with an underground transformer.
- **May 2026:** the school opts for a reconditioned 750 kVA transformer for Radez, with no spare.
- **July 2026:** a spare transformer stored since the high school was built, which the school wanted for the chargers, fails an oil test with high acetylene levels. Expedited reconditioning is quoted at about $13,000.
- **September 2026:** the Radez transformer is in service. RPL is waiting for the 1,000 kVA unit for the high school "for the bus chargers".
- **October 2026:** the high school transformer is expected in late November or early December. The underground connections at the pole were finished before the cold set in.

The reports also show why transformers are the bottleneck. RPL's superintendent wrote in March 2026 that after Covid, transformer prices "skyrocketed" and wait times ran over a year, so the utility stopped ordering stock for several years. A small municipal utility has few spares. One failed unit can push a charging project back by months.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists the district's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway".

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Cobleskill-Richmondville. See our [NYSBIP review](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program) for how the program has been used.
- **Local:** the district buys its own buses, so electric buses will come through voter-approved bus propositions, NYSBIP vouchers and state transportation aid.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Cobleskill-Richmondville's charging start depends on one transformer arriving. When a transformer or service upgrade runs late, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on site, so electric buses can charge until the permanent service is live. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
