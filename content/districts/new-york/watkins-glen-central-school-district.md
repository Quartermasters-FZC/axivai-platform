Watkins Glen Central School District has already asked voters once to prepare its bus garage for electric buses. In December 2024 they said no, 430 to 174. The $35.8 million "SOAR '29" project bundled garage work, including preparation for future EV charging stations, with stadium lights, cafeteria renovations and much else. The garage's readiness went down with the rest. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 842 students in 2024–25, against 846 in 2023–24 and 853 in 2022–23 (NYSED). The district is small and roughly stable.
- **Area:** 141.7 square miles in Schuyler County and NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about six students per square mile, so routes run long with few riders per bus.
- **Budget:** voters approved a $31.4 million budget for 2025–26 in May 2025, together with a diesel bus lease plan (FingerLakes1).

## Fleet and transportation

Watkins Glen runs its own transportation from a district bus garage and storage building. It acquires buses through leases that voters approve, and the plan approved in May 2025 was for diesel buses.

Leasing changes how a switch to electric works:

- **Turnover is fast.** A leased fleet turns over on the lease term, not on the much longer life of an owned bus. Each new lease is a chance to bring in electric buses, but only if chargers are ready when the buses arrive.
- **The garage is the constraint.** A leased electric bus still needs a charger in the district's garage every night. The charger, wiring and service upgrade are the district's to fund, whoever owns the bus.

## Depot power and the grid

The SOAR '29 plan was presented in August 2024 and voted on 17 December 2024. It listed infrastructure and safety work at the bus garage and storage building:

- masonry restoration;
- fire alarm improvements;
- preparation for possible future EV charging stations.

The project failed, so none of that preparation was funded. After the vote the superintendent said that "any proposal after this would be smaller".

Planning with NYSERDA has barely started. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists the district's Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Initial Contact Made". Across the Southern Tier, only five of 63 districts had a completed plan, and 29 had made no contact. NYSED also reports that districts in the region get their power mainly from NYSEG. An FEP and an early load letter from the utility would show whether the garage's existing service can carry the first few chargers or needs an upgrade before any electric lease.

For the wider upstate grid picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Capital:** SOAR '29 would have cost $35.8 million. The district said state aid would cover 65%, with part of the rest from its capital reserve, leaving a 4.08% tax increase. Voters rejected it, 430 to 174.
- **Bus leases:** voters approved diesel bus leases in May 2025.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Watkins Glen.

A smaller follow-up project could fund only the garage's electrical work. That would cost far less than the full SOAR '29 package. Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Watkins Glen's garage lost its EV-charging preparation with SOAR '29, so its first electric leases may arrive before the garage has the power to charge them. AXIVAI can bring battery power to the yard by truck, so the buses run while a smaller garage project and any utility upgrade move forward. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
