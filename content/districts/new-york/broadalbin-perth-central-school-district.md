Broadalbin-Perth is investing in its bus yard, but for fuel, not electricity. Its $37.8 million capital project, now under construction, includes $937,100 to replace and enlarge the diesel and gas tanks at the district's transportation center. In May 2026 voters also approved three more diesel buses. Both decisions set the starting point for electric buses here: a well-maintained diesel depot with no published charging plan. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,563 students in 2024–25, down from 1,597 in 2023–24 and 1,603 in 2022–23 (NYSED). The district projects 1,624 for 2026–27 in its state tax filing, a count that is defined differently.
- **Area:** 87.55 square miles in southeastern Fulton County, in NYSED's Mohawk Valley labor market region.
- **Campuses:** two, at Broadalbin and Perth. The district office is at 20 Pine Street in Broadalbin.
- **Budget:** voters approved a $44,685,000 budget for 2026–27 on 19 May 2026, 357 to 128. Spending rises 3.01% and the tax levy 3.88%, equal to the district's cap.

## Fleet and transportation

Broadalbin-Perth owns and runs its own buses. Its transportation department sends out **23 bus runs a day**, carries more than 1,200 students and logs about **380,000 miles a year**. That is roughly 16,500 miles a year for each run.

NYSDOT recorded 69 safety inspections of the district's vehicles, with four failed, in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026. Buses are inspected twice a year, which points to a fleet of about 35 buses and other vehicles.

Recent bus votes show the replacement cycle:

| Vote | What voters approved | State share (district estimate) | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 21 May 2024 | Two 66-passenger buses, one 14-passenger bus, a van and a large SUV | about 82% | 478 to 130 |
| 19 May 2026 | Three 66-passenger diesel buses | about 75% | 387 to 98 |

Each diesel bus bought now can stay in service well into the 2030s. Under the amended state law, the 2032 rule covers new purchases only, and the whole fleet must be zero-emission by July 2040.

## Depot power and the grid

The state's utility territory map places the district office on Pine Street in **National Grid** territory.

The bus yard is getting a refit through the 2024 capital project, approved on 21 May 2024 by 437 to 177. Construction began in May 2025. The district's March 2024 project newsletter lists $2,023,300 of work at the District Office and Transportation Center, including:

- **$937,100** to replace and increase the capacity of the diesel and gas dispensing tanks;
- **$393,700** to recommission the existing bus wash;
- new roofing sections, windows, doors and fire alarm equipment.

The list includes no charger conduit or electrical service upgrade. A bigger fuel island points to many more years of diesel, which is legal until 2040. It also means the first electric buses will probably need a separate electrical project at the same yard.

The planning has begun. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists the district's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **underway**, although Broadalbin-Perth did not return NYSED's zero-emission survey. In the Mohawk Valley region, 33 of 54 districts had a plan underway, 4 had finished one and 11 had made no contact with NYSERDA. None of the 47 Mohawk Valley districts that described their fleets owned an electric bus, and only two were on order region-wide.

Fulton County winters at the edge of the Adirondacks matter for range. At about 16,500 miles a year per run, the plan should test each run against cold-weather battery range and show which runs would need a midday charge at the garage. For the grid picture across upstate New York, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Capital project:** $37,845,000. The district expected the state to pay about 81% through building aid, with the local share starting on September 2026 tax bills.
- **Buses:** bought through yearly propositions, with state transportation aid covering 75–82% of the cost by the district's estimates.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Broadalbin-Perth.

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

When Broadalbin-Perth buys its first electric buses, the transportation center will need charging power that the current refit does not provide. AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the yard so those buses run while the National Grid service upgrade is completed; AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
