Bainbridge-Guilford buys its buses with cash it has saved. Its 2026–27 filing with NYSED shows a capital reserve of about $4.13 million. The district plans to draw on it, if voters agree, for one 30-passenger bus and two 66-passenger buses. That savings habit matters, because electric buses cost more up front. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 754 students in 2024–25, up from 723 in 2023–24 and level with 753 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 101 square miles in Chenango County, in NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about 7.5 students per square mile. Each bus covers long country roads for few riders, which is typical of the Southern Tier.
- **Budget:** $26,936,072 proposed for 2026–27, up 5.38% from $25,560,016 in 2025–26 (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

The district owns and runs its own fleet. Its schedule of reserves, filed with the 2026–27 property tax report card, shows how it pays for buses:

| Reserve (2026–27 filing) | Balance | Planned use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capital reserve | $4,133,080 | One 30-passenger and two 66-passenger buses, if the voter proposition passes |
| Second capital reserve | $1,050,943 | No intended use in 2026–27 |

Putting buses on a reserve rather than borrowing keeps the tax levy steady. The levy rises only 1.59% in 2026–27, to $7,553,831. The catch is price. A reserve built to buy diesel buses at diesel prices will buy fewer buses once the purchases must be electric. NYSBIP vouchers exist to cover much of that difference.

The planned mix also matters for charging. A 30-passenger bus has a smaller battery and draws less power than a 66-passenger bus, so smaller buses are often the easiest place to start.

## Depot power and the grid

Chenango County has two investor-owned electric utilities, NYSEG and National Grid. The villages of Sherburne and Greene run their own municipal systems (NYS Department of Public Service). Whichever utility feeds the district's bus depot, the spare capacity on its local feeder will set the cost of the first chargers.

Planning has barely started. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Bainbridge-Guilford as having made **initial contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP), but not yet begun one. Across the Southern Tier's 63 districts:

- 5 had a completed plan;
- 22 had one underway;
- 5, including Bainbridge-Guilford, had made initial contact;
- 29 (46%) had made no contact at all.

In a 101-square-mile district, the FEP needs to answer two local questions: which runs fit a single winter charge, and whether the depot's existing electrical service can take the first chargers. See [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap) for the upstate grid picture.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Bainbridge-Guilford. Elsewhere in Chenango County, Georgetown-South Otselic holds one voucher.
- **Local:** the $4.13 million capital reserve is the district's main tool for buying buses. Pairing it with vouchers would let each reserve dollar go further on electric buses.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the district's first electric buses arrive before its utility can upgrade the depot service, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so those buses run on schedule. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
