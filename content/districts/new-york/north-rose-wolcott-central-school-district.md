North Rose-Wolcott's bus garage dates from around 1957, and the district says plainly that it "is not able to service an electric bus." On 17 December 2025 voters approved a $30 million capital project, 170 to 160, whose main purpose is a new transportation facility behind the high school. The margin was ten votes. The district now has a chance to build charging into a depot from the start. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 984 students in 2024–25, down from 1,035 in 2023–24 and 1,058 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 7% fall in two years.
- **Need:** 57% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 123.9 square miles in Wayne County, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Density:** about eight students per square mile.
- **Budget:** voters approved $39,080,000 for 2026–27 on 19 May 2026, 224 to 210.

## Fleet and transportation

The district owns and runs its buses from a garage in the elementary school parking lot. The capital project materials describe the problems:

- The garage was built around 1957 for the smaller buses of the time. Mechanics have little clearance between today's larger buses and the walls.
- Its location in the elementary lot creates a safety hazard at pick-up and drop-off.
- The building is deteriorating and would need extensive repairs to meet code.

The new facility will include a bus garage, a fuel island and a parking lot on district property behind North Rose-Wolcott High School. It will have a properly sized maintenance area, training space, a break room and bathrooms. The old garage will be demolished, and the elementary parking lot and bus loop reconfigured.

The district is still buying diesel. On 19 May 2026 voters approved up to $925,000 from the bus purchase reserve for five diesel buses, 280 to 154. The district's own capital project FAQ says it "does not immediately plan to switch to electric buses."

## Depot power and the grid

The district's campus at 11631 Salter-Colvin Road in Wolcott lies in Rochester Gas and Electric's service territory (Department of Public Service territory map). RG&E will confirm the service available at the new garage site.

The timing matters. The district expects 18 to 22 months from the December 2025 vote to the start of construction, which points to construction starting in mid to late 2027. The bond resolution covers "utility, mechanical, plumbing and electrical improvements". Its listed scope names a bus garage and fuel island, not chargers. Choices made during design in 2026 and 2027 will set the cost of electrification for decades:

- **Service size.** A new building can be given a service large enough for future chargers, or at least the space and conduit for one, far more cheaply than a retrofit.
- **Conduit to parking.** Empty conduit laid under a new lot costs little. Trenching through finished paving later costs much more.
- **A utility load letter.** Asking RG&E early shows how much capacity the feeder has, and how long an upgrade would take.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists the district's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". In the Finger Lakes region, 16 of 70 districts (23%) had a completed plan and 10 (14%) had made no contact with NYSERDA. The FEP should feed the garage design while it is still on paper.

For what happens when a depot is ready before the grid is, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Capital project:** $30 million. $23 million is expected from New York State building aid and $7 million from a capital reserve voters approved in May 2022. The district says there is no additional impact on the tax rate.
- **Earlier projects:** the district calls this the continuation of a phased approach, after capital projects passed in 2021 and 2023.
- **Buses:** diesel purchases come from the bus purchase reserve. NYSERDA's NYSBIP data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no electric bus vouchers for the district.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

North Rose-Wolcott's new garage could open before RG&E delivers the full service its future chargers need. If it does, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses run while the grid connection is completed.
