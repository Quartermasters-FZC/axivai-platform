Hancock covers almost 120 square miles of the upper Delaware valley with fewer than 300 students, about two students per square mile. It has bought buses at every May vote since 2024 and set up a bus reserve to pay for them. What it has not started is the fleet plan. In October 2025 NYSED listed Hancock as having made no contact with NYSERDA, and the district did not return NYSED's zero-emission survey. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 282 students in 2024–25, against 281 in 2023–24 and 302 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Need:** 51% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 119.5 square miles. NYSED groups Delaware County in its Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about 2.4 students per square mile.
- **Budget:** the 2026–27 budget passed on 19 May 2026, 84 to 42 (NYSED).

## Fleet and transportation

Hancock owns its buses. It has asked voters for buses three years running and built a reserve to fund them (NYSED budget vote results):

| Vote | Proposition | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| May 2024 | Bus purchase | Passed 97–53 |
| May 2024 | Establish a bus reserve | Passed 98–51 |
| May 2025 | Passenger buses for student transportation | Passed 82–29 |
| May 2025 | Establish a capital reserve fund | Passed 86–25 |
| May 2026 | School bus purchase | Passed 72–54 |

The reserves matter for an electric transition. State transportation aid repays bus purchases in later years, and an electric bus has a much higher sticker price than a diesel one. A district that pays from a reserve can carry that timing gap more easily than one that has to borrow.

Low density is the other fact that shapes everything here. With so few students over so much ground, runs are long. Each route's winter mileage, with cabin heat on, needs checking against battery range before an electric bus is bought.

## Depot power and the grid

Hancock's buildings sit in NYSEG's territory (New York State utility territory map). NYSEG will decide whether the district's bus parking can take chargers on its current service, or needs an upgrade first. In a rural Delaware County location, an upgrade can mean work on the feeder as well as at the meter.

**The plan.** NYSED's October 2025 report lists Hancock's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) status as "No Contact" and its NYSED survey as "Not Submitted". It was one of nine Southern Tier districts that did not respond. Across the region, 29 of 63 districts (46%) had made no contact with NYSERDA, and only 5 had completed plans.

The FEP is free and is the step that answers the range and power questions. For a district Hancock's size, a request to NYSERDA is the cheapest move available.

**Neighbours.** NYSBIP data shows three vouchered buses in Delaware County: one for Margaretville CSD ($159,250, in a project now closed) and two for Sidney CSD ($174,000 each).

The wider upstate grid picture is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Hancock.
- **Local:** voter-approved bus purchases and the bus reserve created in 2024, plus a capital reserve created in 2025.

How NYSBIP vouchers combine with state aid is covered in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If NYSEG needs to upgrade a rural feeder before Hancock can charge more than a bus or two, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses run during the wait. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
