Corinth Central School District is still buying diesel. In May 2026 its voters approved two new diesel buses for $408,276, by 183 votes to 88. The district has no NYSBIP vouchers. Its buses do have a modern home, though: a garage that opened in 2020. That building is where its electric fleet will one day charge. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,009 students in 2024–25, down from 1,026 in 2023–24 and 1,044 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a fall of about 3% in two years.
- **Area:** 78.08 square miles, in NYSED's Capital Region labor market region.
- **Density:** about 13 students per square mile. Corinth sits in northern Saratoga County, so its routes are long and rural.
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 budget of $29,905,000, with a 2.6% tax levy increase, 173 to 99 (Saratoga TODAY).

## Fleet and transportation

Corinth owns and runs its own buses. It replaces them a few at a time through bus propositions on the annual ballot. The May 2026 proposition bought two diesel buses for $408,276. It passed 183 to 88.

The garage is the district's main asset for electrification. CPL designed it after the old bus garage stopped meeting the fleet's needs:

- total project cost of $5.5 million;
- completed in 2020;
- built to keep the fleet out of the winter weather;
- designed with full architectural, civil, mechanical and electrical engineering.

An indoor garage matters in a northern Saratoga County winter. Electric buses parked inside on chargers can warm their batteries and cabins from the grid before a cold-morning route, not from the battery. The public project materials do not say whether the 2020 design included charger conduit or spare electrical capacity. That is the first question for the district's fleet plan.

## Depot power and the grid

The state's utility territory data place the village of Corinth in National Grid's service territory, so National Grid would size and build any new service for chargers at the garage.

The district's planning has started. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Corinth's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". The FEP is the free study that sizes the chargers and service upgrade a fleet needs. Corinth is ahead of many neighbors on this: across the Capital Region, 31 of 74 districts (42%) had made no contact with NYSERDA, and only two had a completed plan.

The report also shows what the first electric buses nearby look like. Lake George CSD, in neighboring Warren County, ran one electric bus in a fleet of 18. Queensbury ran one in 55. The region reported 27 more zero-emission buses on order.

Two practical issues will shape Corinth's depot plan:

- **Winter range.** At 78 square miles, some runs may need a midday top-up at the garage in deep cold. The FEP should model each run against winter battery range.
- **Service size.** A garage built in 2020 around diesel buses may not have room on its electrical service for a full row of chargers. A load letter from the utility shows how many chargers fit before an upgrade is needed.

For the wider grid picture upstate, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Corinth. Five other Saratoga County districts hold 18 vouchers between them, including South Glens Falls and Shenendehowa with six each.
- **Local:** recent bus propositions have funded diesel buses only. Because Corinth buys its own fleet, its first electric buses will be financed the same way: a voter proposition, NYSBIP vouchers and state transportation aid.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in our [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the fleet plan shows the 2020 garage needs a bigger service, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage, so Corinth's first electric buses can run while the utility upgrade is built. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
