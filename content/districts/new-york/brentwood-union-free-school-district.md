Brentwood already has electric buses on its routes: seven of them, all run by its contractor, Suffolk Transportation Service (STS). Four were added in 2025–26. The district itself has not yet started a fleet plan with NYSERDA. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 17,842 students in 2024–25, against 17,902 in 2023–24 and 17,809 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 14.88 square miles, in NYSED's Long Island labor market region.
- **Density:** about 1,200 students per square mile, far denser than an upstate district. Routes are short, but many buses run at once.

## Fleet and transportation

The district contracts with STS for all its transportation needs. Its 2026–27 transportation budget presentation gives the daily picture:

- **Buses:** about 109 large buses, 173 minibuses and 15 wheelchair buses each day, 297 in all.
- **Riders:** about 16,676 students, carried to 18 district schools, 22 private and parochial schools, and 27 special-needs, UPK and McKinney-Vento locations.
- **Staff:** a driver for each bus plus more than 190 drivers' assistants, over 475 people in all.
- **Electric buses:** "Four new electric buses were added to the fleet this year, there are now a total of seven electric buses in the fleet."

Minibuses outnumber large buses, so much of Brentwood's future electric fleet will be small buses with smaller batteries.

On 5 March 2026 the Board of Education extended the STS contracts for home-to-school, field trip and athletic, and summer transportation through 2026–27. The rate increase will be set by the May cost-of-living adjustment.

**Where the electric buses came from.** In October 2020 the EPA awarded STS a $1,152,687 DERA grant for seven electric buses. The grant was to expand STS's "Clean Buses" partnership with Bay Shore UFSD, which began in 2018, to Brentwood and Central Islip (STS; School Bus Fleet, May 2021). STS runs more than 1,800 vehicles across its operations.

## Depot power and the grid

Brentwood's buses charge at STS facilities, not on district property. On Long Island the grid belongs to the Long Island Power Authority (LIPA), which serves about 1.2 million customers. PSEG Long Island runs it under a contract extended from 1 January 2026 through 31 December 2030.

Two numbers show the scale of the task:

- **297 buses a day** will eventually need charging at the contractor yards serving Brentwood, against seven electric buses today.
- **Long Island's planning gap.** NYSED's October 2025 report lists Brentwood as having made **no contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan. Across Long Island, 97 of 124 districts (78%) were in the same position, and only one had a completed plan. That is the highest no-contact share of the nine upstate and Long Island regions in the report.

For a contracted district, the plan that matters most models route energy and sets out what the next contract should require on yard power. For charging options away from the main yard, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging).

## Funding

- **Transportation budget:** $38,980,209 in 2025–26. The proposed 2026–27 budget is $42,494,843, a 9.01% increase driven by a 5% CPI increase for contracted transportation and more large buses for new school safety zones.
- **Federal:** the 2020 EPA DERA grant ($1.15M, seven buses) went to STS, under its partnership with Bay Shore that was set to expand to Brentwood and Central Islip.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers under Brentwood's name.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Brentwood's electric fleet will grow at STS yards on the PSEG Long Island grid. When a yard needs more capacity than its current service allows, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so new electric buses can run while the utility upgrade is completed. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
