Bolivar-Richburg finished its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan before most of New York had started one. NYSED's October 2025 report lists it as complete, one of 18 in Western New York. The district is still buying conventional buses, and in September 2026 its voters approved a capital project that replaces the roof over the bus garage service bay from summer 2027. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 603 students in 2024–25, down from 637 in 2023–24 and 653 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is an 8% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 98.87 square miles in Allegany County, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region.
- **Density:** about six students per square mile.
- **Budget vote:** on 19 May 2026 voters approved the 2026–27 budget, 73 to 46.

## Fleet and transportation

Bolivar-Richburg owns and runs its own buses. Its 2026–27 budget includes $1,820,745 for district transportation services and $112,200 for the garage building.

On 19 May 2026 voters approved two vehicle propositions:

- **School buses:** up to $543,110, net of trade-ins. The district applies $38,000 from its Transportation Capital Reserve Fund, set up in May 2022, and may borrow the remaining $505,110. State transportation aid repays a large part. It passed 75 to 43.
- **Van:** a student transportation van at up to $43,802.85, paid from its vehicle and equipment reserve. It passed 80 to 39.

NYSERDA's NYSBIP data, updated 26 October 2026, list no electric bus vouchers for Bolivar-Richburg.

## Depot power and the grid

The district's garage is in line for work already. On 8 September 2026 voters approved a capital improvement project, 41 to 10. From summer 2027 it will:

- replace the flat roofs over the maintenance shed and the bus garage service bay;
- put a new roof on the whole elementary building;
- replace the elementary gym floor and update its audio-visual systems;
- add air conditioning to the middle and high school gym's HVAC.

Electrical work for charging is not on that list. With the garage roof open in 2027, the district has a natural moment to check the completed fleet plan against the building. Running conduit or sizing a future service is cheaper alongside construction already planned than as a separate job later.

Rural routes cover nearly 100 square miles. The fleet plan should show which runs a winter battery can cover and how many chargers the garage needs.

For upstate grid and depot issues, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap) and [school-bus depot power](/research/school-bus-depots).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** no vouchers as of October 2026.
- **Reserves:** a Transportation Capital Reserve Fund and a vehicle and equipment reserve, both set up in 2022, already pay part of each year's vehicles.
- **Local:** borrowing for buses, offset by state transportation aid.

Program rules are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the fleet plan shows the garage needs a larger service before charging can start, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage. Bolivar-Richburg's first electric buses could then run while the utility upgrade is completed.
