Adirondack Central School District has already held a public vote on electric buses, and lost it. In October 2023 voters rejected a federal grant that would have paid for three electric buses and their chargers. Two years later they approved a capital project that replaces the bus garage's utility transformer and service entrance feeder. That is the equipment that limits how many chargers the garage can run. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,098 students in 2024–25, against 1,097 in 2023–24 and 1,106 in 2022–23 (NYSED). Enrollment is close to flat.
- **Area:** 360.5 square miles, in NYSED's Mohawk Valley labor market region. That is roughly three students per square mile, so routes are long and loads are light.
- **Budget:** proposed 2026–27 spending is $32,883,774, up 2.11% from $32,204,547 (NYSED Property Tax Report Card).

## Fleet and transportation

Adirondack owns and runs its own fleet. Figures the district published for the 2023 vote:

- **Fleet:** 30 vehicles in total.
- **Route length:** 10 bus runs under 100 miles. The district put a new electric bus's range at 135 miles on a full charge, before losses to cold and terrain.
- **Bus prices:** about $165,000 for a gas or diesel bus, against "well over $400,000" for an electric one, before charging and construction costs.
- **Typical yearly purchase:** three gas buses, at $493,000, or $121,495 locally after trade-ins and transportation aid.
- **Transportation aid:** the district gets 73.9% of approved transportation costs back from the state.

## Depot power and the grid

The 2023 plan contained a useful finding. The district said each Level 2 charger would draw 16.6 kW and that the garage's existing transformer could carry three buses. That is enough for a first small batch, but not for a 30-vehicle fleet.

That transformer is now being replaced. Voters approved an $18,206,250 capital project, 114 to 66, in a re-vote on 9 September 2025. They had first approved it on 20 May 2025, but the legal notice for that vote was published with an error. The re-vote kept the project eligible for full building aid. Work at the bus garage includes:

- the utility transformer and the service entrance feeder;
- the generator and the bus lift;
- the roof, plus the oil-fired furnace and ventilation in bus maintenance.

The new transformer and feeder can be sized for future charging, so it is worth checking that the specification has room for it. Boonville has its own public power utility, the Municipal Commission of Boonville, which is registered with the Public Service Commission. Whichever utility feeds the garage will have to approve the new service size.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists Adirondack's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as "Underway". Across the Mohawk Valley, 4 of 54 districts (7%) had a completed plan, 33 (61%) were underway and 11 (20%) had made no contact.

## Funding

**The 2023 electric bus vote.** In Round 1 of the EPA Clean School Bus program, Adirondack won $395,000 per bus for three buses: $1,185,000 in all. The district asked for extensions. When the last one ran out, it put the grant to a public vote.

The full project was $1,329,738, covering three buses, Level 2 chargers and electrical upgrades. With the grant and state aid, the local share would have been about $30,600. Without the grant, the district estimated $339,885.

On 24 October 2023 voters rejected the proposition, 167 to 248, and the district declined the grant (Flack Broadcasting).

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Adirondack.
- **Local:** the 2025 capital project, including the garage electrical work, carries a zero percent tax increase (Rome Sentinel).

Program amounts are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Adirondack's garage is about to get a new transformer, but a 30-vehicle electric fleet over 360 square miles may still outgrow it. Where the service can't keep up, AXIVAI brings battery power by truck into storage at the garage, so electric buses can run while the utility upgrade catches up.
