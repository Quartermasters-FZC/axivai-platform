East Bloomfield Central School District, known locally as Bloomfield CSD, is about to build a new bus depot. Voters turned down a $35 million plan built around a new transportation center in December 2024. A revised $27.7 million capital project was later approved, and it too is led by a new, relocated transportation facility. Few districts get to design a bus garage from scratch with the 2032 purchase deadline in view. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 712 students in 2024–25, against 717 in 2023–24 and 771 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 7.7% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 60.61 square miles, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Density:** about 12 students per square mile. Rural routes, but shorter than those in the region's largest districts.
- **Budget:** proposed 2026–27 spending is $25,097,830, up 3.21% from $24,318,250. The tax levy rises 1.94%, to $11,737,794 (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

Bloomfield owns its buses and is saving to replace them. NYSED's 2026–27 schedule of reserves lists a capital reserve of $935,000 "to fund student transportation vehicles", up from $731,487 at the previous reporting date. A separate capital reserve of $1,095,000 is set aside for capital project work.

The district has argued that moving the current bus garage would make the Middle/High School campus on Oakmount Avenue "safer". Under the December 2024 plan, the old garage would have become Career and Technical Education space leased to BOCES (WHEC).

**The new depot.** The approved 2025 capital project is led by a "New, Relocated Transportation Facility". Design documents call for an 11,000-square-foot transportation building with sidewalks and parking. The rest of the project covers boiler replacement, HVAC work, fire alarm upgrades, door and window work, and mass notification systems. As of 3 December 2025, the project was approved and in design, with an architect selected (ConstructConnect). Public project listings do not mention chargers, conduit or the size of the new electrical service.

## Depot power and the grid

The NYS Department of Public Service's electric utility territory map places the Village of Bloomfield and the school campus in Rochester Gas and Electric (RG&E) territory. The relocated depot will need its own service, which is the best moment to size it for charging.

Three choices made in design will set the cost of electrification later:

- **Service size.** A transformer and switchgear sized for future chargers costs far less now than a service upgrade after the building opens.
- **Empty conduit.** Conduit to bus parking spaces laid before paving avoids trenching through new asphalt.
- **Indoor or outdoor parking.** Charging indoors helps batteries in an Ontario County winter. Outdoor parking needs pedestals and heavier cabling.

Planning has started. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Bloomfield's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". Across the Finger Lakes region, 16 of 70 districts had completed plans and 42 were underway. Twenty-seven Finger Lakes districts told NYSED they expected to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet, and three said they needed a new depot. Bloomfield is already building one. An FEP finished before design closes can feed charger counts and load directly into the building's electrical drawings. For the upstate grid picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Capital project:** $27.7 million, covering the new transportation facility and building work across both schools (ConstructConnect).
- **Vehicle reserve:** $935,000 set aside for transportation vehicles (NYSED).
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's public NYSBIP project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Bloomfield.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If RG&E can't energize the new depot at full charging capacity by the time it opens, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so electric buses can run until the full service arrives. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
