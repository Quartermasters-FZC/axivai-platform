Brasher Falls Central School District, known locally as St. Lawrence Central, runs its own buses across 154.7 square miles of St. Lawrence County, just south of the Canadian border. Few New York districts face colder winter mornings. Voters bought three more diesel buses in May 2025, and the district has a NYSERDA fleet plan underway. Several nearby North Country districts already run electric buses through the same winters. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Name:** the legal name is Brasher Falls CSD. The district operates as St. Lawrence Central School, which is the name on its NYSDOT bus inspections and in local budget coverage.
- **Enrollment:** 893 students in 2024–25, down from 915 in 2023–24 and 941 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 5% fall in two years.
- **Need:** 62% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 154.7 square miles, in NYSED's North Country labor market region. That is about six students per square mile.
- **Schools:** St. Lawrence Elementary, Middle and High schools, in Brasher Falls (NYSED).

## Fleet and transportation

St. Lawrence Central owns and runs its own buses. NYSDOT recorded 51 safety inspections of its vehicles, with four failed, in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026. Buses are inspected twice a year, which points to a fleet of about 25 buses and other vehicles.

The district replaces buses through yearly propositions. On 20 May 2025 voters approved three bus purchases, 179 to 26, alongside a $31.7 million budget that passed 185 to 20 (North Country Now). Under the amended state law, diesel buses bought now can run until the 2040 full-fleet deadline.

With six students per square mile, routes are long and loads are light.

## Depot power and the grid

The state's utility territory map places Brasher Falls, and the district's address on State Route 11C, in **National Grid** territory.

Winter is the main design constraint. A cold battery loses range, and heating the cabin draws on the same battery. In St. Lawrence County that makes indoor or plugged-in overnight parking, and preheating from the grid before the morning run, part of the basic design rather than an extra.

The district has started the study that sizes this. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **underway**. Across the North Country region, 36 of 65 districts had a plan underway, 4 had finished one and 23 (35%) had made no contact with NYSERDA.

Cold-climate operation is already being tested nearby. The same NYSED report lists six North Country districts already running electric buses:

Region-wide, districts reported seven more electric buses on order. Their experience on routes in Franklin, St. Lawrence and Jefferson counties is the closest benchmark St. Lawrence Central has. For the grid picture across upstate New York, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Budget:** the district's 2026–27 state filing shows spending of $33,878,443, up 6.83%, and a tax levy up 4.75%.
- **Reserves:** NYSED's 2026–27 schedule of reserves lists two capital reserves of about $2.5 million and $2.4 million, with no planned use in 2026–27. It also lists a proposed new $500,000 capital reserve that depended on voter approval.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for the district. The only St. Lawrence County recipient so far is Gouverneur CSD, with four buses.

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the fleet plan shows the bus garage needs a bigger National Grid service for winter charging and preheating, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on site so the first electric buses run before the upgrade is finished. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
