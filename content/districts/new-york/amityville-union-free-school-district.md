Amityville's electric buses, when they come, will most likely arrive through its bus contractor rather than a district purchase. Educational Bus Transportation (EBT) counts Amityville among the Long Island districts it serves, and next door in Copiague it already holds ten NYSBIP vouchers for electric buses. Amityville itself has no vouchers and had not contacted NYSERDA about a fleet plan by October 2025. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 2,678 students in 2024–25, down from 2,721 in 2023–24 and 2,756 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 3% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 5.17 square miles in NYSED's Long Island labor market region, about 518 students per square mile. Routes are short; the challenge is cost, not range.
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 budget of $123,963,246 on 19 May 2026, 740 to 621. Spending rises 2.64% and the tax levy 1.56%, to $68,011,079 (Patch; NYSED).

**Finances shape every contract decision.** The State Comptroller placed Amityville in "significant fiscal stress", its highest category, based on 2022–23 results (Patch, February 2024). That month the board announced 47 layoffs to close a $3.6 million deficit (CBS New York). Unrestricted fund balance stands at 3.98% of the 2026–27 budget, just under the 4% state limit (NYSED). Any rise in transportation cost from electric buses lands on a budget with little slack.

## Fleet and transportation

Amityville's riders travel on contractor buses from EBT. EBT is a member of The Trans Group and serves districts across eastern Nassau and western Suffolk, including Copiague, Massapequa, Seaford and Wantagh (LongIsland.com, January 2026).

The link goes back years. In March 2016 EBT won a $200,000 EPA clean diesel rebate for new buses serving riders in Copiague, Massapequa, Amityville and Half Hollow Hills (Patch).

**The Copiague example next door.** NYSERDA's NYSBIP data lists ten vouchers for Copiague with EBT as operator, at $257,250 each, all at a West Babylon address:

| Approved | Buses | Model | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 November 2024 | 5 | Thomas Saf-T-Liner C2 Jouley | Approved |

In January 2026 Copiague, EBT and the charging company Zenobē announced the ten buses as a first phase, with Zenobē supplying the chargers and battery management. The charging was described as built to scale. If EBT charges at a shared yard, Amityville routes could draw on the same equipment later.

## Depot power and the grid

Amityville's buses would charge at contractor yards, not on school land. The grid on Long Island belongs to the Long Island Power Authority (LIPA), which serves about 1.2 million customers. PSEG Long Island runs it under a contract extended through 31 December 2030.

NYSED's October 2025 report shows how early the planning is:

- Amityville did not submit NYSED's zero-emission survey and had made **no contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan.
- Across Long Island, 97 of 124 districts (78%) were in the same position, and only one had a completed plan.

For a contracted district, the plan that matters most models route energy and sets what the next contract should require on yard power. For charging options away from the main yard, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers under Amityville's name. Contractors can apply for vouchers, as EBT did for Copiague.
- **Federal:** the 2016 EPA rebate ($200,000) went to EBT for clean diesel buses, not electric ones.
- **Local:** the 2026–27 budget passed with a 1.56% levy rise. The district is not the bus buyer, so electric costs will reach it through contract rates.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Amityville's electric buses will charge at contractor yards on the PSEG Long Island grid. When a yard needs more capacity than its service allows, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so new electric buses can run while the utility upgrade is completed.
