Central Square Central School District runs one of the largest district-owned bus fleets in Central New York: NYSDOT inspected its vehicles 228 times in the year to March 2026, which points to about 110 buses and other vehicles. It covers 180.4 square miles of southern Oswego County, the second-largest district by area in its region. It has a NYSERDA fleet plan underway, no electric buses and no capital reserve set aside for them. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 3,316 students in 2024–25, down from 3,410 in 2023–24 and 3,439 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 3.6% fall in two years.
- **Need:** 47% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 180.4 square miles, in NYSED's Central New York labor market region. Only Sandy Creek is larger among the region's 49 districts. That is about 18 students per square mile.
- **Budget:** the 2026–27 state filing shows spending of $106,023,842, up 3.28%, and a tax levy of $34,025,563, up 2.99%. The filing projects enrollment down 3.62%.

## Fleet and transportation

Central Square owns and runs its buses. NYSDOT recorded **228 safety inspections** of its vehicles, five of them failed, in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026. Buses are inspected twice a year, which points to a fleet of about 110 buses and other vehicles. In NYSED's survey, only 8 of the 45 Central New York districts that described their fleets had more than 60 buses.

A fleet that size replaces several buses every year. Each one bought before July 2032 can be diesel. Each one bought after must be zero-emission. Over the 2030s, the fleet will turn over to electric at about the pace it is replaced now, so the garage's power supply has to grow at the same pace.

Central Square does not save ahead for this. NYSED's 2026–27 schedule of reserves lists reserves for workers' compensation, tax certiorari, employee benefits and retirement contributions, but **no capital or bus reserve**. Bus purchases will depend on propositions, state transportation aid and vouchers.

## Depot power and the grid

The state's utility territory map places the village of Central Square in **National Grid** territory.

The scale is what makes Central Square different. Moving 100-plus vehicles to electric will need many chargers and a much larger electrical service at the bus garage, and probably new transformer capacity. Large power transformers have taken two to four years to arrive (NIAC, June 2024). Phasing matters: a utility application filed ahead of the first purchases gives National Grid time to build.

Winter adds to the load. Oswego County lies in the Lake Ontario snow belt. Cold batteries lose range, and buses that preheat while plugged in add to peak demand at the garage just before the morning runs.

The plan is underway. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Central Square's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **underway**. Central New York is behind other regions:

| Central New York, October 2025 | Districts |
| --- | --- |
| Fleet plan complete | 3 of 49 |
| Fleet plan underway | 20 of 49 |
| No contact with NYSERDA | 20 of 49 |
| Running an electric bus | 1 (Fabius-Pompey, 1 bus of 26) |
| Electric buses on order | none reported |

For the grid picture across upstate New York, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Central Square. In recent awards, a full-size electric bus has drawn a $257,250 voucher.
- **Reserves:** no capital or bus reserve in NYSED's 2026–27 schedule.
- **State aid:** as a district-operated fleet, Central Square's bus purchases draw state transportation aid.

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Central Square's garage will need far more power as each year's new buses arrive electric from 2032. AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so those buses run while National Grid completes the service and transformer upgrades; AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
