Buffalo Public Schools has funding in place for 25 electric school buses. The buses will be owned and run by First Student, the district's yellow-bus contractor for about 40 years. Two programs back them: the EPA's Clean School Bus rebates and New York's NYSBIP vouchers. Whether they run on time depends on power at a First Student yard, not at a district garage. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 27,827 students in 2024–25, against 27,450 in 2023–24 and 27,758 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 38.94 square miles, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region.
- **Transportation scale:** First Student carries about 26,600 students a day to 112 public, private and charter school sites (First Student, June 2026).

## Fleet and transportation

Buffalo runs two contracts:

- **Yellow buses (pre-K to grade 8):** First Student. The new five-year contract for 2026–2031 is worth $460 million, an 89% increase on the $244 million paid for 2020–2025.
- **High school:** NFTA Metro bus and rail. The board unanimously approved a five-year, $81 million contract covering about 9,000 high school students. The previous deal, for 2021–2026, was $50 million. New York reimburses 87% of the NFTA cost the year after it is spent (Buffalo News via Mass Transit, 1 September 2026).

Because First Student owns the yellow fleet, every bus purchase, and every charger, is First Student's capital decision. The 2026–2031 contract will run until shortly before the 2032 zero-emission purchase rule takes effect. The contract after it will be the first one written under that rule.

## The 25 electric buses

- **EPA award:** In May 2024, First Student and Buffalo Public Schools won $8,625,000 in third-round Clean School Bus rebates for 25 electric buses. It was the district's first such grant. The Buffalo Board of Education had passed a supporting resolution in August 2023 (First Student).
- **NYSBIP vouchers:** NYSERDA's project data lists 25 battery-electric buses for Buffalo with First Student. All are model-year 2026 IC Bus CE Series (Class 7) buses. Each received a $196,000 voucher, approved on 28 September 2024, for $4,900,000 in total. The records flag Buffalo as a priority district and the buses as located inside a Disadvantaged Community.

The two counts match: both programs name 25 electric buses for Buffalo through First Student.

By March 2025 the district said it was "committed to moving towards electrifying buses" but that First Student was in charge of acquiring them (WSKG). We found no public report of the 25 buses entering service by October 2026.

## Depot power and the grid

National Grid serves Buffalo, and the city already has New York's largest electric bus depot. NFTA's Cold Spring garage opened in September 2024 with 36 overhead chargers and a new NFTA-owned substation that National Grid helped fund. Its electrical capacity covers about 50 buses, half the depot, and the substation can be expanded (Governor's office). That project shows the scale of grid work a large urban bus yard can need.

A 25-bus school yard is smaller, but the questions are the same: service size, a possible new transformer, and the utility's timeline. Buffalo's own planning lags behind. NYSED's October 2025 report shows Buffalo did not submit the state's zero-emission survey. It lists the district's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan status as "Initial Contact Made". In the same region, 18 of 80 districts (22%) had a completed plan.

Winter adds load. In lake-effect cold, buses should warm their batteries and cabins while still plugged in, which raises overnight energy use at the yard. See [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap) for how utility lead times compare with voucher deadlines.

## Where AXIVAI fits

Buffalo has funded buses. Their start date now depends on power at a contractor yard. If National Grid's work on that yard runs past bus delivery, AXIVAI can bring charging power by truck into battery storage on site. The buses can then run, and stay warm through a Buffalo winter, until the permanent connection is live. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
