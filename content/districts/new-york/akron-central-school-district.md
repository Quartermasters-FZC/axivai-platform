Akron Central School District is one of the few in New York with a finished fleet electrification plan. It is also one of the few whose depot depends on a village power system rather than a large utility. The campus sits inside the Village of Akron, which runs its own electric service. In 2024 the Board of Education told the state that the village grid would need upgrades before the district could support a full electric fleet. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,178 students in 2024–25, against 1,171 in 2023–24 and 1,227 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 78.4 square miles, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region. The district spans parts of Erie, Genesee and Niagara counties.
- **Budget:** proposed 2026–27 spending is $40,857,103, up 4.64% from $39,044,762 (NYSED Property Tax Report Card). The transportation line rises from $1,920,348 to $2,033,187.

## Fleet and transportation

Akron owns its buses and replaces a few each year through a separate ballot proposition:

- **2025–26:** $554,000 for the purchase and financing of school buses.
- **2026–27:** up to $572,000 for two 66-passenger diesel buses (up to $176,000 each) and two gasoline van-type buses (up to $110,000 each). The district projects state transportation aid at 69.7% and says the purchase "avoids future bulk replacements".

Both years' buses are diesel and gasoline. Each one bought now will probably still be running after the 2032 purchase deadline.

The bus garage is being rebuilt. Voters approved the $29,829,634 Capital Improvements Project 2023 on 16 May 2023, by 496 to 337. It includes a new employee area, office and restroom addition at the transportation facility. The district's 2026–27 budget presentation lists an "Expanded and renovated Transportation Facility" in Phase 1, scheduled for completion in June 2026.

## Depot power and the grid

**A village utility.** The Village of Akron has its own municipal electric service. It belongs to the Municipal Electric Utilities Association and is regulated by the Public Service Commission. Its franchise covers the village limits and a few historical customers, and it does not take new customers outside the village. The district's campus is inside it.

**What the board said.** In April 2024 the board adopted a resolution asking the state to slow the zero-emission rollout. It made three local points (Akron Bugle):

- A Type C electric bus starts at about $400,000, against $142,000 for a gasoline or clean diesel bus.
- Replacing the whole fleet with zero-emission Type A and Type C buses would cost an estimated $12,500,000.
- The district "is not yet capable of supporting a full fleet of electric buses". The village would need upgrades to both its power supply and its delivery infrastructure, with costs unknown to either party.

The deadline has since moved to 2032 for purchases and 2040 for the whole fleet, which gives the village and the district more time to plan.

**The plan is done.** NYSED's October 2025 report lists Akron's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as "Complete". In Western New York, 18 of 80 districts (22%) had a finished plan, 43 (54%) were underway and 13 had made no contact. Akron now has a sized charging load to put in front of the village. Its next step is a load request for the renovated garage.

The wider upstate picture is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Akron.
- **Local:** buses are funded through annual propositions with state transportation aid (projected 69.7% for the 2026–27 purchase).
- **Capital:** the 2023 capital project already covers the transportation facility. It does not mention chargers.

Program amounts are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Akron's chargers depend on what the Village of Akron's system can deliver, and the cost and timing of village upgrades are still unknown. AXIVAI brings battery power by truck into storage at the garage, so electric buses can run while the village grid is upgraded.
