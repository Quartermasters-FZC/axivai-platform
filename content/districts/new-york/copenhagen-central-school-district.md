Copenhagen Central School District is one of the North Country's small, district-run fleets. It carries 434 students over 88 square miles of Lewis County, on the western edge of the Tug Hill Plateau. Its NYSERDA fleet plan was underway by October 2025, which puts it ahead of a third of the region. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 434 students in 2024–25, against 442 in 2023–24 and 417 in 2022–23 (NYSED). Unlike most rural districts, enrollment is roughly flat.
- **Area:** 88.3 square miles, in NYSED's North Country labor market region.
- **Density:** about five students per square mile.
- **Region:** the North Country's 65 districts cover about 11,309 square miles, the largest area of any region in NYSED's report, for 53,120 students.

## Fleet and transportation

Copenhagen owns and runs its buses. NYSDOT recorded 38 safety inspections of the district's vehicles in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026, two of which failed and needed re-inspection. Buses are inspected twice a year, so that points to a fleet of roughly 18 to 19 buses and other vehicles.

A small fleet changes the electric calculation:

- **Each bus is a big share of the fleet.** Replacing one diesel bus with an electric bus converts about 5% of the fleet at once. The first one or two buses decide how the garage is wired.
- **Spare capacity is thin.** With under 20 vehicles, there are few spares to cover a bus that is charging, cold-soaked or out of service.
- **Winter.** Tug Hill country is known for heavy lake-effect snow. Cold reduces battery range, and pre-heating a bus from the charger before the run, not from the battery, matters more here than in most of the state.

**The capital project skips the garage.** The district's 2024 capital project was approved and in pre-construction by December 2024. Its public description lists roof, masonry, mechanical, gym and general building work on the main campus at 3020 Mechanic Street, with bidding and construction expected in summer 2026. The bus garage and charging are not in that scope. 2025 bid packages covered items such as replacing doors in two elementary wings of the Main Building. Any charger wiring would therefore need its own authorization or a later project.

## Depot power and the grid

The village of Copenhagen sits in National Grid's service territory (formerly Niagara Mohawk), according to the state's utility territory data. National Grid would size and build any new service to the bus garage.

NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Copenhagen's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". The FEP is the free study that models each route against battery range and sizes the chargers and service upgrade a fleet needs.

How Copenhagen compares across the North Country:

| FEP status (Oct 2025) | Districts | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete | 4 | 6% |
| Underway (incl. Copenhagen) | 36 | 55% |
| Initial contact | 2 | 3% |
| No contact | 23 | 36% |

Two things to settle once the plan is complete:

- **Service size at the garage.** A load letter from National Grid shows whether the first chargers fit within the existing service, or whether a transformer or service upgrade is needed first.
- **Winter range.** Each run should be modelled against cold-weather range, not the rated range, to see which routes need a midday top-up.

For the wider upstate grid picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Copenhagen.
- **Local:** because Copenhagen buys its own buses, future electric buses will be financed through voter-approved bus propositions, NYSBIP vouchers and state transportation aid.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Copenhagen's fleet plan shows that the garage needs a National Grid service upgrade, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage, so the first electric buses can charge and pre-heat while that work is done. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
