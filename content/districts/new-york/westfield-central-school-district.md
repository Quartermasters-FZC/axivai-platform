Westfield Academy and Central School has done the planning many districts have not started. Its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan is complete, and its board has already heard the price of the charging work: about $2.5 million. What sets Westfield apart is its power supplier. The village owns its own electric utility, so the depot conversation is with the Village Board of Public Works, not an investor-owned utility. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 592 students in 2024–25, down from 603 in 2023–24 and 604 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 41.93 square miles on the Lake Erie shore, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region.
- **Density:** about 14 students per square mile.
- **Campus:** one K–12 school at 203 East Main Street in the Village of Westfield.

## Fleet and transportation

Westfield owns and runs its own buses, and voters approve its bus purchases at the annual budget vote. The fleet is kept at the district's bus garage on the main campus. In 2024 the district contracted masonry repairs to the garage's bus bays (ConstructConnect).

**The transition plan.** In late 2024 the engineering firm Wendel presented a zero-emission fleet transition plan to the Board of Education (Post-Journal, 16 October 2024). Points from that meeting:

- **Charging cost:** about $2.5 million for the infrastructure. Wendel advised installing all of it at once, most likely through a capital project.
- **Lead time:** the infrastructure is a two-year project. Wendel's presenter said the district almost needed "to start now just to meet the mandate."
- **Incentives:** as a high-priority district, Westfield qualifies for a NYSBIP priority bonus of $28,500 to $39,000 per bus, depending on vehicle type, plus charging vouchers.
- **Board questions:** fire safety, how to evacuate students with disabilities in a fire, charging times, higher utility bills, battery replacement, and climbing hills in winter.

The hills question is a real one. The district runs from the lakeshore up the escarpment south of the village, and those climbs in lake-effect snow draw the most battery power. The plan should test each route against winter range.

**The capital program.** In April 2026 the district broke ground on a $23.5 million capital project focused on athletic fields and classrooms (Observer, 7 May 2026). Public coverage does not mention bus charging. The $2.5 million charging work would need its own project or a later phase.

## Depot power and the grid

The Village of Westfield Electric Department serves the town and village of Westfield, more than 3,300 residential and commercial customers, under the New York Municipal Power Agency tariff. Westfield is a NYMPA member and buys an allocation of low-cost hydropower from the New York Power Authority through it. Rates stayed unchanged from 1999 to 2021 (Observer, February 2021).

Recent Board of Public Works minutes show a small system under strain:

- **Rates:** new electric rates took effect in January 2025, an increase of about 36%. A further increase was expected in October 2025, after statewide power-authority cost increases.
- **One primary feed:** the village sought a NYSERDA grant for a study of a second primary feed into the village. It missed the first round in January 2025. In October 2025 NYSERDA was still reviewing the revised scope.
- **Equipment lead times:** new circuit equipment ordered in early 2025 carried a 72-week lead time, with delivery due in spring 2026. New transformers were ordered, with a supplier kick-off in October 2025. Relay panels for the Bourne Street substation were out to bid.
- **New loads pay:** for a downtown redevelopment, the village told the developer in writing what it would cost to serve the building's added electric load.

For the bus garage, that means three things. A depot load of several hundred kilowatts is large for a 3,300-customer system. The village may ask the district to pay for the upgrade. And any new transformer joins a queue that has already run to more than a year. A load letter from the village, requested early, would show how many chargers the garage can take before upgrades. For transformer timelines, see [transformer lead times in New York and California](/research/ev-charging-grid-connection-wait-times).

Across Western New York, 33 districts expect to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet (NYSED, October 2025).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Westfield.
- **Priority status:** Wendel's presentation placed Westfield among high-priority districts eligible for the larger NYSBIP bonus.
- **Local:** bus purchases go to voters as propositions. The charging work would most likely need a capital project vote.

Program amounts are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the village needs a second feed or a new transformer before the garage can take chargers, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so Westfield's first electric buses run in the meantime. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
