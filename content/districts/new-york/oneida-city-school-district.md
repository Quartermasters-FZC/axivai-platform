Oneida learned in 2025 how much a school system depends on its electrical equipment. A severe storm on 22 June 2025 flooded Oneida High School. The damage, centred on the basement, hit boilers, the fire alarm system and water heaters, "top to bottom across the building," the superintendent told WSYR. The building stayed closed until late January 2026, and the bus schedule was rebuilt twice in the meantime. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,587 students in 2024–25, down from 1,639 in 2023–24 and 1,687 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 6% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 36.06 square miles, in NYSED's Central New York labor market region.
- **Density:** about 44 students per square mile, covering the city of Oneida and the surrounding towns.
- **Votes:** voters passed the budget and a bus purchase proposition in May 2025. In May 2026 they passed the budget, a bus purchase and a capital outlay project (WSYR results).

## Fleet and transportation

Oneida owns and runs its buses. The flood year showed how quickly the fleet can be reassigned.

- **First plan (August 2025):** move high school classes to the old Bishop Grimes school building, with every student offered yellow-bus transportation on a 50-mile round trip by Thruway, coordinated with sheriffs' offices and neighbouring districts.
- **Final plan (September 2025):** high school and middle school students split Otto Shortell Middle School. High schoolers attended from 7 a.m. to 11 a.m., middle schoolers from 11:30 a.m. to 3:48 p.m. Elementary dismissal moved 10 minutes earlier to fit the new bus routes.
- **Back to normal:** full days resumed in late January 2026. The high school reopened on 27 January, a day later than planned because of snow.

The district said insurance and state aid covered the costs, and taxes did not rise for them.

Midday runs and split sessions mean more bus trips per day and less idle time in the garage. For an electric fleet, that cuts into the window for charging. It is a useful stress test for Oneida's fleet plan: can the buses handle an emergency schedule on a winter day?

## Depot power and the grid

Oneida sits in National Grid's territory (NYS electric utility territory map). Its neighbour, the city of Sherrill, runs its own municipal electric system, so the utility can differ across a short distance in this part of Madison County.

Oneida has started planning. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) status as **applied**. Across Central New York, 20 of 49 districts (41%) had a plan underway and only three (6%) had finished. Another 20 (41%) had made no contact.

**The flood lesson for chargers.** The 2025 damage was electrical and mechanical, centred on a basement. Charger cabinets, switchgear and transformers for a bus depot should sit above flood level, with a plan for running buses if the main service is out.

For why upstate grid upgrades take years, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Flood recovery:** covered by insurance and state aid, with no tax increase, according to the district.
- **Buses:** funded by annual voter propositions with state transportation aid (2025 and 2026 propositions passed).
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Oneida.

## Where AXIVAI fits

AXIVAI brings battery power by truck into storage at the bus garage, so Oneida's first electric buses can run before a National Grid service upgrade is finished and the yard keeps some power if the main service fails. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
