Argyle Central School District is a small rural district in Washington County with a NYSERDA fleet plan already underway. That puts it ahead of the 31 Capital Region districts that had not contacted NYSERDA at all by October 2025. It is also holding about $1 million in a capital reserve for a building project, which is the natural place to fund depot wiring. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 475 students in 2024–25, against 477 in 2023–24 and 473 in 2022–23 (NYSED). Enrollment has held steady.
- **Area:** 58.83 square miles, in NYSED's Capital Region labor market region.
- **Density:** about eight students per square mile.
- **Budget:** the proposed 2026–27 budget is $15,535,655, up 1.11% from $15,364,807. The tax levy rises 3.44% to $5,490,000 (NYSED Property Tax Report Card).

## Fleet and transportation

Argyle has no electric buses and no NYSBIP vouchers. Its NYSED reserve schedules list no separate bus reserve.

In the Capital Region, how a district gets its students to school varies more than it does upstate. NYSED's October 2025 survey found:

- 36 of 65 responding districts (55%) own and run all their buses;
- 15 (23%) contract out all transportation;
- 14 (22%) use a mix.

Distance shapes the bus day here. A rural 59-square-mile district means long runs and few riders per stop. Those runs are the ones the fleet plan has to test against winter battery range before the first electric bus is ordered.

## Depot power and the grid

Argyle is in National Grid territory. NYSERDA's distributed solar dataset lists 81 projects in the 12809 ZIP code, all on National Grid circuits fed from its Burgoyne and Battenkill substations. NYSED names National Grid, NYSEG and Central Hudson as the Capital Region's main utilities.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists Argyle's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **Underway**. Across the Capital Region:

- 2 of 74 districts (3%) had a completed plan;
- 38 (51%) had one underway, Argyle among them;
- 31 (42%) had made no contact with NYSERDA.

Thirteen Capital Region districts told NYSED they expect to need an electric service upgrade for a fully electric fleet. A rural feeder built for farm and household loads may have little spare capacity for several chargers. The fleet plan, followed by a load request to National Grid, shows how many chargers the existing service can carry.

Upstate grid timelines are covered in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Argyle.
- **Capital reserve:** Argyle's capital reserve held $1,019,729 at the 2026–27 filing, projected at $1,030,000. The district earmarked it "on capital project if approved by voters". A year earlier the same reserve was flagged for a potential capital project vote in December 2025 (NYSED).
- **Fund balance:** the district planned to apply $1,244,155 of fund balance to the 2026–27 budget, down from $1,525,849 the year before (NYSED).

A capital project that touches the bus area is the cheapest moment to lay conduit for future chargers, even before any electric bus is bought. Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Argyle's fleet plan shows the rural National Grid service can't carry the chargers it needs, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses run while the upgrade is completed. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
