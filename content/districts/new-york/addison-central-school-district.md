Addison Central School District runs its buses out of a garage that is barely a year old. It opened in June 2025, two years before the old 2027 purchase deadline. The legal deadline has since moved to 2032, so Addison has time to plan charging into a building it already owns. Few rural districts start from that position. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 920 students in 2024–25, down from 936 in 2023–24 and 970 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 5% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 163 square miles, in NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about six students per square mile. That ratio drives what transportation costs here: long rural runs and few riders per bus.
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 budget of $38,941,581 on 19 May 2026, 164 to 105. No bus purchase proposition was on that ballot (WETM).

## Fleet and transportation

Addison owns and runs its own buses. Its new transportation building has:

- 29,000 square feet of space;
- 36 bays for buses and other district vehicles;
- offices, a training center and storage;
- a fuel island, with all utilities installed.

The garage was part of the district's $23.9 million Capstone Project, which also renovated the Middle/High School and added parking at the athletic fields. The ribbon was cut on 25 June 2025 (Campus Construction Management Group).

Indoor storage matters for electric buses in a Steuben County winter. Parking a battery inside, on a charger, overnight lets the bus warm its pack and cabin from the grid before the route, not from the battery. Addison has the indoor space. The open question is the electrical service. The building was designed around a fuel island, and the public project materials do not mention charger conduit or spare electrical capacity.

## Depot power and the grid

Two electric utilities serve Steuben County: NYSEG and the member-owned Steuben Rural Electric Cooperative (Steuben County Office of Emergency Services). Which one feeds the garage, and how much spare capacity that feeder has, will set the cost of the first chargers.

The district's planning has started. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Addison's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) status as "Applied". The FEP is the free study that sizes chargers and the service upgrade a fleet needs. Across the Southern Tier region, 29 of 63 districts (46%) had made no contact with NYSERDA at all.

Rural depots raise two practical issues:

- **Range.** In a 163-square-mile district, a few runs may be too long for one winter charge. They may need a midday top-up at the garage. The FEP should model each run against winter battery range.
- **The garage's service size.** Adding chargers for 36 bays could exceed the service a diesel-era building was given. A load letter from the utility, obtained early, shows whether the first few chargers fit within the existing service.

The wider grid picture in upstate New York is covered in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's public NYSBIP project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Addison.
- **Local:** the 2026–27 ballot had no bus proposition. Because Addison buys its own buses, it will finance future electric purchases through bus propositions, NYSBIP vouchers and state transportation aid, not through a contractor.

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Addison already has the indoor space. What it may lack is electrical capacity at the garage. If the FEP shows the service needs an upgrade, AXIVAI can bring battery power to the garage by truck. The first electric buses can then run while the utility work is done. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
