Bemus Point took two votes to buy its first two electric school buses. In September 2024 residents turned down the borrowing, 143 to 132. In March 2025 they approved it, 272 to 227, after the district showed that the bus garage upgrade, worth about $2.5 million, would be almost entirely paid for by others. The upgrade is sized to charge the district's whole fleet, not just the first two buses. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 547 students in 2024–25, down from 554 in 2023–24 and 568 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 4% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 59.15 square miles on the shore of Chautauqua Lake, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region.
- **Schools:** Bemus Point Elementary on Liberty Street, and Maple Grove Jr./Sr. High School with the district office on Dutch Hollow Road.

## Fleet and transportation

Bemus Point owns and runs its own buses. Local coverage of the 2024 vote put the fleet at 20 diesel buses, and the district described the two electric buses as about 10% of it. Diesel replacement continues alongside the electric project: in May 2025 voters approved a bus and passenger vehicle proposition, 243 to 115, with the budget passing 259 to 100.

**The two votes.**

- **2023 award.** The district won a $400,000 EPA Clean School Bus rebate, $200,000 per bus, for two electric buses.
- **5 September 2024.** Voters rejected a $236,000 bond anticipation note for the local share, 143 to 132. The district had planned to repay it over 12 years, at about $20,000 a year, without raising taxes.
- **Extension.** The district asked the EPA for more time. It then learned it could get a 90% rebate on garage upgrades sized for the entire fleet, not just two buses.
- **18 March 2025.** A second vote on the $236,000 note passed, 272 to 227.

In his March 2025 letter the superintendent wrote that the district opposes state mandates but wanted to be "fiscally responsible and plan for the future".

## Depot power and the grid

The bus garage is the core of the project. Before the upgrade it could support only two charging stations. The plan upgrades the electrical supply and the transmission lines to the building so that every future bus can charge there.

- **Cost:** about $2.5 million for the garage and supply upgrade.
- **Utility share:** National Grid, through incentives tied to the EPA program, was to cover about $2.25 million, or 90%.
- **Side benefit:** the district noted that the new lines could also serve the village if it adds public charging later.

Its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan was listed as "Underway" in October 2025. In Western New York, 18 of 80 districts had a completed plan, more than in any other region.

Winter is the operating test. Indoor charging in a heated garage helps, because buses can warm their batteries and cabins from the grid before leaving.

## Funding

- **EPA Clean School Bus rebate:** $400,000 for two buses.
- **NYSBIP:** two vouchers of $159,250 each, $318,500 in all, for 2026 model-year IC Bus CE Series buses, approved 25 July 2024. The project is still open in NYSERDA's data, updated 26 October 2026.
- **Utility make-ready:** about $2.25 million of the $2.5 million garage upgrade.
- **Local:** a $236,000 bond anticipation note.

Before the second vote the district put total eligible funding at more than $3 million. Program rules are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Bemus Point has solved its depot capacity on paper. If the garage upgrade or the new supply lines run late, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage so the two new buses can run while the utility work is finished.
