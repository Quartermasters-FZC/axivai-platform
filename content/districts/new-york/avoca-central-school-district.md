Avoca Central School District made its choice on electric buses in public. Its 2026–27 budget plan said the district would apply for an extension on the state's electric bus purchase rule, because those buses cost "nearly twice" as much as diesel. Voters then approved two more diesel buses, 129 to 27, on 19 May 2026. Ten days later the state moved the purchase deadline from 2027 to July 2032, which gives Avoca one more full replacement cycle to plan its first chargers. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 369 students in 2024–25, against 382 in 2023–24 and 378 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 92.71 square miles, in NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about four students per square mile. Routes are long and buses carry few riders per mile.
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 budget of $15,952,634, 123 to 33. It raises spending 9.54% and the tax levy 2.99%. State and federal aid covers 72.5% of revenue; property taxes cover 16.5%.
- **Cost pressure:** the district cut more than $600,000 in spending to stay inside its tax cap. Debt service rises by $896,556 because of a recent capital project, which also kept the summer program closed in 2025 during construction.

## Fleet and transportation

Avoca owns and runs its own buses out of the district bus garage. The garage is also the district's polling place, where residents vote each May on the budget and on bus purchases.

Avoca replaces buses on a fixed, unusual pattern:

- **Five-year plan.** The district buys buses on a five-year replacement plan. Each purchase comes with a five-year warranty and a commitment to trade the bus in at the end of that term, while resale values are strong and repair costs are low.
- **Paid in cash, not borrowed.** Bus propositions draw on the Transportation Vehicle Replacement Reserve Fund and pay for each vehicle in full at purchase. The reserve held $879,140 on 30 June 2025, earmarked for the buses in the May vote (NYSED property tax report card).
- **2025 vote.** Proposition 2 on 20 May 2025 asked to buy two clean diesel buses and one passenger vehicle for up to $420,000.
- **2026 vote.** Proposition II on 19 May 2026 asked for the same: two school buses and one passenger vehicle, up to $420,000, from the reserve. It passed 129 to 27.

The five-year trade cycle sets Avoca's electric timeline. The diesel buses bought in 2025 and 2026 are due for trade around 2030 and 2031, just before the July 2032 purchase rule. Every bus bought from 2032 on must be zero-emission, so on a five-year cycle the whole fleet turns over to electric by about 2037, three years ahead of the 2040 deadline. The garage needs chargers before that first 2032 purchase.

## Depot power and the grid

Avoca has not started its charging study. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists the district's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) status as **No Contact**. Across the Southern Tier, 29 of 63 districts (46%) were in the same position, and only five had a finished plan.

NYSED says Southern Tier districts buy power mainly from NYSEG, with a few served by small local providers. Avoca's single campus works in its favor: chargers, buses and the garage sit on one site, so one service upgrade can serve the whole fleet.

Three local factors should shape the FEP:

- **Energy prices.** The district's 2026–27 budget presentation reports electric supply costs up 35% and natural gas up 50%. Charging cost per mile will depend on the rate class the garage lands in and on managed overnight charging.
- **Winter range.** On roughly 93 square miles of Steuben County hills, the FEP should test each route against cold-weather battery range, with cabins preheated while the bus is still plugged in.
- **Service size.** A load letter from the utility will show how many chargers the existing garage service can carry before an upgrade.

The grid picture for upstate districts is covered in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Avoca.
- **Local:** Avoca funds buses from its vehicle replacement reserve, approved by voters each year. That reserve, plus NYSBIP vouchers and state transportation aid, will pay for its first electric buses.
- **Tax base:** the levy is about $2.64 million in 2026–27, so every dollar of extra cost per electric bus has to come from vouchers and aid rather than local taxes.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Avoca's first electric buses arrive before its garage service is upgraded, AXIVAI can bring battery power to the garage by truck so those buses run while the utility work is completed. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
