Barker Central School District doesn't run its own buses. Its transportation is provided by a contractor, Ridge Road Express. That puts Barker in the minority in Western New York, where only 27% of districts contract out all their transportation. Even so, Barker has a NYSERDA fleet plan underway. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 617 students in 2024–25, against 620 in 2023–24 and 616 in 2022–23 (NYSED). Enrollment is flat.
- **Area:** 73.5 square miles in Niagara County, in NYSED's Western New York region.
- **Density:** about 8 students per square mile. Riders are spread out, so routes are long for the number of students.
- **Budget:** voters approved $22,916,892 for 2026–27 on 19 May 2026, by 134 to 124. That is a 1.96% rise on 2025–26. The 10-vote margin shows how little room the district has for new costs.

## Fleet and transportation

The district's transportation page states that Ridge Road Express provides Barker's transportation services. The contractor owns the buses, so electric buses for Barker routes will be the contractor's purchase. They will charge at the contractor's yard, unless a future contract puts chargers on district property.

Two things set the pace for a contracted district:

- **The contract.** The new-purchase rule from July 2032 applies to buses bought to serve Barker's routes. The next transportation contract is where the district can set terms for electric buses, charging and who pays.
- **The yard.** The contractor's depot needs electrical service sized for chargers, and that is outside the district's direct control.

NYSED's report shows how the region splits: 44 Western New York districts (56%) own and operate all their buses, 21 (27%) contract out all transportation, and 13 (17%) use a mix.

## Depot power and the grid

Barker's main campus is in the middle of a $25,205,000 capital project, done in two phases. Phase 1 covers most exterior work: rebuilt parking lots, a multipurpose turf field and some window replacement. The new layout separates parent drop-off from the bus loops, so parents no longer wait for buses to clear the lots. Phase 1 bids were opened on 28 April 2026 and included a separate electrical construction contract. The district's reserve filing notes approved use of $2.7 million from a capital reserve for the December 2024 capital improvement project.

None of this is a bus-charging project. But a campus with new bus loops and fresh electrical work is a natural place to plan conduit for future chargers. That matters if a later contract asks for buses to charge on site.

Niagara County is served by National Grid and NYSEG (NYS Department of Public Service). Across Western New York, NYSED reports that 33 districts expect to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet, and 48 had no chargers at all in 2024–25. Barker's FEP is listed as **underway**. In the region, 18 districts (22%) had finished a plan and 13 (16%) had made no contact.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Barker. Contractors can apply for vouchers for buses serving a district's routes.
- **Local:** Barker's spending goes through its contract. Higher costs for electric buses would show up as higher contract rates, not as bus propositions.

See the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program) for program rules.

## Where AXIVAI fits

When electric buses for Barker's routes arrive before the contractor's yard has the power to charge them, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the buses run while the utility work is finished. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
