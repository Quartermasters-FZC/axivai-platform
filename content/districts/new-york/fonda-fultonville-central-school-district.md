Fonda-Fultonville's first electric buses will belong to its contractor. In December 2025 NYSERDA approved vouchers for three electric buses for the district, with Student Transportation of America (STA) named as the operator. The district's own buildings are not where those buses will charge. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,179 students in 2024–25, against 1,201 in 2023–24 and 1,205 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 85.52 square miles in Montgomery County, in NYSED's Mohawk Valley labor market region.
- **Density:** about 14 students per square mile.
- **Campus:** the elementary, middle and high schools share one campus, which the capital project plans treat as a single site.
- **Budget:** voters approved a $37,430,408 budget for 2026–27, 243 to 53, up 3.58% from $36,137,055 in 2025–26.

## Fleet and transportation

The district describes a fleet of about 31 buses that travels about 200,000 miles a year, roughly 6,500 miles per bus. "Our buses are contracted through private contractors": Brown Transportation and STA.

The district also keeps a few vehicles of its own. In 2021 voters approved a five-year lease of a 28-passenger and a 66-passenger bus (not to exceed $157,000), which kept the district at three leased buses. The district said leasing saved money on runs that are not eligible for state aid, such as the late bus. In May 2026 voters approved leasing one bus and buying one with reserves, 250 to 47.

**The vouchered electric buses:**

- three IC Bus CE Series buses, 438.9-inch overall length, model year 2027;
- Class 7 (26,001–33,000 lbs);
- $257,250 each, $771,750 in all;
- approved 9 December 2025, operator STA, dealer Navistar.

NYSERDA flags Fonda-Fultonville as a priority district, and the buses are recorded inside a disadvantaged community census tract.

The same NYSBIP data shows three identical STA buses vouchered for the Johnstown school district, approved on 3 December 2025. Both sets of buses are recorded at the same Fonda location (ZIP 12068), so six vouchered electric buses point to one place in the village.

## Depot power and the grid

Because the buses are contracted, the charging question sits at the contractor's yard, not on the school campus. The district's $51.5 million capital project, going to a vote on 8 December 2026, reflects that. It covers roofs, HVAC, electrical and lighting, security, athletic fields and parking. It includes no bus garage or charging work. State aid would cover 83.3% of the cost and capital reserves the rest.

NYSED reports that Mohawk Valley districts are served mainly by NYSEG and National Grid. Across the region:

- 13 districts expect to need service upgrades for a fully electric fleet;
- 7 districts expect to need a bigger or new bus depot;
- 38 of 52 responding districts (73%) had a utility needs assessment or load letter;
- most districts that commented had finished or were working on fleet assessments with National Grid.

Fonda-Fultonville's own NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan was "Underway" in October 2025. In the region, 4 of 54 districts had a completed plan and 33 were underway.

Mohawk Valley districts rated vehicle range, utility upgrades and lack of external funding as their top challenges. District comments cited doubts about buses in cold weather and on hills. At about 6,500 miles per bus per year, the fleet plan should test the longest winter runs against battery range.

For off-site charging options, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** $771,750 for three buses, approved 9 December 2025 (NYSERDA project data, updated 26 October 2026).
- **Local:** the district pays for transportation through its contracts, plus a small leased and owned fleet approved at each May vote.
- **Regional context:** three Mohawk Valley districts put zero-emission bus propositions to voters, and all three were defeated (NYSED). Contracted service lets Fonda-Fultonville add electric buses without a bus proposition of its own.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If STA's yard needs more capacity than its current service before the electric buses arrive, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so the buses run while the utility upgrade is completed. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
