Franklinville is about to build a new home for its buses. The district's 2024 capital project, valued at $33.8 million, includes construction of a transportation facility, with a listed start date of April 2026. A depot built now will still be in use in 2040, when every bus must be zero-emission. The time to size its electrical service for charging is during design, not after. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 572 students in 2024–25, up from 552 in 2023–24 and 553 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Need:** 53% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 125.7 square miles, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region.
- **Density:** under five students per square mile. Buses cover long rural miles for each rider.
- **Schools:** Franklinville Elementary School and Ten Broeck Academy.
- **Budget:** voters approved the 2026–27 budget of $23,633,440 on 19 May 2026, 110 to 54 (NYSED).

## Fleet and transportation

Franklinville owns its buses. Its 2026–27 budget carries two transportation lines:

- **Pupil transportation:** $1,336,548, down from $1,424,263 in 2025–26.
- **Bus purchases:** $278,420, the same as in 2025–26.

Bus purchases have not always needed a separate vote. Voters approved a school bus proposition in May 2024, 51 to 22. The 2025 and 2026 ballots carried no bus proposition (NYSED budget vote results; district vote notice).

## Depot power and the grid

**The new transportation facility.** In November 2023 the district put a $36.25 million capital project to voters (Olean Times Herald). The district's 2024 Capital Project is listed at $33,763,000. It covers improvements at Ten Broeck Academy and Franklinville Elementary School, and "the construction of a transportation facility" (ConstructConnect). The listing showed it in preconstruction in May 2025 and gives a start date of 6 April 2026.

A new garage gives Franklinville a choice most districts don't have. Conduit, panel space and a service sized for future chargers cost far less during construction than as a retrofit. The project's public descriptions do not mention electric bus charging. Whether the new building includes it is the key question for the district's fleet plan.

**The utility.** The district office on North Main Street is in National Grid's territory (New York State utility territory map). National Grid will set the size and cost of the new building's service.

**The plan.** NYSED's October 2025 report lists Franklinville's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". In Western New York, 43 of 80 districts (54%) were under way, 18 (22%) had completed plans and 13 (16%) had made no contact. The same report says 33 Western New York districts expect to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet.

The nearest electric activity is in Cattaraugus County itself. NYSBIP data lists one vouchered bus for Allegany-Limestone CSD, in a project now closed.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Franklinville.
- **Capital:** the transportation facility is part of the $33.8M capital project. The 2026–27 budget carries debt service for 2025 general obligation bonds ($222,800) and a $100,000 capital outlay project for HVAC controls.
- **Bus purchases:** $278,420 a year in the operating budget.

How NYSBIP vouchers combine with state aid is covered in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the new transportation facility opens before National Grid can supply the full service its chargers need, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the depot so the first electric buses run on schedule. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
