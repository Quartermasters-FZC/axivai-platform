Deposit spent most of 2026 studying a merger with neighbouring Hancock Central School District. The final study, presented on 9 June 2026, included a full inventory of both bus fleets and garages. In August, Hancock's board twice declined to schedule a straw vote, and the process stopped. Deposit will electrify its own fleet, from its own garage. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 462 students in 2024–25, against 451 in 2023–24 and 453 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 124.4 square miles in NYSED's Southern Tier region. The district serves the towns of Deposit, Masonville and Tompkins in Delaware County and Sanford in Broome County (merger study).
- **Density:** under four students per square mile.
- **Budget:** the 2026–27 budget put to voters on 19 May 2026 was $20,197,873, up 3.32% from $19,539,351 (district budget fast facts).

## Fleet and transportation

Deposit owns and runs its buses. The June 2026 feasibility study lists:

- **16 vehicles:** ten large buses (65 or 72 passengers), four 26-passenger buses and two wheelchair-accessible buses;
- **A young fleet:** every vehicle is model year 2019 or newer. Five 72-passenger IC buses date from 2025 and 2026;
- **Six routes**, averaging about 25 miles per run;
- **One garage:** 74 Wheeler Avenue, built in 1963, 11,050 square feet, rated "Satisfactory." It is big enough to store the whole fleet indoors.

Purchases continue on a yearly cycle. The bus proposition on the May 2026 ballot covered one 65-passenger bus, one 28-passenger bus and one passenger van, up to $365,501, and retired one bus of each size plus an SUV. None of the new vehicles is electric, and NYSED's 2025 report does not list Deposit among the four Southern Tier districts running zero-emission buses.

Because the fleet is so new, most of today's buses will still be in service on 1 July 2032. The first zero-emission purchases will replace the 2019–2022 vehicles. That gives Deposit a few years to plan charging before the first electric bus is due.

## Depot power and the grid

The village of Deposit, home to the garage, is in NYSEG's service territory, according to the state's utility territory data.

The 1963 garage was designed for diesel. Deposit's January 2025 capital project ($6.4 million, approved by voters on 7 January 2025) did not change that. The garage's masonry, doors and an extra boiler were listed only as alternates. The core scope was HVAC, windows, classrooms and a playground. The project was funded with $3,796,000 in state aid and $2,604,000 from the capital reserve.

On power:

- **Utility.** The Public Service Commission lists NYSEG as the electric utility for both Broome and Delaware counties. NYSED's October 2025 report names NYSEG as the main utility for Southern Tier districts.
- **Planning.** NYSED lists Deposit's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway." In the Southern Tier, 22 of 63 districts were at that stage, and 29 had made no contact with NYSERDA.
- **Regional need.** Thirteen Southern Tier districts expect to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet. Four expect to need a new depot.

With 25-mile runs, range is not the main issue for a single trip. What decides the cost is whether the 1963 building's service can feed overnight chargers for 16 vehicles. That is the question the FEP should answer, with a load letter from NYSEG. Upstate grid timing is covered in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Deposit. In Delaware County, Sidney (2 buses) and Margaretville (1) hold vouchers.
- **State aid:** Deposit received $529,606 in transportation aid in 2025–26, at a 64.4% transportation aid ratio (merger study).
- **Reserves:** the study showed a $0 bus capital reserve for Deposit, against $1,720,392 at Hancock. Deposit buys buses through annual propositions.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the FEP shows the Wheeler Avenue garage needs a larger NYSEG service, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage so Deposit's first electric buses run while the upgrade is built. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
