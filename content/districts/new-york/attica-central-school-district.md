Attica Central School District handed its regular bus routes to a contractor in 2014, an unusual choice in a region where most districts run their own buses. As long as that model holds, the electric buses that serve Attica will charge at a contractor's yard, not a district garage. That makes the transportation contract, not a bus proposition, the main tool for meeting the state deadlines. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,088 students in 2024–25, against 1,091 in 2023–24 and 1,106 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 148.3 square miles, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Density:** about seven students per square mile. Routes are long and rural.
- **Budget:** the 2026–27 budget is $35,626,084, up 6.27%. It uses $2,033,500 of fund balance and $1,072,682 of reserves, and cuts 18 positions plus some BOCES services. The district names transportation, with fuel, maintenance and driver wages, among the rising costs behind the increase (Attica CSD; NYSED).

## Fleet and transportation

In 2014 Attica and Batavia City School District ran a joint bid. Student Transportation of America (STA) won it for regular school routes, field trips and sports trips, and Attica switched providers for the 2014–15 school year. The local Attica Bus Company kept special-education transportation at that time (The Batavian). STA's own announcement said all three of its new upstate contracts that year, Attica's among them, included customer-paid fuel.

Attica stands out in its region. In NYSED's October 2025 survey, 46 of 68 Finger Lakes districts that answered owned and ran all their buses. Only seven contracted out all transportation.

The district has still kept some bus money of its own. NYSED's 2025–26 reserve schedule lists a district reserve to "Offset Future Bus Purchases", with $560,550 in it.

For a contracted district, the main electrification levers are:

- **Contract terms.** The next bid can ask for zero-emission buses on some routes and set out who pays for charging at the yard.
- **Route length.** In 148 square miles, some runs will need testing against winter range before any contractor commits electric buses to them.

## Depot power and the grid

Attica sits on a utility boundary. NYSERDA's distributed solar dataset shows projects in the 14011 ZIP code on both National Grid circuits (fed from its Attica and Orangeville substations) and NYSEG circuits (fed from Bennington Center). Which utility serves a given yard decides whose make-ready program and timeline apply.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists Attica's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **Underway**. Across the Finger Lakes, 16 of 70 districts (23%) had a completed plan and 42 (60%) had one underway. Twenty-seven districts in the region expect to need an electric service upgrade for a fully electric fleet, and three expect to need a new depot.

The yard question matters most here. Whoever owns the bus yard pays for its service upgrade, and that cost ends up in the contract price. A plan that sizes the charging load by route gives the district a firm number to put in the next bid. For charging options away from a main yard, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Attica or for a contractor serving it. Contractors can apply for vouchers on routes they run for a district.
- **District reserves:** a bus reserve appears in the 2025–26 schedule. The 2026–27 schedule also holds two capital reserves, about $4.65 million in all, for the local share of future capital projects (NYSED).
- **Budget pressure:** the 2026–27 budget relies on $3.1 million of fund balance and reserves, which limits room for new transportation costs.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

When a contractor yard serving Attica needs more power than its current service provides, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so electric buses can run while National Grid or NYSEG completes the upgrade. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
