Electric buses are coming to Albany through its contractors, not through the district. Between October 2025 and March 2026, NYSERDA approved vouchers for 12 battery-electric buses to serve the City School District of Albany. All 12 are owned and run by its yellow-bus companies. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 8,434 students in 2024–25, up from 7,995 in 2023–24 and 7,893 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 6.9% rise in two years.
- **Area:** 21.33 square miles, in NYSED's Capital Region labor market region.
- **Setting:** a compact city with short distances. That matters for both bus eligibility and battery range.

## Fleet and transportation

Albany splits its riders between two systems:

- **Yellow buses** carry eligible elementary students, those living 1.5 miles or more from school. In 2025–26 the district had three yellow-bus partners: First Student, Durham and Rejha.
- **CDTA**, the regional transit authority, carries students in grades 6–12. Eligible students ride regular routes free with an activated student ID, and CDTA runs tripper buses for the three middle schools and Albany High School.

In March 2026 the district said that busing every student who lives under 1.5 miles away would take about 120 more buses. It described that as roughly the size of its current fleet for students living 1.5 miles or more away (WAMC). The yellow-bus fleet is therefore about 120 buses, all contractor-owned.

## The 12 vouchered electric buses

NYSERDA's NYSBIP project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists 12 approved battery-electric buses for Albany. All are flagged as serving a priority district.

Two details matter for charging:

- **The buses are not based in the city.** The First Student buses are registered at ZIP 12077 (Glenmont, Town of Bethlehem), and the Rejha buses at ZIP 12205 (Colonie). The chargers will be at those contractor yards, not on district property.
- **Ten large buses at one yard.** All 10 First Student buses share one ZIP code, so their charging load is likely to fall on a single yard's electrical service.

## Depot power and the grid

National Grid is the electric utility in Albany. Because every yellow bus is contractor-owned, the grid connection is the contractor's application, not the district's. The district still bears the risk: if a yard's upgrade runs late, vouchered buses can arrive before their chargers work.

The district's own planning is at an early stage. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Albany as having made **no contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan. Albany is not alone: 31 of 74 Capital Region districts (42%) were in the same position. For a contracted district, the useful version of that plan covers route energy needs and the contract terms that will carry the 2032 purchase rule, rather than a district garage.

For how utility timelines interact with voucher deadlines, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap). For charging options when a contractor's yard is short of power, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** $2,714,250 in approved vouchers for 12 buses, held by the contractors (table above).
- **District budget:** Albany pays for transportation through its contracts with the bus companies and CDTA, so higher costs for electric buses will reach it through contract rates.

Program amounts and stacking rules are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Albany's first electric buses depend on power at contractor yards in Glenmont and Colonie. If a yard's National Grid upgrade lags behind bus delivery, AXIVAI can supply charging power by truck to battery storage on the yard, so the vouchered buses go into service on schedule. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
