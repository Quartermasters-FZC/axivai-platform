Remsen is one of the smallest districts in New York, but its buses cover a lot of ground. In calendar year 2025 they drove more than 190,000 miles, according to the transportation report to the board. That is roughly 500 miles for every student enrolled. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 381 students in 2024–25, against 379 in 2023–24 and 385 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 68.39 square miles, in NYSED's Mohawk Valley labor market region.
- **Density:** under six students per square mile. Every bus serves a long rural run with few riders.
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 budget of $16,115,375 on 19 May 2026, 147 to 55. The 7.65% spending increase is driven mostly by new debt service from the district's Capital Improvement Project. Debt service rises from $947,132 to $1,669,892.

## Fleet and transportation

Remsen owns its buses and runs them from its own garage, with its own drivers, monitors and mechanic.

- **Fleet size:** the district is selling three older buses and replacing them with two, "bringing us back down to 13 vehicles again" (transportation report, May 2026).
- **Mileage:** more than 190,000 miles in 2025, or about 14,600 miles per vehicle.
- **Recent purchases:** the 2025–26 ballot bought two 66-passenger buses and one 20-passenger bus. New buses 19 and 20 entered service in fall 2025. The district also installed a new lift in the garage.
- **2026 purchase:** voters approved one 66-passenger and one 30-passenger diesel bus, at up to $353,000, by 160 to 42. With transportation aid at 81.1%, the district puts its net cost at $66,717. Delivery was expected in July 2026.
- **Transportation budget:** $771,794 for district transportation in 2026–27, up from $726,826, plus $28,500 for the garage building.

Remsen replaces two or three buses a year. At that pace, the buses bought in 2025 and 2026 will still be in service when the 2032 purchase rule starts. The first electric purchases will come into a fleet that is otherwise diesel.

## Depot power and the grid

Remsen sits in National Grid's upstate territory (NYS electric utility territory map). The bus garage is already a work site: under the Capital Improvement Project, a contractor has been replacing the garage's heating system and boiler controls through the 2025–26 school year (board minutes, October 2025 to January 2026).

That matters for electric buses. A heated garage keeps batteries warm overnight in an Oneida County winter, which protects range on the first morning run. The open question is electrical capacity. The public capital project reports describe heating and controls work, not new electrical service or charger conduit.

Remsen's planning has started. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as **underway**. Across the Mohawk Valley, 33 of 54 districts (61%) were underway and four (7%) had finished. That is one of the highest shares of any region in the report.

For a fleet this size, the FEP should answer two practical questions:

- **Range.** With about 14,600 miles per vehicle a year, Remsen needs to check each run against winter battery range, and plan midday top-ups if needed.
- **Service size.** A 13-vehicle garage may fit its first few chargers on its current service. A utility load letter shows whether it does before the district commits.

## Funding

- **Bus aid:** state transportation aid covers about 81.1% of Remsen's bus purchases (2026 budget hearing).
- **Capital project:** the current Capital Improvement Project is financed by debt with state building aid. Its new debt service enters the budget in 2026–27.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Remsen.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Remsen's fleet plan shows the garage service can't carry chargers for its first electric buses, AXIVAI can bring battery power to the garage by truck so those buses run while National Grid completes the upgrade. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
