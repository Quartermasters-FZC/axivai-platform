Rochester City School District has done the planning step most New York districts have not. NYSED's October 2025 report lists its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **complete**. The buses have not followed. NYSERDA's voucher data shows no electric buses for Rochester through October 2026, and the district rebid its transportation contracts in spring 2026. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 19,768 students in 2024–25, down from 19,927 in 2023–24 and 20,718 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 4.6% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 41.98 square miles, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Riders:** the district transports about 28,000 children a day. That is more than its own enrollment because New York districts also bus eligible students to charter, private and parochial schools.
- **Eligibility:** the Board of Education has set eligibility at more than 1.5 miles from school for all grades.

## Fleet and transportation

Rochester moves its riders through contractors:

- **Yellow-bus contractors:** the district lists First Student, Monroe School Transportation and Ontario Bus Co. as partners.
- **RTS**, the Rochester-Genesee Regional Transportation Authority, has a partnership of more than 30 years with the district. It runs express routes from neighborhood stops to city schools, and fixed-route service used mainly by private, parochial and charter school students.

**The 2026 rebid.** In spring 2026 the district finished a competitive request for proposals. Monroe School Transportation, a contractor for nine years, was not selected. The district said its proposal "was higher than other qualified proposals." Monroe's parent company filed a WARN notice for about 180 layoffs starting 12 July 2026 (WHAM, WHEC). The district had not named the winning provider in that coverage.

Under this model, electric buses for Rochester routes will be bought by the contractors, under contract terms the district writes.

## Depot power and the grid

Rochester is in Rochester Gas and Electric (RG&E) territory. RG&E says it is working with school systems on the switch from diesel. It is looking at each district's timing, the capacity each site needs and the existing infrastructure, and it favors overnight, off-peak charging to ease grid strain (WHAM, September 2025).

Capacity near Rochester is tight in places. Next door, Greece Central School District estimated it would need about 5 MW to charge a planned fleet of 190 electric buses. The substation serving its bus garage on Latta Road had about 1.5 MW to spare (WHEC, January 2025). Rochester's contractor yards will face the same question: how much spare capacity is on the feeder and substation behind each yard?

Rochester's completed Fleet Electrification Plan puts it ahead of most of the state. In the Finger Lakes region, 16 of 70 districts (23%) had a finished plan in NYSERDA's August 2025 data. A completed plan gives the district a basis for contract terms that require bidders to show yard power and a charging schedule. For the regional grid picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Rochester City School District. Suburban Monroe County districts appear in the same data, including Gates-Chili (10 buses) and Penfield (6).
- **Contract costs:** because contractors own the buses, the price of electric buses and chargers will reach the district through its transportation contract rates.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Rochester's plan is done, and its new contracts will decide where the buses charge. If a contractor's yard needs an RG&E upgrade that takes longer than bus delivery, AXIVAI can bring battery power to the yard by truck, so electric buses can run on Rochester routes before the grid work is finished. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
