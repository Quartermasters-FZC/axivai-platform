Berne-Knox-Westerlo has already done the study most districts have not. A NYSERDA-funded electric bus feasibility study for the district concluded that "several of our routes cannot be effectively or reliably completed" with electric buses under current technology and operating limits. In May 2026 voters approved four new gasoline buses instead. The district says it still intends to shift toward electric transportation as infrastructure and funding allow. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 639 students in 2024–25, against 681 in 2023–24 and 655 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 120.2 square miles in western Albany County, in NYSED's Capital Region labor market region. The district's address is 1738 Helderberg Trail.
- **Density:** about five students per square mile.
- **Budget:** voters approved a $24,729,966 budget for 2026–27 on 19 May 2026, 433 to 146. Spending is $7,362 lower than the year before, and the 3.77% levy increase is at the district's tax levy limit.

## Fleet and transportation

BKW owns and runs its own buses. Its replacement plan retires a bus after 7 to 10 years, when repairs to pass state inspection stop being cost-effective. A new transportation supervisor started in December 2025, and the district launched the BusRight app for families in January 2026.

**The 2026 bus vote.** On 19 May 2026 voters approved, 434 to 142:

- three 28-passenger gasoline buses;
- one wheelchair-accessible gasoline bus;
- a maximum cost of $430,783, about 63% of it reimbursed by state aid.

The district explained the choice of gasoline directly. Its feasibility study found that several routes could not be run reliably on electric buses, and the charging stations, electrical capacity and facility changes a full transition needs are a major cost. It says it is pursuing grants to offset that cost.

NYSERDA's NYSBIP data, updated 26 October 2026, list no electric bus vouchers for BKW.

## Depot power and the grid

The study has told BKW where the hard routes are. The next question is power at the bus facility. The district has flagged it already: in its 2025 capital project Q&A, it warned that the state's electric bus and maximum classroom temperature mandates "may require modification of facilities" that could be aided at a lower ratio than ordinary building work.

Two facts shape what BKW can afford:

- **Reserves were empty.** The district's facilities capital reserves were at a zero balance until September 2025.
- **Voters back necessary work.** On 10 November 2025 they approved a $5.142 million project to replace the roofs on both school buildings, 295 to 87, with over 79% state building aid. Work was scheduled to finish in September 2026.

BKW's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan was listed as "Underway" in October 2025. With the 2040 deadline and up to two 24-month extensions, the routes the study flagged have time to wait for longer-range buses, while shorter routes could go electric first. Hilly winter routes are discussed in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** none as of October 2026.
- **Buses:** annual propositions with about 63% state transportation aid.
- **Facilities:** a charging project would most likely go to voters as a capital project, aided at the building aid ratio for that work.

Program rules are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

BKW has named charging stations and electrical capacity as the main cost of going electric. AXIVAI brings battery power by truck into storage on the bus yard, so the routes that do suit electric buses can start running before a permanent service upgrade is funded.
