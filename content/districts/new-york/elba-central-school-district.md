Elba Central School District is one of the smallest districts in the Finger Lakes region: 393 students in 2024–25. It buys its buses from a dedicated capital vehicle fund, which held $472,306 on the state's 2026–27 schedule. Its NYSERDA fleet plan is underway. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 393 students in 2024–25, against 396 in 2023–24 and 375 in 2022–23 (NYSED). The district projects 445 for 2026–27.
- **Area:** 41.09 square miles in Genesee County, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Density:** about ten students per square mile. A small fleet carries few riders over farm roads.
- **Budget:** proposed 2026–27 spending is $14,163,542, up 16.98% from $12,107,723. The tax levy rises 4.56% to $3,387,130 (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

Elba buys its own buses. Voters approved a separate bus purchase proposition alongside the 2021–22 budget, 108 to 24 (The Batavian, May 2021).

Purchases now come from reserves. NYSED's 2026–27 schedule of reserves shows:

| Reserve | Balance | District note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capital vehicle fund | $472,306 | "Expended $87,448 2/2026" |
| New capital outlay project | $300,000 | Held |
| Capital outlay (in use) | $214,525 falling to $114,525 | New capital outlay project |
| New capital project fund | $101,085 | Held |

The vehicle fund matters most for electrification. A district this size replaces a bus or two at a time. Each purchase from the vehicle fund after 1 July 2032 must be zero-emission. Before then, each one is a choice, and a NYSBIP voucher can be paired with the fund for an early electric purchase if the charger and service upgrade are planned too.

The Finger Lakes region is mostly district-operated: NYSED reports that 46 of 68 responding districts own and operate all their buses. Nearby Le Roy CSD, also in Genesee County, ran one zero-emission bus in a fleet of 34, with one more on order.

## Depot power and the grid

The district campus on South Main Street is in National Grid territory, according to the state's electric utility service territory data.

A small fleet changes the grid question:

- **Load.** Two or three Level 2 chargers may fit within a school campus's existing service. A full fleet charging at once may not. The fleet plan should give the number.
- **Order of work.** A load letter from National Grid, obtained before the first bus is ordered, shows whether the first chargers need a service upgrade.
- **Winter.** Rural Genesee County routes need to be checked against cold-weather battery range.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists Elba's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as "Underway". Across the 70 Finger Lakes districts, 16 had completed plans, 42 were underway and 10 had made no contact. Twenty-seven districts in the region expect to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet.

For upstate grid timelines, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap) and [off-grid fleet charging](/off-grid-fleet-charging).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Elba.
- **Local:** the capital vehicle fund ($472,306) and several capital outlay reserves.
- **Fleet plan:** the NYSERDA FEP is free to the district and is underway.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Elba's fleet plan shows the campus service needs a National Grid upgrade, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric bus runs while the upgrade is built. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
