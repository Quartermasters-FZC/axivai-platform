Arkport Central School District is still buying diesel. On 19 May 2026 voters approved two 66-passenger diesel buses and one passenger vehicle, 155 to 59, paid from a bus reserve fund. Under the amended state law that is allowed: new buses only have to be zero-emission from July 2032. The district's NYSERDA fleet plan is underway, so the next few diesel purchases buy time to plan the depot. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 421 students in 2024–25, down from 440 in 2023–24 and 454 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 7% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 65.51 square miles, in NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about six students per square mile.
- **Campus:** a single K–12 campus built around a 1936 school building.
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 budget of $15,390,433 on 19 May 2026, 141 to 74. It is $342,090 smaller than the year before. The tax levy rises 1.89% (WLEA; Hornell Sun; NYSED).

## Fleet and transportation

Arkport owns and runs its buses and replaces them through annual ballot propositions:

- **2025–26:** the ballot included two buses and one school vehicle. NYSED's reserve schedule shows $496,263 set aside for bus purchases that year.
- **2026–27:** two 66-passenger diesel buses and one passenger vehicle, up to $450,000, drawn from the Bus Reserve Fund created on 15 May 2018. The reserve held $837,676 at the spring 2026 filing, with $537,176 expected to remain after the purchase (NYSED).

The district told WLEA in March 2025 that it would buy two diesel buses while it carried out the state-required feasibility study for electric buses.

The bus garage has been part of both recent capital projects. A 2019 project ($10.7 million, approved 122 to 75) renovated it. A second project, approved on 9 December 2025, repaves the bus garage area (WLEA).

## Depot power and the grid

Arkport sits in NYSEG territory. NYSERDA's distributed solar dataset lists the projects in the 14807 ZIP code on NYSEG circuits, mainly from NYSEG's Arkport and Moraine Road substations. NYSEG is the main electric utility across the Southern Tier, according to NYSED.

The fleet plan is the step that sizes chargers and the service upgrade. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Arkport's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **Underway**. Across the Southern Tier, only 5 of 63 districts had a completed plan, and 29 (46%) had made no contact with NYSERDA. Thirteen Southern Tier districts told NYSED they expect to need an electric service upgrade for a fully electric fleet.

Two questions for Arkport's depot:

- **Timing with the capital project.** Construction on the December 2025 project starts in 2027. Charger conduit laid during the garage repaving costs far less than trenching through new pavement later.
- **Winter range.** Steuben County hills and snow cut battery range. The plan should test each run against cold-weather range before the first electric bus is ordered.

Upstate grid timelines are covered in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Arkport.
- **Bus reserve:** voter-approved in 2018 and used for each recent purchase. It can fund the local share of future electric buses too.
- **Capital project:** $11.3 million, approved 63 to 48. It is funded by state building aid, retiring debt and $1,010,000 from the capital reserve, with no added tax. Work runs from 2027 to fall or winter 2028 (WLEA).

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Arkport's fleet plan finds the garage service too small for chargers, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses run while NYSEG completes the upgrade. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
