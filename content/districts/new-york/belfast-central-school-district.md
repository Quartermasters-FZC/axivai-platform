Belfast is one of the smallest districts in Western New York, with 296 students, yet it is ahead of most of the state on electric bus planning. NYSED's October 2025 report lists its NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as complete. Only 18 of the region's 80 districts had reached that point. Its next decision is a $2.3 million building project on 17 November 2026 that includes a new lift for the bus garage. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 296 students in 2024–25, down from 302 in 2023–24 and 307 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 65.61 square miles in Allegany County, in NYSED's Western New York labor market region.
- **Density:** about 4.5 students per square mile. Routes are rural and riders are few.
- **Campus:** one site at 1 King Street, Belfast. The school and the bus garage share the address.
- **Budget:** voters approved a $12,760,000 budget for 2026–27 on 19 May 2026, 89 to 24. The tax levy rises 3.09%, within the cap.

## Fleet and transportation

Belfast owns and runs its own buses and maintains them in its own garage. For 2026–27 it budgeted its bus purchase inside the annual budget, with no separate bus proposition on the ballot. Its 2026–27 budget summary notes that "the diesel school bus cost increased $30,000".

**The bus garage.** The 2024 Building Condition Survey, carried out on 27 December 2024 by the firm CPL, describes the building:

- **Size:** 19,000 square feet, made up of a 2,400 sq ft original building and a 16,600 sq ft addition built in 2008;
- **Electrical distribution:** last rebuilt in 2010, rated satisfactory, with an expected 15 years of remaining life. The survey records that the electrical supply meets current needs;
- **Bus lift:** installed in 2008, with five years of remaining life and a plain recommendation: "Replace bus lift." The district paid for repairs from its repair reserve in summer 2026 as a stopgap.

Those needs are diesel buses and a maintenance shop. Overnight charging for even a few electric buses is a different load. The completed fleet plan should say how much charging the existing service can carry and when an upgrade would be needed.

## Depot power and the grid

Belfast has three advantages many rural districts lack:

- a completed NYSERDA plan, so the charger count and service size are already modelled;
- an indoor garage of 19,000 square feet, where buses can warm their batteries before winter runs;
- an electrical system with years of life left, so any upgrade can be planned rather than forced by failure.

The cost side is the constraint. A district with a $12.8 million budget and a $2.2 million levy has little room to fund a service upgrade locally. State building aid for Belfast is high: the district expects about 95% aid on its 2026–27 capital outlay project.

For the upstate grid picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap) and [school-bus depot power](/research/school-bus-depots).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's data, updated 26 October 2026, list no vouchers for Belfast.
- **Building aid:** about 95% on the 2026–27 capital outlay project.
- **2026 capital project:** $2.3 million, using $250,000 from the capital reserve, presented as tax-neutral. It covers boilers, the bus lift, the auditorium parking lot and classroom air conditioning.

Program rules are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Belfast's fleet plan shows that charging needs exceed the garage's service, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage. The first electric buses could then run while the utility upgrade is designed and built.
