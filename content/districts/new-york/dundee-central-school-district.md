Dundee Central School District keeps its buses on its own campus. The bus garage stands beside the school at 55 Water Street, and the district's $28 million capital project, under construction from 2026 to 2028, gives that garage a generator. Dundee pays for buses from reserves, not new borrowing. Its 2026–27 reserve schedule holds $1.05 million for three buses. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 561 students in 2024–25, down from 574 in 2023–24 and 601 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 7% fall in two years.
- **Area:** 91.84 square miles, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region.
- **Density:** about six students per square mile. Buses cover long rural runs with few riders on each.
- **Budget:** the proposed 2026–27 budget is $21,871,449, 3.8% less than the $22,736,160 of 2025–26. The tax levy rises 3.7%, to $6,697,660 (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

Dundee owns and runs its buses. Its garage shares one campus with the school building. A 2019 construction bid described the work as "a single campus project with both facilities located on the same premises": Dundee High School and the Dundee Bus Garage, both at 55 Water Street (ConstructConnect).

Three local facts shape the fleet's next few years:

- **Buses from reserves.** NYSED's 2026–27 schedule of reserves shows $1,047,411 in a Dundee capital reserve earmarked for "purchase of 3 school buses, pending voter approval".
- **The May 2026 ballot.** On 19 May 2026, voters were asked to approve three transportation vehicles bought from reserve funds with no direct levy impact. A second proposition asked them to restore transportation for students living within 0.6 miles of school buildings. The district put the cost at about $50,000 a year, not eligible for state aid. The same ballot proposed a new 10-year capital reserve of up to $5 million for construction, technology, transportation and infrastructure (FingerLakes1).
- **Transportation costs.** The 2026–27 budget projects transportation spending up nearly 14% while total spending falls (FingerLakes1).

Restoring short-distance riders adds stops and minutes to routes, which matters for later battery sizing. Any bus bought before 1 July 2032 can still be diesel. A bus bought in 2026 should still be running when the full-fleet deadline arrives in 2040.

## Depot power and the grid

The NYS Department of Public Service's electric utility territory map places the Dundee campus in NYSEG territory (data.ny.gov).

The garage is getting a generator. ConstructConnect's listing for the 2025 capital project's electrical contract, estimated at $8.64 million, includes a "bus garage generator" alongside stadium lighting and parking lot replacement. Public project materials do not mention chargers or charger conduit. That makes the generator worth a second look. Its size and transfer switch will decide whether it could also carry chargers in an outage, or only the building.

Planning has started. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Dundee's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". Across the Finger Lakes region, 16 of 70 districts had a completed plan and 42 were underway. In the same report, 27 Finger Lakes districts expected to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet. The region's districts rated utility upgrades among their hardest problems.

For a single-campus district, the bus garage and the school share a service. Adding depot chargers draws on the same supply that feeds the renovated building. The FEP and an early load letter from NYSEG will show how much headroom is left after the capital project's new loads. For the upstate grid picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's public NYSBIP project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Dundee.
- **Capital project:** the $28 million project is funded through state building aid, capital reserves and existing district resources, with no tax increase (FingerLakes1).
- **Reserves:** $1.05 million is earmarked for three buses. A $2.875 million capital reserve carried "no planned use" on the same NYSED schedule.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the FEP shows the campus service can't take depot chargers without a NYSEG upgrade, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage at the garage, so the first electric buses run while the upgrade is built. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
