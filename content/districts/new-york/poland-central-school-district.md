Poland Central School District has about one student per square mile. The district describes itself as 480 square miles, the third largest district in New York by area, behind Saranac Lake and Town of Webb. Its buses travel more than 300,000 miles a year. Range, not route count, is the core question for its electric future. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 467 students in 2024–25 and 2023–24, and 475 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** about 480 square miles by the district's count. NYSED's October 2025 report lists 458.8 square miles. Either way, only Town of Webb (511.2 square miles) is larger in NYSED's Mohawk Valley table.
- **Geography:** the district sits in the foothills of the Adirondacks. Its students live in eight towns in Herkimer County.
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 budget of $18,549,639 on 19 May 2026, 161 to 48. Spending rises 1.40% and the tax levy 2.00%.

## Fleet and transportation

Poland owns and runs its buses.

- **Fleet:** the district lists 22 buses and one station wagon, covering 17 bus routes. The fleet mixes 66-passenger full-size buses and 25-passenger small buses.
- **Mileage:** more than 300,000 miles a year across home-to-school routes, out-of-district special program routes, sports travel and field trips. That is about 13,600 miles per bus.
- **Road limits:** buses can't travel on private or seasonal roads, which shapes where stops can go.
- **History:** Poland once ran the longest bus run in the state, 70 miles a day, picking up pupils from the Piseco Lake area. In 1936 the district had four buses.
- **2026 purchase:** the 2026–27 budget includes one large and two small school buses to replace aging vehicles.

The mix matters. Small electric buses carry smaller batteries, and long rural runs are where those batteries are tested hardest in winter.

## Depot power and the grid

Poland sits in National Grid's upstate territory (NYS electric utility territory map). Its buses are based at the district's own garage.

**The bus garage is part of an $8.6 million capital project** approved by voters in May 2025. The project covers both the main school and the bus garage, with roof replacements, heating and ventilation upgrades, a school-wide lockdown system, intruder locks, sidewalks and a digital sign. The district sent its plans to NYSED in December 2025, in three parts, and expected construction to start in June or July 2026.

The project's public description does not include charger conduit or a larger electrical service at the garage. While the building is open for renovation, that is the cheapest time to add either.

Planning is at an early stage. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Poland as having made **initial contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP). Across the Mohawk Valley, four districts (7%) were at that stage, 33 (61%) had plans underway and four (7%) had finished.

In a district this size, the FEP should test:

- **Winter range on the longest runs.** Some runs may need a midday charge at the garage.
- **Out-of-district trips.** Special program routes and sports travel add long one-way legs with no charger at the far end.
- **Garage service.** A utility load letter shows how many chargers the existing service can carry.

The wider grid picture in upstate New York is covered in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Capital project:** $8.6 million, approved May 2025, covering the school and the bus garage.
- **Capital outlay:** a separate $100,000 project in 2025–26 replaced exterior doors and heating parts. The 2026–27 budget carries another $100,000 for priorities in the building condition survey.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Poland.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the garage service can't carry the chargers that Poland's long routes will need, AXIVAI can bring battery power to the garage by truck so the first electric buses run while National Grid completes the upgrade. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
