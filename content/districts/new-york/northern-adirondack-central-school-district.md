Northern Adirondack Central School District covers 378.6 square miles of Clinton County with 780 students. That is about two students per square mile, the sparsest ratio of the North Country districts we cover. Every student comes to one campus in Ellenburg Depot, so every bus route ends there. In December 2025 voters approved a $38 million capital project that includes campus-wide electrical panel replacements, with construction due to start in spring 2027. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 780 students in 2024–25, against 798 in 2023–24 and 782 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Need:** 45% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 378.6 square miles, in NYSED's North Country labor market region.
- **Density:** about two students per square mile. By comparison, Moriah CSD has about eight and Lyme CSD about five.
- **Schools:** Northern Adirondack Elementary School and Northern Adirondack Middle/High School stand side by side on Route 11 in Ellenburg Depot (NCES).

## Fleet and transportation

With one campus and a district this large, Northern Adirondack's routes are long by necessity. Buses run from the edges of a 378-square-mile district to Ellenburg Depot and back, twice a day, through North Country winters.

That geography is the core electrification question here. A battery bus loses range in deep cold, when it must also heat the cabin. The longest runs may need either a larger battery, a midday top-up at the campus, or a diesel bus kept for those routes under an extension.

The district did not return NYSED's 2025 zero-emission busing survey, so its fleet count and charging plans are not in the state report.

## Depot power and the grid

The Route 11 campus lies in NYSEG's service territory (Department of Public Service territory map). Any charging will draw from a NYSEG service at or near the campus.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists Northern Adirondack's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) as "Underway". Across the North Country's 65 districts, 4 (6%) had a completed plan and 23 (36%) had made no contact. The FEP should answer two local questions:

- **Range against route length.** Which runs fit a winter charge, and which do not.
- **Campus service capacity.** Whether the campus service can carry chargers on top of school load, and what NYSEG would need to upgrade.

The timing of the capital project matters. Proposition 1 includes campus-wide utility upgrades, including new well and water services and **electrical panel replacements**, plus a standby generator at the middle/high school. The project is in design from January to August 2026, then NYSED review, with construction from spring 2027 to fall 2028. Sizing new panels and the generator with future chargers in mind costs far less during design than after.

The wider grid picture upstate is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Capital projects:** on 16 December 2025 voters approved both propositions:
  - **Proposition 1, $38 million:** elementary school roof, bathrooms, building management and fire alarm upgrades; middle/high school interior upgrades, flooring and a standby generator; campus-wide well, water and electrical panel work.
  - **Proposition 2, $10,990,000:** an artificial-turf athletic complex with a 500-seat grandstand, and a new agricultural program building.
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for the district. See our [NYSBIP review](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Northern Adirondack's buses all return to one campus on a NYSEG service. If chargers there need more capacity than the campus can give before NYSEG upgrades it, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses run while the upgrade is completed.
