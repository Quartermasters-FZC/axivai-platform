Bradford Central School District is one of the smallest districts in New York: one K-12 school, 233 students and a fleet of about ten vehicles. That small scale works both ways for electric buses. Every bus parks at the same school, but each purchase is a large share of the budget. Bradford has a NYSERDA fleet plan underway and a capital reserve it has been using to buy buses. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 233 students in 2024–25, against 234 in 2023–24 and 245 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Need:** 52% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Schools:** one, Bradford Central School, on State Route 226 in the town of Bradford.
- **Area:** 46.41 square miles in Steuben County, in NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region. That is about five students per square mile.
- **Budget:** the district's 2026–27 state filing shows spending of $11,816,989, up 14.88% from $10,286,439, with a tax levy of $3,006,643 (up 2.41%).

## Fleet and transportation

Bradford owns and runs its own buses. NYSDOT recorded 21 safety inspections of its vehicles, with **none failed**, in the state fiscal year ending 31 March 2026. Buses are inspected twice a year, which points to a fleet of about ten buses and other vehicles.

A fleet that small changes over one bus at a time. Bradford saves ahead for buses. NYSED's 2026–27 schedule of reserves lists a capital reserve of about $1.45 million, with the stated purpose "purchase of school bus to be approved in May 2026 budget vote". The projected balance falls by $200,000.

Under the amended state law, a diesel bus bought in 2026 can stay in service until the 2040 full-fleet deadline. From July 2032, every new purchase must be zero-emission. For a ten-vehicle fleet on a regular replacement cycle, that puts the first electric bus in the early 2030s.

## Depot power and the grid

The state's utility territory map places the school on Route 226 in **NYSEG** territory.

Because all the buses return to one school, Bradford needs only one charging site. The questions are its size and its timing:

- **Service size.** A rural school building is often fed by a service sized for lights, heating and kitchen loads. The first one or two chargers may fit within it. Charging the whole fleet probably will not.
- **Winter.** Cold Southern Tier mornings cut battery range on hilly rural routes, so the fleet plan should test each route against winter range and allow for preheating while the bus is still plugged in.

The planning has started. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists Bradford's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan as **underway**, although the district did not return NYSED's zero-emission survey. That puts Bradford ahead of much of its region: of 63 Southern Tier districts, only 5 had a finished plan and 29 (46%) had made no contact with NYSERDA.

For the grid picture across upstate New York, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **Reserves:** about $1.45 million in a capital reserve earmarked for bus buying, plus a smaller $617,000 capital reserve with no planned use (NYSED schedule of reserves, 2026–27).
- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Bradford. Across the state, 461 buses have been vouchered across 79 recipients.
- **State aid:** as a district-operated fleet, Bradford's bus purchases draw state transportation aid on top of local funds.

Amounts and eligibility for the state programs are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If the school's NYSEG service cannot take more than a charger or two, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on site so Bradford's first electric buses run while the service upgrade is completed. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
