East Rochester Union Free School District is one of the most compact districts in upstate New York. It covers 2.97 square miles, about 300 students per square mile, inside a village laid out around a railroad. Its routes are short. The electrification question here is less about range than about where the buses that serve it will charge. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 889 students in 2024–25, down from 926 in 2023–24 and 927 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 4% fall in one year.
- **Area:** 2.97 square miles, in NYSED's Finger Lakes labor market region. It is the smallest of the 70 Finger Lakes districts by area; only Brighton (3.07), Gananda (6) and West Irondequoit (6.91) are under 10 square miles.
- **Community:** the Village of East Rochester, incorporated in 1897 as Despatch, became a coterminous town and village in 1981. The village had 6,334 residents in 2020. CSX's double-track Rochester Subdivision runs east to west through it with no grade crossings (Wikipedia).
- **Budget:** proposed 2026–27 spending is $33,913,788, up 4.23% from $32,537,765. The tax levy rises 2.11%, to $16,325,038 (NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

East Rochester's public budget filings show no bus-buying program. None of the reserves on NYSED's 2026–27 schedule of reserves is earmarked for vehicles. The planned uses listed are Chromebooks, wireless upgrades, building and athletic equipment, retirement costs and insurance. The 2026–27 levy also carries no separate proposition spending, the line where a bus purchase vote would appear.

That sets East Rochester apart from most of its region. NYSED reports that 46 of 68 responding Finger Lakes districts own and operate all their buses, seven contract out all transport, and 15 use a mix.

Electric buses already run close by. In the same NYSED report, neighboring Penfield CSD operated two zero-emission buses in a fleet of 80, with six more on order. Six Finger Lakes districts had zero-emission buses in service, and the region had 12 on order.

## Depot power and the grid

The NYS Department of Public Service's electric utility territory map places East Rochester in Rochester Gas and Electric (RG&E) territory.

In a district this small, the site question comes first. A compact village has little spare land for a bus yard with chargers. Wherever East Rochester's buses park overnight, that yard's service will need to grow as the fleet turns electric. If the buses park outside the district, the work happens on someone else's property and on the RG&E feeder that serves it.

Planning is at an early stage. NYSED's October 2025 progress report lists East Rochester's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) status as "Initial Contact Made". Only two of the 70 Finger Lakes districts were at that stage; 16 had completed plans and 42 were underway. Districts in the region rated utility upgrades, lack of outside funding and lack of voter support as their hardest problems. For charging away from a main yard, see [park-out charging](/research/park-out-charging). For the upstate grid picture, see [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's public NYSBIP project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for East Rochester.
- **Local:** no vehicle reserve and no bus proposition appear in the 2026–27 property tax report card. A future electric purchase would need a new reserve or a voter proposition.

Program amounts and eligibility are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Where the yard serving East Rochester's buses lacks the electrical capacity for chargers, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on site, so electric buses can run while RG&E completes the upgrade. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
