Dryden Central School District keeps buying buses the traditional way. On 19 May 2026 its voters approved a school bus bond of up to $400,000, 369 to 145. Yet NYSED's October 2025 report shows the district had made no contact with NYSERDA about a fleet electrification plan. It was also one of nine Southern Tier districts that did not answer the state's zero-emission busing survey. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,261 students in 2024–25, up from 1,210 in 2023–24 and 1,236 in 2022–23 (NYSED). That is a 4% rise in one year, unusual for a rural upstate district.
- **Area:** 93.47 square miles in Tompkins County, east of Ithaca, in NYSED's Southern Tier labor market region.
- **Density:** about 13 students per square mile.
- **Schools:** three elementary schools (Cassavant, Dryden and Freeville) and a middle/high school on the main campus at 118 Freeville Road.
- **Budget:** voters approved a 2026–27 spending plan of $50,346,154, 376 to 140. That is $1.74 million (3.59%) more than the $48,602,723 adopted for 2025–26.

## Fleet and transportation

Dryden runs its own Transportation Department. Its buses serve three elementary buildings in different parts of the district as well as the main campus, so a single route plan has to reach the village of Freeville and the outlying hamlets.

The district replaces vehicles through regular bus bond propositions. The 2026 proposition authorised "the purchase of school buses and other transportation vehicles at a maximum cost of $400,000", repaid by a tax collected in annual installments. The proposition did not specify electric buses, and the district's budget materials do not mention zero-emission vehicles.

The same ballot carried a $100,000 capital project for doors and pool plumbing at the Middle/High School (approved 425 to 90). The larger 2022 capital project, worth $12 million and approved on 5 April 2022, covered boilers, windows, roofs, parking lots and a renovation of Dryden Elementary. The district's description of it does not include the bus garage or electrical work for charging.

## Depot power and the grid

NYSED reports that districts in the Southern Tier region get their electricity mainly from one utility, NYSEG, with a few small providers. Across the region:

- 13 districts expect to need electric service upgrades for a fully electric fleet;
- 50 of 54 responding districts had no charging stations in 2025–26;
- 38 of 54 (70%) had completed a utility needs assessment or held a load letter.

Dryden is not in those counts, because it did not submit the survey. Its fleet plan status is "No Contact", the same as 29 of the region's 63 districts (46%). The district has no public figure yet for how many chargers its garage could support, or what a service upgrade would cost.

Southern Tier districts rated vehicle range, voter support and utility upgrades as their hardest problems. All three apply in Dryden: its routes are rural, its winters are cold, and every bus purchase goes to a public vote. The NYSERDA fleet plan is the free study that answers these questions, run by run.

The statewide grid picture is in [New York's electric school-bus gap](/research/ny-implementation-gap).

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Dryden.
- **Local:** the May 2026 bus bond ($400,000) is the district's current vehicle funding. Future electric purchases would come through the same propositions, combined with NYSBIP vouchers and state transportation aid.
- **Capital:** the 2022 project ($12 million) used state building aid and capital reserves with "no additional tax impact". A charging project would need its own capital authorisation.

Program amounts are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

If Dryden's garage needs a bigger electrical service before its first chargers, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so electric buses can run while NYSEG completes the upgrade. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
