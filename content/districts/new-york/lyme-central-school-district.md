Lyme Central School District has numbers most small districts lack. In January 2026 its treasurer presented a fleet analysis and infrastructure survey from National Grid and Polara Energy. It found that about half of Lyme's bus routes could be electrified, that the bus garage would need a new transformer, and that electricity would cost about $20,000 a month more than the district now spends on diesel and gasoline. Lyme kept buying gasoline buses. On 15 December 2026 its voters decide an $8.5 million capital project that includes work at the bus garage. For the statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 318 students in 2024–25, against 329 in 2023–24 and 319 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Need:** 51% of students are economically disadvantaged (NYSED, 2024–25).
- **Area:** 65.72 square miles around Chaumont on Lake Ontario, in NYSED's North Country labor market region.
- **Density:** about five students per square mile.
- **Budget:** $10,086,999 for 2026–27, with a 3.90% levy increase, approved 184 to 45 on 19 May 2026. Transportation accounts for $539,851 of it.

## Fleet and transportation

Lyme owns and runs a small fleet with its own drivers. Recent bus votes have all been for gasoline buses:

| Vote | Proposition | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20 May 2025 | One 65-passenger school bus | Approved |
| 19 May 2026 | One 65-passenger and one 35-passenger gasoline bus with wheelchair access, $305,000 | Passed 193–36 |

The board has tracked the mandate closely. A bus dealer briefed it on electric bus types, charging stations and grants in December 2024. "Zero Emission Bussing (ZEB) Updates" became a standing agenda item in 2025–26.

Routes are long for a district this size. In March 2026 the board heard that a summer bridge closure would add about 15 minutes to routes, with the first child on some buses riding for up to about an hour.

## Depot power and the grid

Lyme's campus on Academy Street in Chaumont lies in National Grid's service territory (Department of Public Service territory map). The January 2026 survey set out what electrification would mean at the garage:

- **Routes:** about 50% of the district's routes could be run by electric buses. The rest would need diesel or gasoline, a waiver, or a different route plan. The superintendent told the board a waiver can be applied for.
- **Transformer:** a new transformer would be required at the bus garage. Large power transformers take 2–4 years to deliver (NIAC, June 2024). Smaller distribution units are faster, but still a lead-time item; see [grid connection wait times](/research/ev-charging-grid-connection-wait-times).
- **Running cost:** about $20,000 a month more than current fuel spending. That estimate assumed electricity at $0.10 per kWh. The board heard the rate had since risen to about $0.17 per kWh.

Energy prices are now a regional issue. In March 2026 the board heard that North Country superintendents had adopted a joint resolution on 30–40% increases in energy costs. State budget proposals reviewed at the same meeting included electric school bus infrastructure aid.

NYSED's October 2025 report lists Lyme's NYSERDA Fleet Electrification Plan (FEP) status as "Initial Contact Made". Across the North Country's 65 districts, 4 (6%) had a completed plan and 23 (36%) had made no contact.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Lyme (see our [NYSBIP review](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program)). Neighbouring Jefferson County districts have used the program: Alexandria CSD holds four battery-electric bus vouchers.
- **2026 capital project:** on 8 October 2026 the board approved an $8.5 million proposition for a special vote on 15 December 2026. Its scope covers the main building and the bus garage, including gas tank replacements, septic replacement and "utility, mechanical, plumbing and electrical improvements". About 92% of eligible costs are expected to qualify for state aid, with the first tax impact in 2028–29. It would be funded by $669,000 and $546,000 from two capital reserves, $535,000 of district funds, and up to $6.75 million in bonds.
- **Buses:** gasoline buses are financed through bus propositions and state transportation aid.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Lyme's own survey says the garage needs a new transformer before it can charge a fleet. While that transformer and service upgrade are on order, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses can run on the routes the survey found suitable.
