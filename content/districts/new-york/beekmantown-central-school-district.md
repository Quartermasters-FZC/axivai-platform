Beekmantown Central School District has said plainly what its bus garage needs. In January 2025 the district's superintendent told CNHI News that the current transportation facility "will need significant upgrades" for electric buses. The list included high-capacity power supply, more space for chargers and a changed garage layout. The district's 38 buses make 92 trips on a school day, before athletics. For statewide rules and funding, see [New York electric school buses](/electric-school-buses/new-york).

## District profile

- **Enrollment:** 1,905 students in 2024–25, down from 1,942 in 2023–24 and 1,917 in 2022–23 (NYSED).
- **Area:** 92.64 square miles in Clinton County, in NYSED's North Country labor market region. The district includes Cumberland Head Elementary as well as the main campus.
- **Density:** about 21 students per square mile, against a North Country average of under 5 (NYSED).
- **Budget:** $58,937,231 proposed for 2026–27, up 3.08% from $57,176,023 (district budget page; NYSED property tax report card).

## Fleet and transportation

Beekmantown owns and runs its fleet. Figures the district gave in January 2025:

| Item | Figure |
| --- | --- |
| Buses | 38 |
| Routes | 23 |
| Schedule | Dual-run: elementary and secondary runs separately |
| Daily trips | 92, not counting athletics and other trips |

Late buses run Monday to Thursday at 5:00 pm and Friday at 4:15 pm. The district says a late-bus ride takes one to one and a half hours, depending on ridership. Ride times like that are what makes range the first question on rural routes.

**Buying vehicles.** Beekmantown replaces vehicles through annual propositions, financed by tax:

| Ballot | Vehicles | Maximum cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2025–26 | Four student transport vehicles | $663,375 |
| 2026–27 | Three student transport vehicles | $536,978 |

The 2026–27 transportation budget is $2,048,712. That includes $250,189 for diesel, gasoline and oil, down $25,000 from the year before, and $161,759 for parts and tires. The bus garage building has its own $41,600 line.

## Depot power and the grid

The superintendent named three garage upgrades the district expects to need:

- high-capacity power supply installations;
- more space for charging stations;
- changes to the garage layout.

The superintendent also said the district will need to work with utility providers to make sure the school facility has enough electrical capacity. The district planned a transportation feasibility study as its first step. NYSED's October 2025 report lists Beekmantown as having made **initial contact** with NYSERDA about a Fleet Electrification Plan, but not yet begun one.

Clinton County's investor-owned electric utilities are NYSEG and National Grid. Plattsburgh and Rouses Point run municipal systems (NYS Department of Public Service). In the North Country, 13 districts expect to need service upgrades for a fully electric fleet, and 6 expect to need a new bus depot. The region ranked vehicle range, facility construction and utility upgrades as its hardest problems.

The main campus already has construction under way. Voters approved a capital project on 16 May 2023, which includes a classroom addition. The 2026–27 ballot asked to raise its cost cap by $2,000,000, paid from available district funds, to cover higher building costs. The district's construction updates cover the classroom addition, a playground and kitchen work at Cumberland Head Elementary. None mention the bus garage.

## Funding

- **NYSBIP:** NYSERDA's project data, updated 26 October 2026, lists no vouchers for Beekmantown. In Clinton County, Plattsburgh City holds one voucher.
- **Local:** vehicles are bought through yearly propositions of roughly $500,000–$660,000. Electric buses would need those figures to grow, or vouchers to close the gap.

Program details are on the [New York page](/electric-school-buses/new-york) and in the [NYSBIP report](/research/new-york-school-bus-incentive-program).

## Where AXIVAI fits

Beekmantown has already said its garage needs high-capacity power. While that upgrade is designed and built, AXIVAI can bring battery power by truck into storage on the yard so the first electric buses run. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites.
