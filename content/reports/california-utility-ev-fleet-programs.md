If you are electrifying a bus depot, truck yard or transit garage in California in late 2026, the funding picture has changed. The two largest utility make-ready programs, PG&E's EV Fleet and SCE's Charge Ready Transport, no longer take applications. Both must sign their last contracts by 31 December 2026. What remains is Electric Rule 29 (Rule 45 at SDG&E), which pays for the utility side of the meter. Alongside it sit SDG&E's Power Your Drive for Fleets, PG&E's Flex Connect pilot and the municipal rebate programs at SMUD and LADWP.

This guide sets out what each one pays for, who qualifies, where it stands in November 2026, and how long the grid connection really takes. Every figure is from a utility or CPUC source, listed at the end.

## PG&E: EV Fleet closes, EV Power Ready and Flex Connect remain

**EV Fleet.** The CPUC approved EV Fleet in May 2018 (D.18-05-040) with a $236 million budget for medium- and heavy-duty make-ready. On 30 June 2026, PG&E reported 861 applications and 444 viable contracts committing 8,002 vehicles. Construction was complete at 205 sites, and 189 sites with 4,228 vehicles were running. Of the signed contracts, 204 (46%) are in disadvantaged communities. PG&E had spent $100.8 million and had $135.5 million left. It expects to use the full infrastructure budget, with a waitlist of about 5–10 projects.

PG&E closed the application window on 30 June 2026 and is signing its last contracts by 31 December 2026. In 2024 the CPUC lowered the program's site floor from 700 to 375 because sites cost more than expected (Resolution E-5347). PG&E now reports that it has passed both the 375-site and 6,500-vehicle goals.

The cost data in that resolution are useful for budgeting. PG&E had fully invoiced 26 school bus sites with 270 buses by 30 September 2023. On average, each site cost PG&E $186,045 for make-ready up to the meter, and PG&E paid a further $39,861 rebate for customer-owned work behind the meter. PG&E's FAQ puts the EV Fleet process at about 9 to 13 months from design to completion. Customers must also run the chargers for 10 years and share charger data.

**EV Power Ready (Rule 29).** This is PG&E's name for its Rule 29 service. PG&E pays for and builds the service extension from its distribution line to the meter: transformer, conductors, trenching, conduit, riser materials and utility permits. The customer pays for everything behind the meter, including the chargers. If a distribution line extension is needed, the customer pays for that under Rule 15. Chargers must be installed within 30 days of energization, or on an agreed timeline of no more than 180 business days. They must stay in service for five years. New sites default to PG&E's Business EV rate, which has no demand charges and uses a monthly subscription instead.

**Flex Connect.** This pilot is for sites where PG&E says capacity upgrades will take longer than 12 months. PG&E sends a day-ahead hourly power limit to the site's energy management system, and the chargers adjust to it. In PG&E's example, a customer asking for 2 MW would otherwise be held to 0.5 MW all year until upgrades finished. Under Flex Connect, it gets the full 2 MW most of the time.

By September 2026 Flex Connect had five customers and about 85 prospective ones. Participants got the capacity they needed in 90% of hours, and PG&E says the process takes four to eight months. Flex Connect is still a pilot. PG&E is working to formalize it under CPUC oversight.

## SCE: Charge Ready Transport closes, Rule 29 remains

**Charge Ready Transport (CRT).** CRT is a seven-year program that launched in 2019. It has a $342 million budget and a goal of at least 500 sites and 8,490 medium- and heavy-duty EVs. At least 15% of the infrastructure budget goes to transit agencies and at least 40% to disadvantaged communities. SCE builds low- to no-cost infrastructure on both sides of the meter. The customer buys the chargers from SCE's approved product list.

Three groups get a charger rebate: transit agencies, school districts, and sites in disadvantaged communities (except Fortune 1000 companies). The rebate covers 50% of equipment cost, capped by power band:

| Charger power | Rebate cap per charger |
|---|---|
| 0–19.2 kW | $1,800 |
| 19.3–49.9 kW | $7,800 |
| 50–149.9 kW | $23,300 |
| 150 kW and above | $39,200 |

SCE no longer accepts CRT applications. Both parties must sign Program Participation Agreements by 11:59 pm on 31 December 2026.

**Rule 29.** SCE's Rule 29 covers utility-side planning, design and engineering, plus the transformer, conduit and substructures, trenching, metering, utility permits and easements. A site that uses a Charge Ready program can't also use Rule 29. SCE's own figures show what this is worth. Across 19 financially completed Rule 29 projects, utility-side cost averaged $300,203 per project. Ratepayers carry that cost, not the fleet.

## SDG&E: the fleet program is active, Rule 45 is closed

**Power Your Drive for Fleets.** The CPUC authorized about $107 million for this program in 2019. As of April 2026 SDG&E still presented it as available to Class 2–8 on- and off-road vehicles, with two options:

1. SDG&E pays for, builds, owns and maintains all infrastructure up to the charger. The fleet buys the chargers.
2. SDG&E builds to the meter. The fleet builds behind the meter and receives a rebate of up to 80% of that cost.

SDG&E's own case study shows how long a large fleet project takes. San Diego MTS's IRIS Rapid Transit project took about 55 months from interest form to completion in 2025. That included 20 months from the start of construction to meter set, and SDG&E called it "on-time".

**Rule 45.** SDG&E's version of Rule 29 closed to new applications on 20 August 2025. SDG&E's 2024 rate case decision (D.24-12-074) had capped spending at $7.58 million. SDG&E says more than 100 Rule 45 projects were already pending when the cap was set. In November 2025 a group of EV charging companies and NRDC petitioned to lift the cap. A proposed decision denying the petition was on the CPUC's 29 October 2026 voting agenda. Until something changes, SDG&E fleets outside Power Your Drive for Fleets fall back on standard Rules 15 and 16. Under those rules, the customer pays a larger share of the line extension.

## SMUD and LADWP: municipal rebates

The CPUC does not regulate SMUD or LADWP, so Rule 29 does not apply in their territories. Both offer per-charger rebates instead.

**SMUD** lists these incentives per handle, with standard and equity-site amounts:

| SMUD incentive | Standard | Equity site |
|---|---|---|
| High-power Level 2 (6.6 kW or more) | $3,500 | $7,000 |
| Non-public DC fast, under 50 kW | $7,500 | $15,000 |
| Non-public DC fast, 50–149.9 kW | $15,000 | $30,000 |
| Non-public DC fast, 150 kW or more | $30,000 | $60,000 |
| Panel upgrade | $1,000 | $2,000 |
| Transformer upgrade | $5,000 | $10,000 |
| DC fast with onboard storage | $100 per kWh | $100 per kWh |

Equity sites score at the 75th percentile or higher on CalEnviroScreen 4.0. Applications go through SMUD's PowerClerk portal.

**LADWP** pays medium- and heavy-duty rebates of $8,000 per Level 2 charger. DC fast chargers get $25,000 (50–149 kW), $68,750 (150–274 kW) or $125,000 (275 kW and above). Rebates are capped at $2 million per premises for Class 3–8 vehicles. Vehicles must be on-road Class 2b–8 EVs that qualify for a California incentive, and chargers must stay in service for five years. LADWP's program page said in October 2026 that applications were closed. It planned to reopen enrollment in November 2026 with revised terms, funding amounts and documents. Its dashboard lists a 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027 funding period for medium- and heavy-duty chargers.

## How long energization really takes

AB 841 (2020) created the utility-side EV rules. The CPUC approved them in Resolution E-5167 on 7 October 2021: Rule 29 at PG&E and SCE, and Rule 45 at SDG&E. Decision 24-09-020 (12 September 2024) then set targets for the steps each utility controls. For Rule 29/45, the targets are an average of 182 calendar days and a maximum of 335. For capacity work, the maximums are 684 days for a new or upgraded circuit and 1,021 days for a substation upgrade.

The utilities' 1 April 2026 filings show the gap between those targets and what a fleet experiences:

| Measure | CPUC target | PG&E (1 Apr 2026 filing) | SCE (1 Apr 2026 filing) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rule 29 utility-controlled time, average | 182 calendar days | 111 calendar days | 150 business days (2 projects completed in 2025, against a 125-business-day target) |
| Rule 29 application to energization, average | No target | **560 calendar days** (161 projects, applications Jan 2023–Dec 2025) | **571 business days** (median 518) |
| New or upgraded circuit | 684 days max | 950 days | Not reported |
| Substation upgrade | 1,021 days max | 1,285 days | Not reported |

PG&E's averages count only the 48% of applications that had been energized, so the true wait is probably longer. PG&E's own steps take about 111 days. The rest of the 560 days goes to customer design, permits, construction and other steps the targets don't cover. PG&E's Rule 29 end-to-end average rose from 508 days in its September 2025 filing to 560 days in April 2026. SCE's guidance to fleets is blunter: 18 months or more for simple circuit upgrades, three years or more for new circuits, and six years or more for new substations.

## What a California fleet should do now

- **Apply for Rule 29 early.** In PG&E and SCE territory, it is now the main way to get the utility side paid for. The clock that matters is roughly 18 months end to end, not 182 days. File as soon as the site and load are known, and line up design and permits in parallel.
- **Check the capacity answer first.** If the utility's load study shows a circuit or substation upgrade, plan for two and a half to three and a half years on PG&E's record. At PG&E, ask whether the site fits Flex Connect.
- **Fund chargers separately.** Rule 29 stops at the meter and pays nothing toward chargers or customer-side wiring. The CPUC's next ratepayer round (Funding Cycle 1, 2025–2029, up to $1 billion across the six IOUs) is aimed at behind-the-meter make-ready and charger rebates. The new California Clean Fuel Reward takes $7,500–$120,000 off commercial EVs, but it excludes buses.
- **Get on the municipal lists.** SMUD requires approval through its portal before you build. LADWP funds by fiscal year and says it may prioritize reservations by project readiness.

School districts should read this alongside the [California electric school bus page](/electric-school-buses/california), which covers state bus funding and depot readiness.

## Where AXIVAI fits

The utility programs pay for the grid connection. They don't shorten the 18 months to three years a fleet may wait for it. AXIVAI brings battery power by truck into storage on the yard, so buses and trucks can charge before the grid connection arrives. When the utility energizes the site, the yard moves to grid power. AXIVAI is preparing its first pilot sites. See [off-grid fleet charging](/off-grid-fleet-charging).
